# v0.70.0 — what the packer owed, and whether anybody was ever paid

Twelve tools, closing Sprint 5 of the Gap Closure Plan: the money end of the
grower-packer pipeline.

---

## The gap, restated

v0.67.0 built the two registers a grower keeps about somebody else's numbers:

| The paper | The register |
|---|---|
| a scale ticket | Scale Ticket — what the grower says arrived |
| a packout settlement | Settlement Statement — what the packer says they owe |

Neither of them touched the ledger, and the module docstring said so by name:
*"the tool that books settlement proceeds is a later sprint."* This is that
sprint. The chain it completes is four documents long:

```
Scale Ticket(s) → Settlement Statement → Sales Invoice → Payment Entry
```

Everything new lives in `erpnext_mcp/tools/sales.py`.

---

## There is no Delivery Note, and that is the design

ERPNext has a Delivery Note. It is the wrong shape here, for the same reason a
Scale Ticket is not one — v0.67.0 argued the first half of this and this
release is the second.

A Delivery Note is the **seller's** record of goods leaving on the seller's
terms: priced, against a Sales Order, in Items and UOMs the seller controls. In
grower-packer the seller controls none of that. The packer owns the scale,
prints the ticket, decides the variety and the grade, and states the price
months later on a settlement.

So the Scale Ticket **is** the delivery evidence, and nothing in this release
writes a second record of one delivery. `get_season_summary` traces deliveries
through tickets rather than through a stock ledger, and there is no
`create_delivery_note` in the catalogue. Adding one would produce a document
that disagrees with the ticket with nothing to say which is right.

## Two paths out of a settlement, and only ever one of them

| | `create_sales_invoice_from_settlement` | `post_settlement_to_gl` |
|---|---|---|
| produces | a draft **Sales Invoice** | a draft **Journal Entry** |
| receivable is | a document | a party balance |
| AR ageing | per invoice, aged, allocatable | drift against the ledger |
| payment | `receive_payment` allocates to it | reconcile by hand |
| **default** | **yes** | for operations that prefer it |

The invoice is the default because it is what gives AR ageing, payment
allocation and every standard receivables report something to work with. The
journal entry exists because some operations reconcile settlements against a
bank deposit rather than against an invoice, and forcing them through a
document they will never look at is worse than giving them the three GL
movements directly.

**A settlement that has been through one is refused by the other, by name.**
The forward link (`Settlement Statement.sales_invoice`) and
`posted_journal_entry` are both checked by both tools, in both directions. Two
revenue postings for one statement is a double count nobody finds until the
year end, and it is the single failure this pair of tools is shaped to prevent.

Both are draft-only. `submit_sales_invoice` and `submit_journal_entry` are the
separately-switched tools that post, which is the split every create/submit
pair in this app has had since v0.1.0.

## The invoice totals to the settlement, not to a recomputation

This is the subtlest thing in the release and the one most worth reading.

A settlement line carries a packed weight, a price and a gross amount, and
v0.67.0 deliberately **keeps a stated gross amount** rather than deriving it —
a packer who applied a pool adjustment to one line is telling the truth and the
multiplication is not.

ERPNext's Sales Invoice Item has no such tolerance. It computes
`amount = qty × rate` on every validate. So a line that disagrees with its own
arithmetic cannot keep both numbers, and the choice of which to move is the
whole design:

```
    packed 11,200 lb   price 0.40/lb   stated gross 4,200.00
    11,200 × 0.40 = 4,480.00 ≠ 4,200.00

    →  qty  11,200      rate 0.375      amount 4,200.00
       stated_price_per_unit: 0.40
       rate_differs_from_statement: true
```

The **rate** moves. The amount is what the packer said they owed, and it
survives to the invoice total. The stated price is reported beside the rate that
was used, per line, so the adjustment is visible rather than absorbed — and
`total_check` names the variance against the settlement either way, so a
settlement that could not be reproduced exactly says so before anybody submits
it.

## Deductions are negative charge rows

Each deduction becomes an `Actual` Sales Taxes and Charges row with a negative
amount against `deduction_account`:

```
    revenue recognised GROSS       16,600.00   (income)
    packing and cold storage       -7,360.00   (expense)
    receivable                      9,240.00   (net)
```

Netting them into the revenue line would produce the same receivable and delete
the number a grower most wants a year later — *what did storage cost me*.

**The deduction account is refused rather than guessed.** ERPNext has no company
default for it, and picking whichever leaf Expense account sorts first would put
a season of packing charges somewhere nobody chose. `include_deductions: false`
is the escape hatch: it invoices the gross and says, in a `warning`, that the
receivable will not clear until the deductions are booked separately.

## The reads

- **`get_settlement_shrink`** splits shrink into what the packer called cull and
  what nobody accounted for. Shrink is delivered minus packed; cull is the part
  of it the packer reported; the rest — juice, storage loss, fruit not yet run —
  is `unexplained_weight`. **A cull percentage is what a grower renegotiates a
  contract over; an unexplained percentage is what a grower asks a question
  about.** They are different findings and they are reported separately. Per
  variety and grade it puts the priced lines beside the matched tickets, and a
  variety on one side and not the other is reported as such rather than dropped
  — a packer regrading a load produces exactly that.

- **`get_packout_summary`** aggregates by variety, grade, customer, field or
  month, and **returns null rather than allocating** what cannot be attributed:

  | grouped by | delivered | packed | culled |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | customer, month | headers | headers | headers |
  | variety, grade | matched tickets | priced lines | **null** |
  | field | matched tickets | only where one field owns the settlement | same |

  A packer states one cull weight per statement, so no part of it belongs to a
  single variety. A settlement pooling two fields cannot be split back into
  them. Whatever is left over comes back under `unattributed`. **A pro-rata
  packout by field is a made-up number that looks exactly like a measured one**,
  and it would feed a per-acre KPI.

- **`get_ar_aging`** is the receivables mirror of `get_ap_aging`, grouped by
  customer and cross-checked against GL Entry, with per-customer `drift`. It
  complements `get_outstanding_invoices` rather than replacing it: that one
  lists invoices, this one lists customers, and a collections call and a
  dashboard want different shapes.

- **`get_season_summary`** traces the whole pipeline and names the three gaps —
  fruit delivered that no settlement claimed, settlements nobody invoiced or
  posted, invoices nobody collected. `pipeline_health` reads `complete` only
  when all three are empty. Every stage is filtered by **its own** date, so a
  November delivery settled in January is in the window and its settlement is
  not, which is why unmatched tickets near the end of a season is normal rather
  than alarming.

## The rest

- **`receive_payment`** records money in as a draft Payment Entry,
  Receive/Customer only — so a tool that can collect money cannot be talked into
  spending it. Named invoices, or **oldest first** across everything outstanding
  when a cheque arrives with no advice. A payment larger than everything
  outstanding is not refused and is not spread onto invoices that do not exist:
  the remainder comes back as `unallocated_amount`, a real on-account balance.

- **`reconcile_settlement_to_tickets`** attaches a stub that turned up after the
  settlement was filed. It runs the same four checks
  `create_settlement_statement` runs — draft ticket, already-matched ticket,
  wrong company, wrong packer — and all of them before anything is written, so a
  settlement is never left with half its tickets claimed. It changes no weight,
  price or total on the settlement. What it reports is `variance_change`: how
  far the disagreement with the packer moved, and in which direction.

- **Two link fields, set together.** `Settlement Statement.sales_invoice` ships
  in this app's own DocType JSON (`bench migrate`); `Sales
  Invoice.settlement_statement` is a **Custom Field**, installed by
  `after_migrate` and again lazily on first use. This is the second place this
  app extends a doctype it does not own, after `compliance_fields.py`, and the
  argument is narrower than that one's: the link has to be readable from both
  ends or neither end can be trusted. Either being absent is **reported** as an
  unset link, never silently skipped.

- **One shared non-stock Item per variety and grade** (`FRUIT-HONEYCRISP-XF`),
  created once and reused — never one Item per statement. Same reasoning, and
  the same shape, as the per-category expense items v0.68.0 introduced.

- Six mutating tools default **off**; six read tools default **on**, in a new
  "Sales, Settlements & AR" settings section.

## Upgrading

`bench migrate`. It adds `sales_invoice` to Settlement Statement and installs
the `settlement_statement` Custom Field on Sales Invoice. Until it runs,
`get_season_summary` reports every settlement as uninvoiced and says so in its
own `note` — read that before treating the number as a finding.
