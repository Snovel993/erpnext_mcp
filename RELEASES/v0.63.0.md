# v0.63.0 — the page they were shown, and the page nobody can quietly change

Three pieces of work. Two of them are the ends of the signing flow that had no
artefact behind them; the third is a wage floor an operator can set without a
release.

---

## 1. Step 1 — the signer saw the form

`API_CONTRACT.md` §17.5 does not describe a missing feature. It describes a
bind, and then names the fix:

> It does not render the unsigned page, and that is a server-side gap rather
> than a choice. `generate_i9_pdf` and `generate_w4_pdf` answer with a private
> `file_url`, and this app authenticates to the sidecar with `X-FarmOps-Token`
> rather than to Frappe — so that URL is a login page here. **The fix is one
> route** — an unsigned-page read that hands bytes down the way
> `submit_form_signature` hands the signed ones back.

`get_document_preview` is that route.

The evidence chain has five steps and the first of them is that the signer was
**shown** the document. `FormSignatureViewModel.advance()` already enforces the
order — there is no transition that reaches the pad without a presentation step
having happened — and until this release the presentation step could show the
form's name, its section, its box and the verbatim attestation, and could not
show the page any of it was about. A rendered preview would have been an empty
viewer.

### The bytes travel, because the URL cannot

Same answer `submit_form_signature` gives on the way out, and same answer
`get_employee_badge_pass` gives for a `.pkpass`. Under three keys:

```json
{
  "content":        "JVBERi0xLjcK…",
  "content_base64": "JVBERi0xLjcK…",
  "base64":         "JVBERi0xLjcK…"
}
```

Not a rename in any direction. `content` is what §15.1's decoder reads,
`content_base64` is what this app's file tools answer with, and `base64` is what
the signature answer puts the signed page under. A preview that spelled it a
fourth way would be the one call in the flow a client has to special-case.

### It draws once, and it does not overwrite

`signatures._redraw` argues at length that drawing a federal form nobody asked
for is this app deciding something that is not its to decide. The same argument
gives the answer here rather than contradicting it: a caller asking for a
preview **by name, in that call, to put the page in front of the person about to
sign it** has asked. So:

- a form with no page gets one drawn — which on a fresh I-9 is every time, and
  without it this route would answer "no page" on the exact case the pad opens
  for;
- a form that has one is handed back as it stands. `render_i9_pdf` refuses a
  second render without `overwrite` because the likeliest thing in that field is
  the copy somebody printed and had signed, and a preview that re-rendered on
  every screen open would repoint it a dozen times a hire day.

### `stale` is the key the presentation step branches on

True means the record has changed since the page was drawn. That matters more
here than it looks: the fingerprint taken at signing covers **the record**, not
the page, so showing a signer a stale page means hashing something other than
what they read. `refresh=true` redraws for a caller who needs certainty.

### `signature_boxes` comes back with it

What can be signed, which boxes already carry a signature, and the **verbatim**
attestation for each. §17.5 is explicit that the attestation shown at the pad is
the sentence being sworn to rather than a summary of it, and a pad that
discovers Section 1 is taken by submitting a signature to it discovers it with
the worker standing there.

## 2. Step 5 — the artefact is tamper-evident

`tools/signing_evidence.py` opened in v0.60.0 with five steps and one of them
deferred:

> This module is where 1, 2 and 4 are written down and where 5's hash is taken;
> step 3 stays where it was. **The rendered page that composites the whole
> packet is a later release**, and it will be drawn FROM these rows rather than
> from a second copy of the same facts.

This is that release, and the page is drawn from those rows.

### Resistant is not evident

`pdf_signing.py` has stamped captures into the page **content** and flattened
the AcroForm since v0.51.0, so a signed I-9 has no annotation to delete and no
field to clear. That is tamper-*resistant*: altering it takes a real PDF editor
rather than a click in Preview.

It is not tamper-*evident*, because resistance says nothing about **detection**.
A page that has been edited with a real editor looks exactly like one that has
not, and the person holding it has no way to tell. Two things change that:

**A verification page**, appended, naming for every signature on the form: the
signer, the badge scanned, how identity was established, the capacity, the
moment, the device, the address, the coordinates, the evidence row and the
fingerprint of the record as it was presented. A page that has been altered
contradicts its own appendix.

**A SHA-256 of the finished file**, on the evidence rows. This is the check that
catches an edit to *any* page including the verification page itself.

### The file hash cannot be printed on the file

It is taken over the finished bytes, and printing it would change them. So the
page carries the **document** fingerprint — the hash of the record's content as
the signer was shown it, taken before the ink landed — and the register carries
the **file** hash. Two hashes answering two questions:

| Question | Answered by | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Is this the form they saw? | `document_hash` | the row, and printed on the page |
| Is this the file we produced? | `sealed_pdf_hash` | the row only |

### This module places no signatures

`render_i9_pdf` and `render_w4_pdf` already stamp every capture at the form's
own named widget rectangles — the I-9's `Signature of Employee` and `Signature
of Employer or AR`, the W-4's measured Step 5 band — and flatten the form
afterwards. `seal_signed_document` redraws through those and appends. A second
implementation of that geometry here would put the same signature in a slightly
different place, on the one document where that is not a cosmetic difference.

### An unsigned form is refused

A verification page on a form nobody signed is an official-looking appendix that
vouches for nothing, and somebody would file it. That refusal is the point
rather than caution.

A signed form with **no evidence row** is sealed anyway, with the page saying so
in as many words. Every signature collected before v0.60.0 is in that state and
cannot grow a row retrospectively — the badge scan, the device and the
coordinates were never captured, and inventing them is the one thing an evidence
register must never do.

### Three columns that move on an append-only row

`Signing Evidence` grows `sealed_pdf`, `sealed_pdf_hash` and `sealed_at`, and
they are the only thing on that doctype that is not fixed at the moment of
signing. Every other column says something about the signature — who made it,
how they were identified, when, where, what the document said. These three name
an artefact produced **afterwards**, and it legitimately changes: a Form I-9
signed in Section 1 in July is sealed once, and when the employer signs Section
2 in August the form is sealed again and the July attestation now appears in a
two-block document. Freezing the July row at the one-block file would point an
auditor at a copy missing half the signatures on the form it describes.

They are written with `frappe.db.set_value(..., update_modified=False)` — the
same door `render_i9_pdf` uses for `generated_pdf_on` — so
`SigningEvidence.before_save` stays an absolute refusal for every path a person
or a tool could take to revise what the row says. **Nothing is deleted**: each
seal is a new File on the document and the previous one stays attached, so the
chain of sealed copies is itself a record.

### It runs after the signature and cannot take it down

`submit_form_signature` seals what it just signed, reports it under `seal`, and
follows `include_pdf` — a caller that turned the page off wanted only the write,
and sealing produces a page.

The ordering rule is the one `tools/signatures.py` opens with. Store the image,
write it onto the form, record the evidence, close the task, redraw the PDF,
seal it; each step may fail without undoing the one before it, because **the
signature is the irreplaceable artefact and the person who drew it has gone back
to work**. A bench without reportlab gets `seal.sealed: false` with the reason
and a federal record that is complete without it.

The idempotent retry path seals nothing, for the same reason it writes no
evidence row: a marginal link would otherwise fill somebody's personnel record
with a near-identical PDF per attempt.

## 3. The wage floor, as a row rather than a constant

`payroll_calc.calculate_full_payroll` has **paid** the floor since v0.49.0 —
gross is the greater of what the work earned and what the hours were owed, per
state, with the overtime premium carried. Two of its own arguments had never
been supplied by anything:

| Argument | Declared since | Supplied by | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `min_wage_rates` | v0.49.0 | nothing | Every run used the table compiled into the module. An Oregon rate change was a release. |
| `min_wage_regions` | v0.49.0 | nothing | Oregon's Portland metro rate — the highest of the three — was unreachable from any tool in this app. |

### Where the rates live now

`State Tax Configuration` grows `minimum_wage`, `minimum_wage_non_urban` and
`minimum_wage_portland_metro`. It is the right home because a minimum wage is a
fact about a state in a year, which is exactly what that record already is: one
row per company, state and tax year. No new doctype, which is the design
philosophy's own instruction about table sprawl.

**A zero means "not set here", never "the floor is zero".** Currency fields
default to 0 on every row of every doctype, so treating one as an override would
let a site that filled in nothing but its SUTA rate silently drop the floor to
nothing for its whole payroll — and the makeup that catches an underpaid piece
rate would compute against it and find nothing wrong. A rate has to be positive
to win, which is also what makes this release change nothing on a site that
configures nothing.

### Where the region lives

`Farm Salary Structure` grows `min_wage_region`: Standard, Non-Urban or Portland
Metro. **On the structure rather than on the shift**, because it is a fact about
where somebody *works* rather than about one day of it, and a crew that moves
between blocks inside one region must not have its floor move with it.

A region a state does not define falls back to that state's standard rate. So a
Portland-metro worker who spent a week over the river is owed Washington's
$16.66 — which is what naming the region against both states produces, with no
per-state table on the structure and no branch that would have to know which
states have regions.

### The cross-check reads the same table

`payroll_integration` runs an independent per-state check on the wages as paid,
and it now takes the configured rates. Without that a farm which raised its own
floor would see every topped-up slip flagged as below the minimum, and a farm
whose configured rate was lower would see nothing flagged that should be. A
cross-check reading a different table from the thing it checks is not one.

## 4. The floor, visible before anybody posts

The figures were all being computed. What was missing was a place to read them
on the calls a person actually makes.

### `preview_payroll`

A `minimum_wage` block: the region, the rate table it used, which states took
their floor from their own configuration rather than the shipped default, the
makeup, the floor, what the work earned, and a sentence. And the makeup is now
on the **summary line**, because the whole reason a preview exists is to be read
before anybody posts, and the figure has been in a nested key nobody opens since
v0.49.0.

**`compliant` and `makeup` are deliberately two different facts.** The floor is
paid, so a slip that needed makeup was paid lawfully — it is compliant *because*
the makeup is on it. The makeup is the number that says the **rate** is set below
what the hours are worth, on the employer's own cost, recurring every period
until somebody changes it. A reader asking "is this lawful" reads the first; a
reader asking "is our piece rate right" reads the second. Conflating them would
either report every underpriced bucket as a violation or hide it entirely.

A `Salary` structure is **not** topped up and says so. Whether a salaried
employee is exempt from the minimum wage — executive, administrative,
professional, or one of the agricultural exemptions — is a fact about their job
that this app does not hold, and raising an exempt supervisor's pay because a
sixty-hour harvest week divided their salary below the floor would be inventing
an obligation.

### `calculate_payroll`

Two columns the Farm Payroll Slip doctype has carried since v0.49.0 and this
path never wrote: `earned_gross` and `minimum_wage_makeup`. The period path
(`_slip_row`) has filled both from the start; this one stored the topped-up
gross and nothing saying it had been topped up, so a stored row could not answer
"how much of this was makeup" and the audit trail for a below-floor piece rate
stopped at the preview.

And the entry is a **draft**, so the result now carries what somebody reads
before submitting it: `topped_up` (paid lawfully, rate too low), `below_floor`
(should be empty; a row there on a piece-rate or hourly slip usually means a
shift carries no `work_state`, so no legislature and no floor applies), and
`salaried_shortfall` named separately rather than folded into either.

## 5. Permissions

`W-4 Form` is granted read and write to **Farm Manager**. This is the v0.59.3
finding restated for the other federal form: `signatures._require_write` gates
every signature on Frappe's own `has_permission(..., "write", doc=...)` rather
than on a role list of this app's, so the W-4's Step 5 signature box,
`seal_signed_document` and `get_document_preview` were all closed to the role
that runs onboarding.

**It is not a widening**, which is the part worth checking. `w4.submit_w4`
inserts with `flags.ignore_permissions = True` behind
`employee.require_hr_role()`, and `HR_ROLES` names Farm Manager — so that role
has been creating and editing W-4 records through this app since the wizard
shipped, on every site, with no DocPerm saying so. What the table said and what
the role did were already out of step.

Read and write, not `create` and not `delete`, for the reasons `HIRING_FORMS`
gives. **The signer is still the worker**: the W-4's box carries no `form_type`
so the authorized-signer roster does not gate it, and `signatures._identity`
refuses a badge resolving to anybody other than the form's own employee on
exactly that box. Write permission decides whose account may hold the pad; the
identity check decides whose hand may sign.

`Tax Form` is deliberately **not** granted. A 941 or an OR-WR is signed by an
officer of the employer under penalties of perjury, this app keeps no register
of officers, and a Farm Manager is not one by virtue of running the orchard.

## 6. What is not in this release

**The iOS side.** `MobileAPI.swift` does not name `get_document_preview` yet —
the handset half is a viewer on the `.reviewing` step, which is separate work.
Both new methods are listed in `PENDING_IOS_INTEGRATION` rather than in
`MOBILE`, so the test file keeps claiming only what the Swift actually calls.
`seal_signed_document` may never need naming at all: the ordinary flow gets its
seal from `submit_form_signature`.

**A migration for existing signed forms.** Nothing back-fills a sealed copy for
a form signed before this release. `seal_signed_document` produces one on
demand, and a sweep that sealed every historical form would attach a file to
every personnel record on the site as a side effect of a `bench migrate`.

**A `Tax Form` preview for a Farm Manager.** The route handles the doctype and
the permission gate will refuse the role, which is correct — see §5.
