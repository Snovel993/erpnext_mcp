# v0.82.0 — what a bin weighs, and who decided

Agricultural master data. Five doctypes, three child tables, ten tools. The
three registers everything else in this app had been taking on trust from
whoever happened to be calling it.

---

## The one idea

Three questions this app has been answering for a year without ever being able
to say where the answer came from:

- **What is a crop's pre-harvest interval?** A spray check needs it. Spray on
  Tuesday with a seven-day PHI and pick on Sunday, and the fruit cannot lawfully
  be sold.
- **What does a market pay for a grade?** A breakeven is only as good as the
  split it assumes between what packs out fresh and what falls to processing.
- **What does a bin weigh?** A settlement multiplies by it.

Until now each of those came from the caller. Which means the site could not
tell a considered figure from a plausible one, and neither could anybody reading
the record a year later.

Now each is a row. Somebody can read it, correct it, and be held to it.

---

## None of the three belongs to a company

This is the design decision worth arguing, because every other register in this
app is entity-scoped and these three are deliberately not.

| Register | Why |
| --- | --- |
| `Crop` | A **species**. A sweet cherry is a sweet cherry on both sides of a corporate boundary, and the days between bloom and harvest do not consult the deed |
| `Market` | A **place in the world**. Two growers shipping into the Pacific Northwest fresh cherry market are shipping into *one* market |
| `Agricultural UOM Context` / `Conversion` | A bin holds what it holds regardless of whose name is on it |

Scoping `Market` would be the worst of the three: it would give one site two
answers to what a No. 1 is, which is precisely the failure the doctype exists to
prevent — reproduced one level up. Per-company narrowing already exists where it
belongs. A **settlement** names both the market and the company.

`test_permissions` asserts this. The four appear on the list of doctypes
deliberately left unscoped, with the argument written beside them, so a later
release that adds a company column has to delete a paragraph to do it.

---

## `Field.crop` stays free text, and that is not an oversight

The obvious move on shipping a `Crop` master is to turn `Field.crop` into a Link
to it. This release does not, for two reasons, and the second is the one that
decided it.

**A Link beside the old Data column gives the site two answers.** `Field.crop`
has been free text since it shipped, on the stated grounds that "a block of
table grapes is not a schema change". Adding a Link does not replace that
column; it sits next to it, and nothing says which wins.

**A Link makes migrate ORDER load-bearing.** Three other features were landing
into this same tree the same day, each recording a crop as a string. If one of
them ships a Link to `Crop` and lands before this release, `bench migrate` dies
on `LinkValidationError` before it reaches their doctypes — the exact failure
`harness.LinkValidationError` was written to model after v0.12.0 hit it live.
The peer session building the spray and IPM pipeline agreed to keep `crop` as
Data on all three of its doctypes for this reason.

The upgrade, when somebody wants it, is a patch that seeds `Crop` rows from the
distinct `Field.crop` strings already on the site and only then changes the
column. It should **fold case when it dedupes** — a site with "Gala" and "gala"
would otherwise get two Crop rows, and one of them would stop matching anything
keyed on the string. That is a release of its own, not a field option.

## Blank is not zero, and the PHI is where it matters

A crop with `default_phi_days` of `0` has genuinely no pre-harvest interval. A
crop with none recorded is one nobody has checked. These are different facts and
the column is nullable so it can tell them apart.

`list_crops` reports the second as a gap rather than folding it into the first.
It is the same judgement `list_irrigation_zones` makes about water tests, and it
is here for a sharper reason: a gate that reads a missing PHI as zero clears
fruit that a label would hold.

Every tool that reports a PHI also reports **what it is not**:

> `default_phi_days` is the crop's own floor, used when nothing more specific is
> known. The BINDING pre-harvest interval is the one printed on the label of the
> material actually applied, and on a single crop it ranges from zero days to
> thirty.

That sentence is a module constant, written once, because four copies is how one
of them comes to say something slightly weaker than the others.

## Two window rules that look alike and are opposites

**Half a harvest window is refused.** A start with no end is a season nothing
closes, and every reader has to guess the other month.

**A window that wraps the year is accepted.** November to February is a real
harvest. The obvious `start <= end` check would refuse the southern hemisphere
and the greenhouse both — a rule about integers wearing the costume of a rule
about farming.

`harvest_months` is computed and wraps correctly, so callers do not do that
subtraction themselves and get it wrong about half the time. The two tests sit
adjacent in `test_agronomy.py`, so removing one to "fix" the other is visibly
wrong.

---

## A contradiction is refused; a judgement is reported

Getting this boundary wrong in either direction is how a register becomes either
unusable or useless, so it is drawn explicitly:

**Refused** — `maturity_years` on a variety of an *Annual* crop. An annual
finishes inside the season it is planted for; there are no non-bearing years to
capitalise. Both facts cannot be true.

**Reported** — every recorded variety sitting in one pollination incompatibility
group. They will not set fruit for each other and the block finds out four years
later, which is exactly the kind of thing a register should say out loud. But
the pollinizer may be in a neighbouring block, or planted and never recorded
here, so refusing it would refuse a real orchard.

## Why ERPNext's own conversion table could not hold this

ERPNext ships `UOM Conversion Factor`: one global factor per unit pair.

A bin of cherries is about 800 lb. A bin of apples is about 900 — the same stack
of boxes, a hundred pounds apart. Enter both into a crop-blind table and the
second overwrites the first; enter either and the site is quietly wrong about
the other crop, in a direction nobody notices until a settlement is disputed.

So `Agricultural UOM Conversion` makes **the crop part of the key**, with a
generic row as the fallback rather than the only option.

```
                    resolution order
crop-specific row  ─┐
generic row        ─┼─►  the factor, plus WHICH of these produced it
inverted row       ─┤
one-hop chain      ─┘
```

`get_uom_conversions` **resolves rather than looks up**:

- **It inverts.** "One bin is 800 lb" and "one lb is a 800th of a bin" are the
  same fact. Requiring both to be entered would require an operator to keep two
  rows in step by hand.
- **It chains one hop.** Bins → pounds → tons. One hop and no further: past
  that it is multiplying three nominal figures together, and the compounding
  error exceeds the answer's worth.
- **A chain reports the weaker of its two bases.** An exact hop composed with a
  nominal one is nominal. Reporting the chain as Exact because half of it was
  would be the most misleading thing this module could say about a number.
- **It always says which it did.** A factor handed back without its provenance
  is a number the caller cannot check, and these are the numbers that multiply
  into settlements.

### `basis` is not bookkeeping

| Basis | What it claims | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Exact` | A definition — 128 fl oz to a gallon, 43,560 sq ft to an acre | **Cannot carry a crop.** A quantity that varies by fruit is not a definition |
| `Nominal` | The trade's rule of thumb — "a lug is eighteen pounds" | Right enough to plan with, not right enough to settle a dispute with |
| `Operation Average` | This farm's own weighed figure | **Must cite a source.** It wins every lookup, so the bar for asserting one is higher |

A shrink dispute turns on whether the weight was defined, assumed, or weighed. A
settlement that cannot say which is a settlement with no argument behind it.

### Three refusals, because they need three different actions

- **No row at all** — record one, or check the unit names.
- **No *active* row** — a superseded factor is kept switched off so last
  season's settlements stay explicable, and is not consulted.
- **Rows for other crops and none for this one** — which is *correct* rather
  than missing. The refusal names the crops that do have one. Answering with
  somebody else's fruit is how a settlement goes wrong by a factor nobody
  traces.

## Harvest and Scale Ticket are two contexts, not one list

A bin is a **container**. A pound is a **weight**.

A field crew hands in bins and the shed reports pounds. Those are two
measurements of one delivery, and a single list accepting either is a list that
lets them be summed. So `Agricultural UOM Context` carries `applies_to`, and a
context whose units span two dimensions is refused — where this app knows the
dimension. A unit it does not know is not checked, because an operator adding
their own should not have to teach the module about it first.

An **empty** unit list is also refused. An allow-list with no rows either forbids
everything or permits everything depending on who reads it, and both readings
have been shipped by somebody.

---

## Sizes are millimetres, not row sizes

Cherries trade by row — 9-row, 10-row, 11-row. Apples trade by count per box.
Both are **inverse** scales: the bigger fruit carries the smaller number. A
column holding those would sort backwards in every report and add nothing a
millimetre does not say.

Convert once, at the grade standard. The sales desk keeps speaking rows.

Beside it, two percentage columns bounded in opposite directions and for
opposite reasons. `max_defect_pct` is bounded 0–100, because a tolerance outside
that is a transcription of something else and would make every comparison pass.
`premium_pct` is bounded only *below*, at -100: a grade worth less than the base
is the normal case — juice against fresh, orchard run against fancy — and a
column refusing negatives would make every operation invent a base grade nothing
falls under.

## What is seeded

Install and every migrate lay down a starting book, checked by docname, creating
only what is not there:

- **Sweet Cherry** (Bing, Rainier, Sweetheart, Skeena, Lapin), **Apple** (Fuji,
  Gala, Honeycrisp), **Pear** (Bartlett, Anjou) — with rootstocks, pollination
  groups, expected yields, years to maturity, and water demand at each of seven
  growth stages.
- **Pacific Northwest Fresh Cherry**, **Washington Processing**, **Export –
  Asia/Pacific** — with grade ladders and USDA shipping points.
- **Harvest**, **Spray**, **Field Area**, **Scale Ticket** unit contexts, and the
  conversions between their units.

**The numbers are a starting book, not your farm's.** Every conversion but the
three definitions is `Nominal`, the yields are expectations, and the grade
premiums are illustrative *shapes* — that fresh pays a multiple of processing,
and that the spread within fresh is the largest single assumption in a breakeven.
An operation that leaves them untouched and quotes them at a lender has misused
them. `list_markets` reports which markets still carry no grades precisely so the
ones nobody reviewed stay visible.

Nothing here can fail an install. Every record is attempted alone and a failure
is printed and stepped over. On a Frappe bench with no ERPNext there is no `UOM`
master, so the units and the conversions that link to them are skipped **by
name** — while the crops and markets, which link to neither, are seeded anyway.

The unit rows go into ERPNext's own `UOM` register. That is seeding **rows** into
a foreign master, which this app already does in `company.ensure_party_types`, as
distinct from adding **columns** to one, which `hooks.py` promises it never does.

## Where this plugs in

Nothing in this release rewires an existing feature. It puts the answers
somewhere they can be read, and names the four places that should now read them:

| Feature | What it should read |
| --- | --- |
| Spray | `Crop.default_phi_days` as the floor for a pre-harvest interval check — with the label's own interval preferred wherever it is known |
| Breakeven | `Market.grade_standards` — `premium_pct` turns a projected size distribution into a projected price, and `premium_spread_pct` says how much the projection is worth getting right |
| Irrigation | `Crop.water_requirements` — keyed on growth **stage** rather than month, because bloom moves a fortnight between a warm spring and a cold one |
| Scale tickets | `get_uom_conversions` for bin-to-pound, with the crop passed |

Wiring each of those is a change to that feature's own gate, and belongs to that
feature's release rather than to this one.

## The tools

| Tool | Default |
| --- | --- |
| `list_crops`, `get_crop` | **on** |
| `create_crop`, `update_crop` | **off** |
| `list_markets`, `get_market` | **on** |
| `create_market`, `update_market` | **off** |
| `list_ag_uom_contexts`, `get_uom_conversions` | **on** |

Six reads on, four writes off — the app's standing rule, unchanged.

Both update tools **replace child tables wholesale** when the table is passed,
and never merge. A merge needs a stable row key; these rows have none a caller
can see, and a half-merged variety list is worse than either operation done
plainly. Omitting the argument leaves the table alone; passing an empty list is
how a caller genuinely clears it.

Neither create tool can be re-keyed afterwards. The docname is the crop name and
the market name, and every record that names one spells it — so renaming is a new
record plus a migration of what pointed at the old one, not an edit.
