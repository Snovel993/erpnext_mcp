# v0.49.0 — the floor is paid, the premium is in it, and a day can be paid two ways

v0.48.2 wrote sixty-one scenario tests over the piecework pay rules and found
four places where the code and a reader's assumption came apart. It was a
test-only release: every gap was pinned as a passing assertion of the wrong
behaviour, on purpose, so that closing one would fail a test somebody had to
read. All four are closed here, and every one of those assertions was rewritten
deliberately.

This release changes what workers are paid. Read §1 and §4 before running a
period.

---

## 1. The higher-of rule is applied, not just reported

Ask most people how piecework pay works and they will say "the greater of the
piece total and minimum wage for the hours". Through v0.48.2 this app did not do
that. Forty-seven buckets at $1.50 in an eight-hour Oregon day paid **$70.50**,
the $47.10 shortfall was priced against the worker's name, and gross was left
alone.

**That was wrong, and not marginally.** FLSA §6, ORS 653.025 and RCW 49.46.020
all make the minimum wage a floor under the WAGE. A piece rate is a method of
measuring pay; it is not a way to earn less than the hours were worth. The same
day now pays **$117.60**.

```
min_wage_floor  = regular_hours × minimum + overtime_hours × minimum × 1.5
gross_pay       = max(earned_gross, min_wage_floor)
makeup          = max(0, min_wage_floor − earned_gross)
```

### The makeup is never silent, and that is the half worth keeping

The old posture existed for a real reason, stated in `payroll_integration.py`'s
header since v0.35.0: *"a payroll engine that quietly inflated gross pay would
hide the fact that a rate is set too low to be lawful."* That reason still holds.
So the top-up does not disappear into gross.

| where | what |
| --- | --- |
| `slip["earned_gross"]` | what the work itself came to |
| `slip["minimum_wage_makeup"]` | what the floor added |
| `slip["minimum_wage_makeup_by_state"]` | and in which state |
| `slip["minimum_wage_by_state"][ST]` | hours, floor, earned, makeup, paid, per state |
| Farm Payroll Slip | two new Currency columns, `earned_gross` and `minimum_wage_makeup` |
| run summary | `topped_up_to_minimum_wage`, `total_minimum_wage_makeup` |
| a journal entry | a warning naming the amount and saying the entry is fine and the rate is not |

Nobody on `topped_up_to_minimum_wage` is underpaid — that is the whole change.
Every name on it is a rate set below what the hours are owed on the work actually
being done. `below_minimum_wage` is now what it says: pay that did **not** reach
the floor, which on a slip this engine computed can only happen on a salary
structure (§1b) or on wages that arrived from somewhere else.

### 1b. One exception, on purpose: Salary is reported, not topped up

Whether a salaried employee is exempt from the minimum wage at all — executive,
administrative, professional, or one of the agricultural exemptions — is a fact
about their job that this app does not hold. Raising an exempt supervisor's pay
because a sixty-hour harvest week divided their salary below $14.70 would be
inventing an obligation.

So a salaried shortfall is computed and **reported**, exactly as every shortfall
was before this release, and somebody who knows the answer decides. Piece rate
and hourly carry no such question: those hours are owed the floor.

## 2. The floor carries the overtime premium

`check_minimum_wage_by_state` compared gross against `minimum × hours` — flat,
every hour alike. A picker who works fifty hours has a statutory floor of forty
at straight time plus ten at one and a half:

```
40 × $14.70  +  10 × $22.05  =  $808.50        not  50 × $14.70 = $735.00
```

Four hundred buckets at $1.50 over those fifty hours grossed $780 under v0.48.2
and **passed** the flat floor while being $28.50 short of the law. Nothing in the
app computed the $808.50, so nothing reported it.

Both checks carry the premium now — `payroll_calc.check_minimum_wage` takes
`overtime_hours`, `payroll_integration.check_minimum_wage_by_state` takes
`overtime_hours_by_state` — and `minimum_wage_floor()` is the one function both
use, so they cannot drift apart.

The premium follows the hours into the state they were worked in, which is the
same chronological split v0.35.0 already used to decide whose overtime it was.
Four Oregon tens and a Washington Friday: Oregon's floor is $588 with no premium
in it, Washington's is $249.90 with nothing but.

## 3. A worker can be part piece-rate and part hourly

`pay_type` was one field on one salary structure and `calculate_gross_pay`
branched on it once for a whole slip, so a picker who spent the morning on
buckets and the afternoon on irrigation was paid the piece rate for the
afternoon — which is nothing, because irrigation produces no buckets.

**Farm Shift and Farm Shift Crew Member now carry `pay_type` and `pay_rate`.**
Blank is the ordinary day and means "the way this worker's structure says"; the
field only has to be filled in on the day that was different. Farm Salary
Structure carries `hourly_rate`, the standing answer to "what is an hour of this
worker's non-piece time worth", so most sites set it once and never touch a
shift.

```
6h picking, 90 buckets × $1.50 = $135.00
2h irrigation,   2h × $16.00   =  $32.00
                                 -------
day                               $167.00     and the floor is tested on all 8h
```

**The method is 29 CFR 778.115**, the one the FLSA gives for an employee working
at two or more rates in one workweek: each stretch is paid its own way at
straight time, and the overtime premium is half of ONE regular rate blended
across the lot. Paying a premium per segment at that segment's own rate needs an
agreement under 778.419 and is not something a farm has.

`calculate_mixed_gross_pay` is a strict generalisation, not a second opinion:
`test_payroll.MixedPayTypes` runs single-pay-type inputs through both paths and
asserts they agree to the cent, because a worker's pay must not depend on which
branch their shift happened to take.

An hourly stretch with no rate anywhere earns nothing **at the rate**. Paying
irrigation hours $1.50 an hour because that is the bucket rate would look
deliberate; zero is loud, and the minimum wage makeup pays for those hours and
puts the fact on the slip.

## 4. The piece-rate overtime premium is half-time, not time and a half

The one figure in this release that goes **down**.

29 CFR 778.111: the regular rate for a piece worker is straight-time earnings
over hours worked, and the piece earnings have already paid straight time for
every hour — including the ones past forty. What is owed on top is therefore one
HALF of the regular rate.

Through v0.48.2 this app paid the full 1.5x on top. That was more than the law
asks, which is why it was left alone in v0.35.0 with the reasoning written down.
It could not survive §3: a mixed day has one blended regular rate and only the
half-time premium makes the two paths agree, and two piece-rate workers on the
same farm being paid by different rules depending on whether one of their days
had an hourly segment on it is worse than either rule.

```
400 buckets × $1.50 = $600 over 50 hours   →   regular rate $12.00/hr
v0.48.2   $600 + 10 × $12.00 × 1.5  =  $780.00
v0.49.0   $600 + 10 × $12.00 × 0.5  =  $660.00
```

**In practice this rarely reduces anybody's cheque, because §1 and §2 land on the
same slip.** That fifty-hour week is owed $808.50 by §2, so it pays $808.50 —
$28.50 more than v0.48.2 paid, not $120 less. The reduction only bites where
piece earnings already clear the overtime-inclusive floor comfortably, which is
the picker having a good week.

`OT_MULTIPLIER` (1.5, total) and `OT_PREMIUM_MULTIPLIER` (0.5, on top) are two
named halves of one rule. Hourly work is unchanged: its regular pay covered the
regular hours only, so the whole 1.5x lands on the overtime ones.

## 5. Weekly overtime — verified, not changed

The fourth item in the brief was a verification, and it verified.

- A ten-hour day in a thirty-hour week is **not** overtime. Neither state has a
  daily threshold and this app does not invent one.
- A forty-five hour week is five hours of overtime, and the day the fortieth hour
  falls on is split rather than counted whole either way.
- A multi-week pay period is several thresholds, not one. Forty-five hours in
  week one and thirty-five in week two is five hours of overtime; summing the
  period and comparing to eighty finds none of it.

All three were already right and are now asserted by name in
`test_piecework_rules.OvertimeUnderPieceRate`.

**Run `bench migrate`.** Five new columns across four doctypes. A bench running
this code against an unmigrated database keeps working — the shift and structure
reads go through `compat.existing_fields` — and simply loses the overrides.

Slips written before this release read back with `minimum_wage_makeup` of zero
and `earned_gross` equal to gross, which is exactly what they were.

## Tests

`tests_standalone/test_piecework_rules.py` grew from 61 to 87 and every claim
that asserted a gap now asserts it is shut. `test_payroll.MixedPayTypes` is new.
`test_payroll_integration.MinimumWageIsPerState.test_the_shortfall_is_not_added_to_gross`
is now `..._is_added_to_gross_and_still_named` — the reversal is the release.

`TheMixedDayThroughTheTools` seeds Farm Shift rows with the new columns on them
and goes through `preview_payroll_for_period` and `run_payroll_for_period`,
because a field that only exists in a fixture is a field a real site does not
have and the whole feature is a column.
