# v0.69.0 — Document Intelligence

*Sprint 4. Five tools, one DocType, nine columns on ERPNext's Item, and two
mobile routes.*

A phone reads a piece of paper; this decides whether to believe it.

---

## The gap

`classify_receipt` (v0.67.0) tells a capture screen which register a photograph
belongs in. Nothing then checks whether what the phone *read off* the photograph
is true.

That matters most on a pesticide label, because the numbers on one are numbers
somebody schedules a crew against. An REI misread as 12 hours instead of 120 is
a crew sent into a block four days early — and the record would look exactly
like a correct record, because the only copy of that number was the one the
phone produced.

## The pipeline is three stages, on purpose

Each stage is wrong in a way the next one can catch.

1. **On-device OCR and extraction.** Vision reads the label; extraction pulls
   `rei_hours`, `phi_days`, an EPA registration number out of what it read.
   Fast, offline, and *cannot tell whether what it read is true* — `0` and `O`
   are the same shape at 200 dpi in a dusty chemical shed.

2. **Deterministic validation** — `erpnext_mcp/document_intel.py`, pure, no
   database, no network, no model call. Everything checkable without judgement.

3. **LLM assessment** — judgement. Does this read like a real label; is the
   product plausible for the crop; is anything odd in a way no rule anticipated.

**Stage 2 is not a cheap approximation of stage 3.** A model asked whether
`524-537` is a well-formed EPA registration number will usually say yes, and
occasionally say yes about `S24-S37`. `EPA_REG_PATTERN` never does. The
deterministic stage is *better* than the judgement stage at the work it covers,
which is why it runs first and why a finding it makes cannot be overruled.

## There is still no model call anywhere in this app

`proposals.py` argues the architecture at length and it holds here unchanged:
**the AI is the MCP client.** A model reading a scanned label calls
`validate_document_extraction` and may hand its own assessment along in the same
call, shaped `{status, issues, confidence, reasoning}`. What the tool does is
what a tool can do and a model cannot do for itself — run the checks a model is
bad at, refuse an assessment that is the wrong shape, and record which model
said it.

A site with no model in the loop still gets every deterministic check, still
gets a stored record, and still gets told — in an issue with a code and a
message rather than in a silence — that the judgement half did not run.

## The rules that have teeth

Beyond the obvious presence-and-format checks, four are worth naming because
they catch things a careful person reading the screen would not:

- **The REI against the active ingredient it names.** `LABEL_REI_HOURS` holds
  the intervals for the actives that turn up in Pacific Northwest tree fruit, as
  *ranges*, because one active carries different intervals at different
  formulations and crops. A 12-hour REI beside phosmet is reported as a
  disagreement with 120–336 hours. **Always a warning, never a correction**: the
  label is the law and this table is a reader's memory of the label.

- **The PHI against the REI.** You cannot harvest a block you may not walk
  into. A PHI of 0 days beside a 120-hour REI is an error; a PHI merely shorter
  than the REI is a warning, because it does happen on a mechanically harvested
  label.

- **The ingredient statement summing past 100%.** An ingredient statement
  including inerts totals exactly 100, so a figure above it means a
  concentration came from the wrong column or a decimal point was lost.

- **The holder's name against the record it is filed against.** Tokens are
  sorted before comparing, so `Delgado, Ana Ruiz` and `Ana Ruiz Delgado` are one
  name — a straight ratio scores that at 0.55 and would flag every licence that
  prints surname first. A missing middle name is a warning; a different person
  is an error.

Every check that compares a field to what was actually *printed* needs
`ocr_text`. Without it those checks are skipped and an `info` issue says so
rather than the absence passing quietly.

## Corrections are proposals. Nothing applies them

`corrected_fields` comes back *beside* `extracted_fields` and never in place of
it, and each entry names the rule that proposed it and carries the reading it
would replace. A screen can show "the phone read `1O163-169`, the shape of an
EPA number says `10163-169`" and let a person decide.

An OCR correction applied silently is an OCR error nobody can find afterwards —
which is the whole reason `ocr_text` sits next to `extraction_json` on the
record where the two can be compared by somebody who trusts neither.

## Status, and what `Pending` actually means

`Pending` means *nothing definite was found and nothing has judged it*. It does
not mean "this is fine" and it does not mean "we did not look".

- A deterministic **error** outranks everything. An expired licence is expired
  whatever a model thinks of the photograph, so the merged status stays
  `Flagged` — or `Rejected` if the model went further.
- Otherwise the worst reading wins: `Rejected` > `Flagged` > `Pending` >
  `Validated`. The cost of looking at a document that turns out to be fine is a
  minute; the cost of the other mistake is an inspection finding.
- With no assessment at all, an `llm_validation_unavailable` issue names the
  reason, so a `Pending` record can always be traced to either *nothing judged
  it* or *something judged it and was unsure*.

**Confidence is the lower of the two readings, not their mean** — two
independent looks at one document, and if either is unconvinced the document is
not convincing. The deterministic half is itself two penalties *multiplied*, not
averaged: how much of what was read is wrong, times how much of the document was
read at all. A clean extraction that captured three fields out of eight does not
score as though it had been checked.

## Revalidation, and why the OCR text is kept

`revalidate_document` re-runs every check against the **stored** extraction.
Nothing is re-photographed and nothing is re-extracted. A label whose registered
intervals were revised, or a licence that has since expired, gets a fresh answer
without anybody walking back to the chemical shed.

`revalidation_count` increments and `last_revalidated` is stamped every run, so
a document revalidated four times and still `Flagged` is distinguishable from
one flagged this morning. The stored assessment is **reused** unless a new one
is passed — a re-run that dropped it would move a `Validated` record to
`Pending` and look like the document had gone stale, when what changed was only
that nobody re-sent the assessment.

**The document's own expiry beats the cadence.** A licence is not due for
revalidation on the anniversary of somebody scanning it; it is due when it
expires. And a type that never goes stale — a receipt, task evidence, a
signature — carries no due date and is never on the list, which is what stops a
site with ten thousand receipts and forty licences returning ten thousand rows.

## Nine columns on ERPNext's Item

The label's own numbers, on the product, so a Spray Log's `rei_hours` has
something to be checked *against* rather than being the only copy of a number
somebody typed from memory: `epa_registration_number`, `signal_word`,
`rei_hours`, `phi_days`, `phi_crop`, `active_ingredients`, `application_rate`,
`ppe_requirements`, and `label_scan_validation` linking back to the validation
they were read off.

**None is `reqd`, and it is not the usual backlog argument.** An Item is a bin,
a picking bag, a length of irrigation pipe and a box of gloves as well as a jug
of captan; a required REI would make every non-chemical Item unsaveable. A
`depends_on` hides them on anything that is not a chemical — matched on the item
group's *name* rather than a hard-coded list, and **shown unconditionally on any
Item that already carries an EPA number or a signal word**, because a
`depends_on` that hides data somebody has already entered is not a display
preference, it is a way to lose a record.

`compliance_fields.py` gained a `depends_on` field on `ComplianceField` to carry
this; it is the first target that needed one.

## The record carries a Company, and that was a decision

`Document Validation` links to `Company`, so a Company User Permission scopes it
at the framework level like every other register on the site.
`test_permissions.py` asks exactly this question of every new DocType — *does it
link to a Company, and if not, should it?* — and the answer here is that an
applicator licence's OCR text and an I-9 document's number are one entity's
personnel data. The value is resolved from the **source record's own** company
where there is one, so nobody has to say it twice.

## The two mobile routes are spelled differently from the contract

`POST /farmops/api/mobile/validate_document` and
`POST /farmops/api/mobile/get_document_validation`.

The Sprint 4 contract named `POST /farmops/api/validate-document` and
`GET /farmops/api/document-validation/<name>`. This transport builds every path
from the method's own name under `/mobile`, takes POST only, and matches whole
paths rather than patterns — a hyphen is not a Python identifier and a path
parameter has nowhere to land. Honouring the spelling would mean forking a
router whose closed, readable-in-one-screen table is its entire design, and
which `test_farmops_api.py` asserts in both directions. **The bodies and the
answers are the contract's, unchanged.**

`image_data` is accepted and deliberately not stored: stage the image with
`stage_file_chunk`/`finalize_staged_file` and pass `scan_file_url`, which is the
path every other image on this surface already takes.

`list_document_validations`, `list_revalidation_due` and `revalidate_document`
have no route. Two are an office's registers rather than anything a phone at a
shed reads, and the third re-decides a stored status, which is a supervisor's
call at a desk.

## What this release deliberately does not do

- **No sweep.** `revalidation_due` is a column `list_revalidation_due` reads.
  Nothing raises a Compliance Alert off it, nothing emails, nothing is
  scheduled. That is a later release's decision.
- **No writing back to the Item.** `label_scan_validation` links an Item to a
  validation; nothing in this release copies a validated REI onto the product.
  Applying a machine's reading to master data is a person's call, and the column
  that records whether a person made it is `human_confirmed`.
- **No OCR.** This app does not read an image. Extraction is on the device,
  where it is fast and offline, and that has not changed.
