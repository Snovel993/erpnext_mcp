# v0.64.0 — what happened to Ana, and which shift the work was done on

Two pieces of work and two bugs that fell out of trying to write the first one.

The shift has anchored compliance since v0.19.3 and collected its own weather
since v0.19.4. What it could not do was answer a question about **one person on
it**, or tell you which shift a **task** was done on. Both gaps had the same
shape: the data was already stored and nothing read it.

---

## 1. The crew is not one person

`Farm Shift Crew Member` has carried `joined_at` and `left_at` since the crew
table shipped, and `erpnext_mcp/shifts.py` argued at the time why:

> The obvious objection to a crew-shaped record is that payroll is person-shaped,
> and it would be a real objection if the crew table were a list of names. It is
> not.

Payroll took the argument up. Compliance never did. Every exposure read on the
shift surface — the weather timeline, the heat record, the threshold events — is
scoped to the **crew**, and a crew that turned over across the afternoon is a
crew for whom that reading is wrong in both directions at once.

Ana joined at 09:40 and left at 13:00. The shift crossed 80 °F at 10:00 and
peaked at 97 °F at three. A heat record scoped to the crew says Ana was exposed
to 97 °F for a nine-hour shift. She was there for three hours and twenty minutes
of it, and she was present for two of the five water breaks — the other three
were called before she arrived and after she left.

`get_shift_crew_timeline` is the read that says so.

### Every figure against the worker's own span

```json
{
  "employee": "HR-EMP-00010",
  "joined_at": "2026-07-24 11:30:00",
  "present_until": "2026-07-24 15:00:00",
  "hours_present": 3.5,
  "pay_type": "Piece Rate", "pay_rate": 4.25, "pay_basis_from": "crew row",
  "exposure": {
    "readings_in_span": 14,
    "peak_temp_f": 97.0,
    "readings_at_or_above_the_heat_threshold": 14,
    "first_crossing_in_span": "2026-07-24 11:30:00",
    "minutes_bracketed_by_crossings": 195.0,
    "present_at_shift_first_crossing": false
  },
  "events_in_span": 3,
  "care_events_in_span": 2,
  "breaks": { "rest_owed": 1, "rest_taken": 1, "meal_owed": 0, "short": false }
}
```

`present_at_shift_first_crossing: false` is the one to read. The shift crossed at
10:00; **Ana's** clock starts at 11:30, because -1131's obligations run from the
crossing **for the people who were exposed to it**. The shift-level
`arrived_after_the_first_crossing` list is the worklist behind it.

`care_events_in_span` counts the water, shade, rest and observation events inside
the worker's envelope, plus the Individual-scoped ones naming them. A crew break
at 08:00 is not care given to somebody who arrived at 09:40, and counting it
would flatter the operation in exactly the place an investigator checks.

### Nothing is interpolated, and the field name says so

`minutes_bracketed_by_crossings` is a **bracket** — first at-or-above reading in
the span to the last — not a sum of exposure. The readings are samples every
fifteen minutes and the temperature between two of them is a thing nobody
measured. `sample_gap_minutes` reports the real cadence beside it, because an
afternoon reconstructed hourly from the archive brackets far more loosely than a
live quarter-hour timeline and the two must not read alike. A single at-or-above
reading brackets `0.0`: a moment is not a duration.

`breaks` is per person and **null** without a policy, not zero. Entitlement is a
function of hours worked, so the four-hour picker and the ten-hour foreman are
owed different numbers across the same afternoon.

## 2. Which shift the work was done on

`Farm Task` and `Farm Shift` were two registers with nothing between them. A
completion carries a point in time — this cabin, this worker, these two
photographs, 11:52. A shift carries the period an exposure regime asks about.
Both records could be perfect and still leave nobody able to answer "what was
done during the July 15 shift" without reconciling two registers by hand, which
is the reconciliation that does not happen on the afternoon somebody asks.

Both doctypes gain a `farm_shift` link, and they are **not** the same field. The
task's says which shift the work was **raised for**; the assignment's says which
it was **done on**. They diverge the moment a job dispatched for the morning gets
finished after lunch, and a re-entry-interval question needs both.

### It can be set at four moments, and inferred at one

`create_farm_task`, `assign_farm_task`, `start_farm_task` and
`complete_farm_task` all take `farm_shift`. Each writes it **only when given** —
blanking a link the clock-in established would silently detach a completion from
the compliance record it belongs on.

The clock-in also **infers** it, because nobody types a shift docname into a
phone: from the one open shift the worker is rostered on, and only when there is
exactly one. Two open shifts naming the same person infer nothing and say so.
Guessing would put the evidence on a crew that was somewhere else, which is a
false entry on a compliance record rather than a missing one — and those are not
equally bad.

A shift at another company is **refused by name**. A completion filed against
another entity's shift is the one direction an auditor cannot unpick.

### The evidence flows onto the shift's own timeline

A completion with a shift appends one `Task Completed` event — its own event
type, not `Other`, because a timeline where every dispatched job reads "Other" is
one nobody can scan, and scanning it is why an inspector opens a shift record.

The event carries the timestamp, the worker who filed it, a sentence naming the
work and its outcome, the signature file, and the weather **as it stood at or
before** the work finishing. 11:52 sits between the 11:45 and 12:00 readings, and
reaching forward would stamp the record with a measurement that did not exist
yet.

It does **not** copy the photographs. Evidence lives on the assignment; the event
names it in `producer_record_name`. The event is a pointer with enough on it to
be read without following.

`logged_by` **is** written here and is left empty by the weather sweep. Somebody
did this work and signed for it, so naming them is the record being true. Nobody
observed a temperature — the sweep did.

One event per assignment, and a replay writes nothing: `shift_evidence` on an
idempotent resubmission reads back what the first call put there.

`get_shift` answers from the other side with `farm_tasks` — the work still open
on the crew, which no event can be because it has not happened yet.
`list_dispatch_board` takes `farm_shift` and reports `by_shift` and
`not_anchored_to_a_shift` when it does not.

## 3. The calendar looks again at the moment the world changed

v0.17.1 moved the sweep from nightly to hourly and said why:

> Nightly meant a worker who walked a cabin at eight in the morning saw the alert
> asking them to walk it for the rest of the day, on the phone, having just done
> the work.

Hourly closed that from a day to an hour. This closes it to nothing.

`refresh_compliance_alerts` grew an `alert_types` allowlist with the **same
promise its `regime` filter has**: a rule outside the narrowing raises nothing
and **dismisses nothing**, because a narrowed sweep that cleared the rules it did
not run would empty most of the calendar and look like progress.

`complete_farm_task` calls it for two sets of rules:

- the rule that **raised** the task, which is by definition the one whose
  condition the work was meant to change;
- every rule whose target register is the record the completion **produced**.
  Nobody had to link the task to the rule — the **record** is the link, and this
  is the half that makes a task compliance-native rather than merely
  compliance-adjacent. A spray task filing an application record, a detector
  round filing an inspection, a water task filing a test: the rule that asks
  "when was the last one" reads exactly that register.

**It is the sweep, called sooner — not a shortcut around it.** Nothing dismisses
an alert because a task was completed; the rule re-runs and its own condition
decides. A task completed against a condition that is still true leaves the alert
standing, which is the outcome that matters most: doing the work and fixing the
problem are two different facts, and both have to be reportable.

`compliance_evaluation` on the result says which rules were asked and what they
decided. It is null where a completion could not have changed any rule's answer —
a hand-raised task producing no record is exactly that, and saying so is
different from silence.

The hourly job is untouched and still does the work that matters most, because
most of what makes an alert stale is not a task completion at all. A certificate
lapsing at midnight is nobody's tap on a phone.

## 4. Two bugs the crew envelope found

Writing a test that logged a break and read it back through an envelope failed
twice, for two unrelated reasons, neither of which had a test and neither of
which announced itself.

**`log_shift_break` failed on every call since v0.58.0.** It read its duration
with `as_float(args, "duration_minutes")` — but `as_float` takes the **value**
first and the label second, as all hundred-odd of its other call sites do. So it
passed the whole request dict where a number belonged, `float({...})` raised, and
the refusal quoted the entire request payload back as the offending "number". The
tool was unreachable and the error leaked the caller's own body.

**`EVENT_FIELDS` never fetched the six break columns.** They have been on
`Farm Shift Compliance Event` since v0.58.0 and `shifts.events_of` did not select
them, so their absence was not a missing key on a payload — it was silent wrong
arithmetic in three places at once:

- `describe_event_row`'s break branch is gated on `break_kind`, so it could never
  be entered and no break event ever reported its kind or duration;
- `breaks.worker_breaks` skips an Individual break naming somebody else — with
  `applies_to` absent it defaulted to **Crew**, so one person's cool-down counted
  as a break taken by the whole crew;
- the same function totals `duration_minutes`, absent and therefore read as zero,
  so paid and unpaid break minutes were **zero on every shift**, including in
  `get_shift_production`.

The second hid the first: with the columns unfetched, nothing downstream could
tell a broken break from an absent one.

Both are fixed and both have regression tests, including one asserting that a
genuinely non-numeric duration is still refused and that the refusal names only
the field.

## Migration

`bench --site <site> migrate`. Three doctypes gain a field, one gains a Select
option, and `ERPNext MCP Settings` gains the switch for the new read tool.

Nothing is backfilled and nothing needs to be. A task raised before this release
carries no shift, reports none, and its completions behave exactly as they did —
the link is additive, and `not_anchored_to_a_shift` on the dispatch board is the
honest measure of how much of a farm's work has been anchored so far.

The catalogue is now **413 tools, 189 read**.
