# v0.33.0 — the shape can be drawn, and the county already has it

**Open a Parcel, press the polygon tool, trace the block against the satellite
image, press Save Boundary. Or type the tax lot number and let the assessor's own
polygon do it.**

Tool surface **unchanged**. No new MCP tools, no new doctypes, no new hook keys.
Two whitelisted Desk methods, a draw toolbar on three forms, and a county GIS
proxy.

---

## The thing this release had to argue its way past

v0.32.0 shipped a map on seven forms and said, at length, that it would never
write:

> Nothing here writes. The map is read-only in every direction — there is no
> drag-to-move marker, no draw-a-polygon tool, and no save path of any kind. A
> boundary is compliance evidence and it is set through `set_field_boundary`,
> `set_zone_boundary` or `set_parcel_boundary`, which validate the shape, refuse
> a self-intersection, compare the area against the recorded acreage and
> recompute every derived field. A map that could nudge a vertex would be a way
> to change all of that by accident, with no validation and no audit row.

Every word of that is still true, and it is the reason this release is shaped the
way it is rather than the reason it should not exist.

**The thing being refused was a map that wrote to the field.** Nothing here does.
The draw tools produce a polygon; the polygon goes to
`erpnext_mcp.api.gis.save_boundary`; that calls `set_parcel_boundary`,
`set_field_boundary` or `set_zone_boundary` — the same three tools the model
calls, unchanged, with every check they have always made. A vertex nudged by
accident does not get saved quietly; it gets an area disagreement and a refusal,
on screen, before anything changes.

**And the field was always writable anyway.** `boundary_geojson` is an ordinary
Long Text on all three doctypes with no `read_only` flag: anybody with write
permission could already paste a polygon into the form and press save — which
stores it with nothing derived, no validation, no area check, and a centroid left
over from the previous shape. This endpoint is strictly the safer of the two
doors, and it is the one with a map next to it.

## What changed

### `erpnext_mcp/api/gis.py` — two whitelisted methods

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `save_boundary(doctype, name, geojson, dry_run=0)` | validates the caller, then calls the boundary tool for that doctype |
| `query_county_parcels(county, tax_lot, lat, lon)` | asks a county's ArcGIS parcel layer, by tax lot number or by a point |

**The gate had to be rebuilt.** `security.authorize()` — the master switch, the
shared `X-MCP-Token`, the CIDR allowlist — is called by `mcp.handle` and never
runs on a `@frappe.whitelist()` path. `api/guard.py` is the wrong gate for this
door: its fourth check is an Active Mobile Access Grant, which would mean the
operator could not draw a boundary on their own Desk until they had enrolled
themselves as a field device.

So:

1. a named user — Guest is refused before anything is read or fetched,
2. `frappe.has_permission(doctype, "write", doc=name, throw=True)` on the
   **specific** document, so a User Permission scoping somebody to one company
   scopes this too,
3. a closed list of three doctypes. No dispatcher, no method-name argument.

**Check 2 matters more than it looks.** The boundary tools end in
`doc.save(ignore_permissions=True)` — correct for them, because the MCP transport
authorised three layers earlier — so a wrapper that forgot to ask would have
handed every signed-in account on the site a write to every parcel.

**The `allow_<tool>` switches are deliberately not consulted**, the same call
`api/__init__.py` made for the phone in v0.17.1. Those switches are the model's
leash. `allow_set_parcel_boundary` off means "the model may not redraw the farm",
and reading it here would mean an operator who distrusts the AI also loses the
ability to trace a parcel by hand — which is not what the switch says and not
what they asked for. There is a test that turns the switch off, watches the MCP
tool refuse, and watches the Desk path succeed.

### `geo_map_widget.js` — a draw toolbar on three forms

Parcel, Field and Irrigation Zone get `attach_editable_boundary`: a Leaflet.draw
toolbar (polygon, rectangle, edit, delete), a **Save Boundary** button that is
disabled until something changes, and a **Revert** button.

- **One record, one boundary.** Drawing a second shape replaces the first; the
  far commoner case is somebody redrawing because the first attempt was wrong.
- **Two shapes become a MultiPolygon**, not a refusal. A parcel cut in half by a
  county road is two pieces of one parcel, `geo.parse` accepts a MultiPolygon,
  and telling somebody to draw it as one shape would mean drawing a boundary
  across a road that is not theirs.
- **`allowIntersection: false`** is the client-side half of the check
  `geo.validate_geometry` makes on the server. The server check is not removed —
  it is what protects the other two callers.
- **The editable map renders on a record with no boundary at all**, which the
  read-only version deliberately does not. An empty map is nothing to look at and
  everything to draw on, and a parcel with no shape yet is exactly the record
  somebody opens meaning to trace it. It opens over The Dalles at zoom 12 when
  there is nothing at all to fit to — a hardcoded default, argued in the file, on
  the grounds that the honest alternatives are a world view nobody can start from
  and a geocode of a farm road.
- **The other four map forms are untouched.** Housing Unit, Asset Register, Farm
  Shift and Farm Task draw a marker or a track and stay exactly as read-only as
  they were.

**No area is computed in the browser.** Leaflet.draw offers a live acreage readout
while you drag; taking it would put a second area implementation in the app, in a
different language, and the day it disagreed with `geo.area_acres` by three per
cent nobody would know which figure the compliance record was built on. The server
answers with the area it actually stored, after the save, and that is the only
figure shown.

**Warnings get a modal, not a toast.** Every warning the boundary tools emit is a
thing somebody has to decide about — a block that now hangs outside its parcel, an
acreage that disagrees by eight per cent, three zones left outside the shape. A
toast that fades after four seconds is how those get missed.

### Satellite imagery is the default layer

Esri World Imagery (free, no key), with OpenStreetMap one click away in a layer
control:

```
https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/tile/{z}/{y}/{x}
```

Note `{y}` **before** `{x}` — that is an ArcGIS tile path (`/tile/{z}/{row}/{col}`)
and not the XYZ order every other tile URL uses. Getting it round the wrong way
produces a map of somewhere else entirely rather than an error.

A street map cannot be traced against. An orchard block's corner is a change in
canopy, a headland or a road edge — all visible in imagery, none of them on a
street map, which for most of this county draws two roads and a lot of white. OSM
is still the better layer for finding a cabin by its driveway, which is why it is
one click away rather than gone.

Both attributions are in the layer options and are the condition of use rather
than a courtesy.

### The Wasco County import, on Parcel and no other form

```
https://public.co.wasco.or.us/gisserver/rest/services/Taxlots/FeatureServer/0/query
```

| Query | Parameters |
| --- | --- |
| by tax lot | `where=MapTaxlot='2N11E35BA-01600'&outFields=*&outSR=4326&f=geojson` |
| by point | `geometry={"x":-121.18,"y":45.6}&geometryType=esriGeometryPoint&inSR=4326&spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects&outFields=*&outSR=4326&f=geojson` |

Fields read: `MapTaxlot`, `Taxpayer`, `CalculatedAcres`, `AccountNum` — each with
a list of alternative spellings, because an ArcGIS layer's field names are
whatever the person who published it typed.

**Why Parcel and no other form.** A county publishes tax lots, and a tax lot is
the unit the assessor, the deed and the tax bill all agree on — which is exactly
what a Parcel is. It has never heard of an orchard block or an irrigation zone, so
an import button on those forms would be an invitation to set a block's boundary
to the whole lot it happens to sit in.

**The result is previewed before it is applied.** Dashed purple, on the satellite
image, next to the block the operator already knows, with both acreages and the
taxpayer name beside it. A tax lot number typed with one character wrong returns a
real parcel, somewhere else, and every number on it looks plausible — drawn, it is
obviously the wrong piece of ground in under a second. **Apply** is what commits
it, through `save_boundary` like everything else.

**Both acreages are always shown, never one.** The county's figure is computed on
its own projected grid; this app's is spherical, from the same polygon. They agree
to a fraction of a per cent when the import is right, and a reader who can see
both can tell a projection difference from the wrong parcel.

### What the import fills in, and what it refuses to

An empty field being filled is not an overwrite. A field that already holds a
value is left exactly where it is, and the difference is reported.

| Form field | From the county |
| --- | --- |
| `parcel_id` | `MapTaxlot` — filled if blank |
| `acreage` | `CalculatedAcres` — filled if blank |
| `county` | the service's own label, trimmed to "Wasco" |
| `title_holder` | **never** |

**The acreage matters more than it looks.** `set_parcel_boundary` refuses a
polygon that disagrees with the recorded acreage by more than a quarter — so a
parcel recorded at 131 acres and a tax lot that is really 41 will be refused by
the server, which is the correct outcome and usually means the wrong lot was
imported. Filling a *blank* acreage from the county is safe for the opposite
reason: the county's acres and the county's polygon are the same measurement, so
they cannot disagree with each other.

**`title_holder` is never touched.** It is a Link to a Related Party on this site;
the county's `Taxpayer` is free text off a tax roll. Resolving one to the other by
string match is how a parcel ends up owned by the wrong entity in an accounting
system. It is shown, and left to a person.

## The three mistakes this integration is most likely to make

All three have tests, because all three fail *quietly*.

**1. `x` is longitude and `y` is latitude.** That is the opposite order from every
other coordinate pair in this app. Swapping them asks about 45.6°E, −121.18°N — a
point in the Southern Ocean — so the answer is an empty result rather than a wrong
one, and nothing would ever say what actually happened.
`test_x_is_longitude_and_y_is_latitude` reads the outgoing parameters.

**2. An ArcGIS error is an HTTP 200.** The service answers
`{"error": {"code": 400, "message": "…"}}` with a 200 and a JSON content type and
no `features` key at all, so a client that checks only the status code reports a
malformed query as "the county has never heard of your parcel". It is checked by
name, first, in `parse_features`.

**3. `outSR=4326` is the whole integration.** The layer stores Oregon Stateplane
North in **feet** (WKID 2913). A polygon in feet parses as perfectly valid GeoJSON
whose coordinates are somewhere near longitude 7,600,000 —
`check_coordinates_look_like_degrees` would flag it and nothing would fix it.

## Security posture

**Nothing here is a general HTTP proxy.** The host and path come from the
`COUNTIES` literal in `api/gis.py`. The caller chooses a key in that dict and
supplies a tax lot or a coordinate pair; there is no argument from which a URL
could be built, and a test asserts every configured URL is HTTPS and on a county
domain.

**The tax lot is an allowlist, not an escape.** It is formatted into
`MapTaxlot='…'`, which is a SQL-ish expression evaluated by somebody else's
server, so anything that is not letters, digits, dots and hyphens is refused
outright rather than quoted or stripped. Escaping would mean implementing another
vendor's dialect correctly with no way to test it. `'; DROP TABLE taxlots; --`,
`2N11E35BA-01600' OR '1'='1` and a bare space are all refused by name.

**The county lookup needs write permission on Parcel, not read.** The only thing
an imported polygon is for is setting a parcel's boundary; gating on `read` would
leave the site hosting an outbound HTTP fetch that any signed-in account — a
Family Member, an Advisor — could drive.

**The outbound request is bounded**: a 15-second timeout, a 4 MB cap on the
response body read in chunks, and 20 features maximum handed back to a form, with
the truncation reported rather than silent.

**Every call leaves an audit row** in MCP Action Log, prefixed `desk:` so it
sorts and filters apart from the model's `mobile:` and bare tool rows.

## Degradation

`requests` is imported defensively, like shapely, h3 and segno before it. It is a
Frappe dependency and every real bench has it; a bench that somehow does not loses
the county lookup **by name**, with the pip command, rather than failing to import
the module and taking `save_boundary` down with it. Drawing a boundary by hand
needs no network at all and keeps working.

Leaflet, Leaflet.draw and both stylesheets are fetched from a CDN by the widget
when a form that needs them is opened. There is no `app_include_js` or
`app_include_css` — both would put an asset on every Desk page for every user,
most of whom will never open a Parcel — so the draw plugin is not fetched at all
on the four read-only map forms, and a bench with no outbound internet gets the
same sentence-plus-coordinates fallback v0.32.0 shipped, with a line added saying
that drawing needs the map.
