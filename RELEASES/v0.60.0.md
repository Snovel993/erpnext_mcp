# v0.60.0 — the evidence chain behind a signature

An auditor's first question is *was it signed*. This app has answered that since
v0.55.0: the drawn image, the moment and the address, on the box the form
prints, which is what 8 CFR § 274a.2(h) asks for.

Their second question is **how do you know it was him**, and until this release
the honest answer was that a phone said so.

A PNG in a column proves that somebody drew a shape on a piece of glass. Five
things turn that into a record that survives a challenge:

1. the document was **presented** — the signer saw it before signing;
2. **identity was verified at the pad** — a badge scanned, an employee ID
   tapped, a photograph taken. Not asserted afterwards by whoever filed it;
3. the **signature** was captured;
4. **context** was captured — when, on what device, at what coordinates, from
   what address;
5. the artefact is **tamper-evident** — what was shown is hashed, so a form
   altered after signing stops matching what the signer attested to.

Step 3 already existed. This release is 1, 2, 4 and 5's hash. The composited
page that renders the whole packet is a follow-up, and it will be drawn *from*
these rows rather than from a second copy of the same facts.

---

## 1. `Signing Evidence` — one row per signature event

Not six more columns on the I-9. A signature event is not a property of a form:
one Form I-9 carries three of them, signed by two different people in two
different capacities on two different days; a W-4 carries one; a training
acknowledgment carries one per attendee. Columns would mean
`section_2_signer_badge`, `section_2_device_id`, `section_2_gps_latitude` — and
the same six again for Supplement B, and again on every form that grows a
signature line. That is the table sprawl this app's design philosophy names
outright, and it makes *"show me every signature this foreman collected in
July"* a query that has to know every form in advance.

One row per event is the shape the question has:

| | |
|---|---|
| `signer`, `signer_name`, `signer_user` | who signed, and separately **which account made the call** — one authorised person filing on behalf of another is a real thing in a packing shed |
| `signer_badge`, `verification_method` | the identity step: `Badge QR`, `Employee ID` or `Photo`, and the card that was scanned |
| `document_type`, `document_name`, `signature_field`, `signature_role` | which record, which box on it, and in what capacity |
| `signature_image`, `document_hash`, `hashed_fields` | the capture, and what the record said when it was shown |
| `device_id`, `ip_address`, `gps_latitude`, `gps_longitude` | the context |
| `company`, `signed_at`, `status`, `supersedes` | |

**Append-only, and more strictly than the other logs.** An audit row that can be
edited is a weakened audit trail; an *evidence* row that can be edited is not
evidence. The controller refuses every write after the insert, `in_create` takes
the Desk's New button away, and **there is no tool that creates one** — the
signature path writes them and nothing else does. A tool that could add a row
would be a tool that could manufacture an identity check that never happened.

A replaced signature **appends**: `overwrite=true` writes a new row naming the
old one in `supersedes`, and the old row keeps saying exactly what it said.

## 2. Identity is refused, not recorded, when it fails

**A badge that resolves to somebody else stops the call**, before the image is
stored, on the boxes the worker signs in their own name. Either the wrong person
is at the pad or the wrong form is open, and a signature filed across that gap
would attest under one person's penalty of perjury to another person's document.
Resolution goes through `resolve_badge`, which already refuses an unknown card, a
retired one, one from another entity and one whose holder has left — four
sentences an operator can act on, and four ways an identity check can be quietly
wrong if it is written a second time.

**Verification that fails open is not verification.** `verification_method:
"Badge QR"` with no badge attached is refused rather than recorded: the column
would look like proof and hold nothing.

**No badge at all is not an error.** An operator signing a 941 at a desk has no
card to scan. The row says `Unverified` and the register reports how many of
those it holds, rather than claiming a check that did not happen.

**The capacity comes off the box, not off the caller.** Section 1 of a Form I-9
is a worker attesting under their own penalty of perjury; Section 2 is the
employer attesting that it examined that worker's documents. A caller may state a
`signature_role` and it is *checked* against the box — never believed over it. A
client that could label them would be choosing which of two legal acts it had
just performed.

## 3. The authorized-signer check now asks the whole question

"May this account sign" was only ever half of one. The roster in I-9 Settings
answers **which form**; the entity whose record is being signed is a Company User
Permission, checked somewhere else. A signer on the roster and scoped to a
different farm used to be refused with a sentence about `write` permission —
true, and it sends an operator to the permission manager when the roster is where
they need to look, or the other way round.

`signers.authorized_signer_for_company` asks both and names the actual gap.

**The roster does not grow a company column**, deliberately. Who this employer
has authorised to examine documents is a question about the employer, and on a
multi-entity family operation the same three people sign for all of it — a
per-company roster would be three copies of one list, and the copy somebody
forgot to update would be a signature refused in the packing shed. An account
with no User Permission stays unrestricted: Frappe's rule, kept so an operator's
own login works, with the fail-closed reading on the mobile door where
`guard.require_scope` refuses a phone with no entities outright.

## 4. The document hash, and why it is quiet

Taken **before** the signature is written — "what did they see" is a fact about
the document at presentation, and a fingerprint computed afterwards proves only
that this app had just written a signature.

The naive version — hash every column — is correct and useless. A Form I-9 is
signed by the worker in July and by the employer in August, and the employer's
half *fills in columns that were empty in July*. Under a whole-document hash,
completing a form correctly would report the worker's own attestation as
tampered, and an integrity check that fires on every properly-handled form is one
nobody reads.

So the hash covers **the columns that held something when the record was
presented**, minus the signature columns, the rendered PDF and the workflow
status — all of which this app writes as a consequence of somebody signing.

* information **added** later does not change it. The signer was shown a blank
  box and attested to nothing about it;
* information **changed** later does. The value in the hash is the one they were
  shown;
* information **erased** later does. The key drops out of the recomputed set.

That rule cannot be re-derived after the fact, so the row stores the field list
beside the hash — which also answers, without anybody taking it on trust, *which
parts of this form does this signature vouch for*. `get_signing_evidence`
recomputes over exactly those fields on every read.

## 5. What the phone sends, and what comes back

`submit_form_signature` declares five more arguments: `signer_badge`,
`verification_method`, `device_id`, `gps_lat`, `gps_lon`.

`gps_lat` and `gps_lon` are in `API_CONTRACT.md` §14.2 and have been **dropped**
since v0.57.0 — by the documented mechanism, `routes.bind` keeping only the keys
a signature declares. The reason was never that a location is worth nothing; it
was that the server had nowhere to put one, and a route that accepted data it
discarded would be worse than one that refused it. There is somewhere now.

`signed_on` is still dropped. §14.2 stamps it when the pad opened, and a handset
that could set the attestation's timestamp could backdate it.

The answer carries `evidence`, `evidence_status` and `evidence_note` — reported
rather than silent, for the same reason `task_note` is. The person at the pad has
done everything asked of them either way, and an operation that is quietly
collecting the weaker kind of evidence should find that out from the answer
rather than from an auditor.

**The idempotent retry writes no evidence row and says so.** That branch exists
not to write, and a row for a signature that was not collected would be the
register asserting an identity check on a call that made none — on the path where
it would happen most.

## 6. Two reads, no writes

| Tool | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `list_signing_evidence` | **on** | by document, signer, badge, capacity, company or date range. Reports `unverified_count` separately — a register that only gave its total would hide the rows that cannot answer the question it exists for |
| `get_signing_evidence` | **on** | one event in full, hash re-checked against the record as it stands now, plus `superseded_by` |

**Permissions.** Farm Manager and Compliance Officer read it. System Manager and
HR Manager hold it fully, from the doctype's own permissions. **No role in this
app gets write access** — including the two that read it. That is not caution
about a new doctype: a grant that could not be used would still say, in the one
register whose value is that nobody edits it, that somebody is expected to.

A Foreman does not get it — the evidence trail over signatures collected on a
board is checked by somebody who is not running the board. A Field Worker does
not get it: badge IDs, device identifiers and coordinates for every signature on
the operation is a movement record for the whole crew. Family Member and Advisor
do not get it; whose I-9 was signed where is the operating company's personnel
file.

`Signing Evidence.company` is a required Link, so **whose** evidence a reader may
see stays Frappe's question and not this app's — no query condition of its own.

## Upgrading

```sh
bench --site <site> migrate
```

Nothing changes for a site that upgrades and sends no new arguments: signatures
are collected exactly as before and each one writes an `Unverified` evidence row
— a true statement about a signature captured without an identity check. Wiring
the badge scan into the pad is what turns those into `Recorded`.

`tests_standalone/test_signing_evidence.py`, 60 assertions over six claims: the
