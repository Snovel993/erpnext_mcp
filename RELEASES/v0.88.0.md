# v0.88.0 — the block has a life, and a fiscal year cannot see it

Wave 3: the spray program, the crop protection pipeline, and the value block
lifecycle. Thirteen doctypes, twenty tools.

They ship together because they are one chain. A scout finds something. A
threshold decides whether it is worth answering. The answer is often a spray. The
spray shuts a block for a number of hours. And every hour and every gallon of it
lands against the ground that consumed it.

---

## The one idea

A tree fruit block planted in 2021 spends four years costing money and returning
none, then a decade returning more than it costs, then a decline somebody has to
decide the end of.

```
              2021   2022   2023   2024   2025   2026   …   2035
  cost         ███    ███    ███    ██     ██     ██          ██
  revenue       ·      ·      ·     ▁▁     ███    ████        ██
  status    ── establishing ────────┼── productive ──────┼ declining
```

Read one of those years as a profit and loss statement and the block is a
catastrophe. Read the whole life and it is the best investment on the farm.

**Every general ledger in existence answers the first question and none answers
the second**, because the ledger's period is the fiscal year and the block's
period is fifteen of them. That is not a defect in the ledger. It is a different
question, and it needs a record with a plant year that stays fixed and a season
year that moves.

So `get_block_profitability` refuses to call an establishing block's negative
margin a loss:

```
  is_meaningful_as_profit_and_loss: false
  verdict: "NOT A PROFIT AND LOSS. This planting is Establishing in leaf year 4
            — it is not expected to return anything yet, and the 176,000.00 it
            is down is establishment INVESTMENT carried in the block's basis,
            not a loss against a crop."
```

Every figure is still reported. The numbers are real and somebody needs them.
What the app will not do is put the word *loss* next to them.

## Three features, and the seam between each pair

### 1. The spray program

`Spray Tank Mix` is the **recipe**; `Spray Application` is the **event**. That
split is what lets a farm approve one mix in March and file forty applications
against it without forty chances to retype a rate.

Several products in one tank, **each at its own rate per acre**. Not one rate for
the tank: a cover spray is two or three answers to two or three different
problems, and only a per-product rate can be checked against a label.

Dual flip nozzles are first class, and the two halves live in different places
for a reason:

|  | lives on | because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which product is on set A or B | the tank mix product line | it is part of the recipe |
| Which set was running on a block | the application's block row | the flip happens at a block edge |

A farm that recorded only the first would have no way to say which block got
which product.

**The seam with v0.78.0.** `create_spray_application` does **not** refuse a tank
with no computable label interval. `record_spray_application` **does**. Both are
right:

|  | `record_spray_application` | `create_spray_application` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| purpose | the restricted-entry window | the record of the pass |
| foliar nutrient, no REI | **refuses** | records, opens 0 windows, says so |
| why | a zero-hour window reads as *this block is clear* | refusing pushes a real pass onto a clipboard |

Getting either backwards is the failure that matters. One puts somebody in a
treated row; the other loses the record a state inspector asks for.

**Weather is recorded, never enforced.** Wind above the label window is drift.
Wind **below** it is a temperature inversion — still air holds the spray in a
layer that moves further off-target than a breeze would have — which is the half
people forget, so it earns an advisory of its own. A refusal would not prevent
the spray; the tank went out three hours ago. It would only prevent the *record*
of it, which is the half an inspector asks for and the operator is least
motivated to keep.

### 2. Crop protection

Six threat categories: Insect, Disease, Weed, Vertebrate, **Abiotic**,
**Nutrient**. The last two are what every pest register leaves out and every
grower records anyway — frost and hail have no organism behind them, and a tissue
deficiency is a finding a threshold has to be able to express.

Which is why **the comparison is a column, not an assumption**:

```
  Insect   Codling Moth   Greater Than  5.0   per trap      ⇒ act ABOVE
  Nutrient Boron          Less Than     2.2   % dry weight  ⇒ act BELOW
```

A hard-coded greater-than would fire on every healthy block and never on a
deficient one. Wrong in both directions at once, and silently.

**Beneficials override the threshold**, and that is the whole difference between
integrated pest management and pest counting:

```
  mites 10/leaf   threshold 5/leaf   OVER
  predators 8     ratio 0.8          minimum 0.5   ⇒ HOLD AND RE-SCOUT
```

The block is already handling itself. The spray that "fixes" it kills the
predators and guarantees a worse flare in three weeks. The hold **replaces** the
control options rather than joining them — presenting *hold* alongside *cover
spray* is presenting no recommendation at all.

**The sustainability scale**, 0–100, weighting the IPM ladder:

| Cultural | No Action | Biological | Mechanical | Behavioral | Unclassified | Chemical |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.90 | 0.75 | 0.70 | 0.30 | 0.20 |

Two of those numbers are arguable and both are deliberate. **Chemical is not
zero** — a correctly timed threshold-driven spray *is* integrated pest
management, and scoring it zero would tell a farm its best chemical decision was
worth the same as its worst. **Unclassified sits below chemical** — an action
nobody categorised cannot be shown to have been anything else, and a scale that
gave it the benefit of the doubt would reward not filling the field in.

Once anything is accepted, the score describes what was **chosen** rather than
what was offered. Without that switch a farm offered a release and a spray, which
chose the spray, would keep the credit for having been offered the release.

**It is not a certification.** Not USDA organic, not Protected Harvest, not LIVE,
not IPM Institute. No standards body has blessed these weights and none was
asked. What it is good for is a season-over-season number on one farm that moves
when the programme actually changes — which is more evidence than "we practise
IPM" has ever had.

### 3. The value block lifecycle

`Field` already carries a crop, a variety and a planting year. Those are the
block's **current** state. `Planting Season` answers what a single set of columns
structurally cannot: *what was out there in 2023, and what did that year cost,
and what did it return.*

**Perennial and annual are genuinely different shapes**, and an Annual whose
plant year and season year differ is refused. An annual *is* its planting: it
goes in, produces, comes out. Left as written, several years of establishment
cost land against one year's revenue and the block reads as ruinous in one season
and free in the others.

**Status is the lifecycle, not the health**, and it is deliberately **not**
derived from the dates. `productive_from` and `productive_through` are estimates
made at planting; the transition is a judgement somebody makes standing in the
block. A fourth-leaf planting not yet carrying a commercial crop is still
Establishing whatever the plan said in 2021, and a farm that had its status
flipped underneath it by a date typed four years ago would stop trusting the
column.

## Two counts kept apart, and one named overlap

Cost reaches a block two ways, and **adding them is wrong**:

```
  ledger      (GL Entry → cost centre)        authoritative, most costs are here
  attributed  (Block Cost Entry)              the three things the ledger cannot hold:
                                                · a shared cost split across blocks
                                                · a cost with no ledger entry at all
                                                · the capitalisation decision, per cost
```

`get_block_cost_summary` reports them separately and sorts the attribution rows
into three buckets. Rows swept from the ledger are excluded, because the ledger
is counted directly. Rows carrying a journal- or GL-entry reference are
*probably* already inside it — and those are excluded **and listed**:

> 3 attribution row(s) totalling 900.00 carry a journal entry or GL entry
> reference, so they are probably already inside the ledger figure. They are
> EXCLUDED from the total and listed here instead …

Including them inflates the block's costs. Dropping them silently understates.
Naming them lets a farm settle it in a minute from the voucher numbers.

And `get_block_revenue_summary` is an **attribution** of revenue the ledger
already recognised, never a recognition of it. The ledger records that a
settlement paid $84,000; nothing in it records that four blocks grew the fruit.
The two answer *what did the business earn* and *which ground earned it*.

## One thing deliberately not done

`crop`, `variety` and `rootstock` are `Data`, not links to the `Crop` register
that landed in v0.82.0. Three reasons, and the third decided it:

1. `Field.crop` has been free text since it shipped, with the stated rationale
   that a block of table grapes is not a schema change.
2. A new Link beside that old Data column gives a site two answers to "what grows
   here" and no rule for which wins.
3. A Link makes `bench migrate` **order** load-bearing between two releases
   landing into one tree. If v0.88.0's doctypes loaded before v0.82.0's `Crop`,
   migrate would die on a link validation error before it reached them.

The upgrade path, when somebody wants it, is a patch that seeds `Crop` rows from
the distinct strings already on the site — folding case, so that "Gala" and
"gala" do not become two rows — and only then changes the column. That is a
release of its own.
