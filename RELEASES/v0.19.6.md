# v0.19.6 — the window standard

**Released 2026-08-03. `services/windowed_reports.py`, the `Financial KPI
History` cache, an overnight sweep, a rolling dashboard chart, three new tools
and one retrofit.**

v0.19.5 produced a defensible number for a period. This release makes the number
readable without knowing what season it is, and does it for every financial
report in the app rather than for one.

Suite: 3,895 → **3,980 passing**. Tool surface: 235 → **238** (106 read, 132
mutating).

---

## 1. The line this release draws

> A single agricultural period compared with another single agricultural period
> says the operation collapsed in January and recovered in September — every
> year, on every farm, whether or not anything happened.

Everything here follows from that sentence.

Agricultural revenue is aggressively seasonal, so a headline figure over one
quarter is not merely imprecise. It is confidently wrong in a direction that
changes with the month somebody asked. Q3 is harvest and Q1 is pruning; the line
falls off a cliff every January and climbs back every September on every
operation in every year. A reader who does not already know that reads a crisis.
A reader who does knows the chart tells them nothing.

A **trailing twelve months** fixes it by construction, not by convention. The
window is always one complete turn of the operation's own calendar, so pruning,
thinning, harvest and the winter are each inside it exactly once no matter when
it is read. Consecutive points differ only by the month that entered and the
month that left, which means the figure **moves when the business moves** — the
only property that makes a trend worth drawing.

That is why TTM is the standard lens for public-company reporting and for lender
covenants. It fits a farm better than it fits the businesses it was invented for.

---

## 2. It is a standard, not a feature on one metric

The thing that makes this a release rather than a patch to
`get_sustainable_cf_per_acre` is that **every** financial report takes the shape.
`services/windowed_reports.py` turns any point-in-time computation into a
windowed one, and registering a computer is the whole of putting a figure under
the standard:

```python
windows.register(
    "revenue", revenue,
    kpi_key="revenue", label="Revenue", value_key="total",
    sum_keys=("total",), list_keys=("by_account",),
    bucket_additive=True, unit="currency",
)
```

The boundaries, the history, the cache, the statistics, the warnings and the MCP
tool all follow. It is reachable through `get_windowed_report` immediately,
without another tool, another switch and another catalogue section — a framework
whose every KPI costs a tool is a framework with six KPIs in it.

Three ship, and they are deliberately not three of a kind:
`sustainable_cf_per_acre` (a ratio, non-additive), `ocf` (a flow, and still
non-additive — see §5), and `revenue` (additive). One of each is the only honest
way to claim a mechanism generalizes.

## 3. Three blocks, and each corrects the other two

```python
{
  "point_in_time": { "value": 910.0, "period_start": "2026-07-01", ... },
  "window":        { "value": 921.0, "period_start": "2025-08-01",
                     "period_end": "2026-07-31", "components": {...} },
  "historical_averages": {
     "prior_ttm_mean": 861.0, "prior_ttm_stddev": 15.2,
     "current_vs_mean_pct_delta": 6.9,
     "current_vs_prior_year_pct_delta": 4.2, ... },
}
```

`point_in_time` answers *where were we in the period just finished*. `window`
(also `ttm` when the type is TTM) answers *how is the business running over its
natural cycle*. `historical_averages` answers *is that good or bad for THIS
operation* — the question the first two cannot touch and the one somebody setting
a budget is actually asking. A TTM figure of 921 means one thing above a
five-year mean of 861 and the opposite below one of 990.

Snapshot alone flatters harvest. TTM alone hides an emerging trend inside eleven
months of history. A historical average without the current figure describes a
farm that no longer exists. All three, always.

## 4. The boundary rule, which is one rule and not five

```
period_end   = last completed `computation_step` boundary <= as_of
period_start = add_months(period_end, -window_months) + 1 day
```

Read on 2026-08-03: **Monthly** ends 2026-07-31 and starts 2025-08-01;
**Quarterly** ends 2026-06-30 and starts 2025-07-01; **Daily** ends 2026-08-02
and starts 2025-08-03.

**The part-finished period is excluded and that is the whole point.** Three days
of August against twelve months of everything else is a figure that falls every
first of the month and recovers by the thirty-first, and an operator reading it
on the fourth will believe the fall. A window whose last period is complete can
be compared with the one before it; a window with a stub on the end cannot be
compared with anything, including itself an hour later.

`period_start` is the day **after** the same calendar date twelve months back,
because `period_end` is inclusive everywhere in this app. 2025-08-01 to
2026-07-31 is twelve months; 2025-07-31 to 2026-07-31 is twelve months and a day.
Same argument as `inclusive_days` in v0.19.5, about the same off-by-one: the
shape of error nobody notices and everybody inherits.

**Quarterly and Yearly steps follow the company's own fiscal year.** A month is a
month on every calendar anybody uses; a quarter is not. An operation whose year
opens on 1 July has a first quarter running July to September, and stepping its
history by calendar quarters would put every year-end close in the middle of a
bucket. The anchor is reported as `fiscal_year_start_month` rather than left to
be inferred, and a non-calendar year produces a warning saying its quarters are
fiscal.

This **diverges from `sustainable_cf_per_acre_by_quarter`**, deliberately, and
the two are not in conflict. That report draws calendar quarters because it is
read beside a lender's own pack, and a "Q1" starting in July is one nobody
outside the operation can line up with anything. It presents four discrete
quarters. This is the history of a rolling window. Different jobs, different
anchors, and both say which they used.

## 5. The window is computed whole. It is not twelve monthly answers added up.

The correctness rule that decides the module's shape, and it has two halves.

**The first is arithmetic.** Sustainable CF/Acre is a *ratio*, and the average of
twelve monthly ratios is not the ratio of the twelve-month totals. A month with
two productive acres and a month with two hundred contribute equally to the first
and not at all equally to the second, and only the second is the number anybody
means.

**The second is subtler and is why bucket-summing is not a shortcut for sums
either.** `kpi.approved_in_period` counts an adjustment whose period falls
*inside* the window — deliberately, so a quarterly insurance recovery is not
counted in a quarter and again in the year containing it. A Q1 adjustment falls
inside **no monthly bucket at all**, so a TTM figure assembled from twelve
monthly buckets would silently drop it. The loss would be invisible: the number
would simply be lower, with nothing anywhere saying why.

So a computer declares whether it is `bucket_additive`. Revenue is — a sum over
GL rows with no containment rule in it, where summing buckets agrees with the
direct computation and hands back the per-month trail for free. Sustainable
CF/Acre and normalized OCF are not, and are computed once over the whole window.
`aggregate_components` implements the merge for the ones that can use it and is
not reached for the ones that cannot.

The merge itself is three different kinds of thing: **sums** add, **weighted
denominators** re-weight over the longer window (a block productive for six of
twelve months contributed a full month's weight to each of six buckets and
contributes half its acreage to the year — summing twelve monthly time-weighted
acreages would give twelve farms), and **lists** concatenate and de-duplicate by
docname.

## 6. Partial history is a warning, never a quietly smaller number

A site with four months of ledger has no trailing twelve months. The honest
answer is four months of data labelled as four months of data. Both wrong answers
are available and both get quoted: annualizing it, which invents eight months of
a season that has not happened, or returning it unlabelled as a TTM figure, which
is the same invention with the evidence removed.

So the window is computed over whatever is there and the payload says:

> only 4 month(s) of ledger history available (the first posting for Highland LLC
> is 2026-04-10); the 12-month window is PARTIAL. The figure covers what is there
> and is not annualized — annualizing it would invent 8 month(s) of a season that
> has not happened.

Every statistic from a short series reports `prior_ttm_count`, and anything that
cannot be computed is **null rather than zero**. A standard deviation of zero
means a perfectly steady business; a standard deviation of null means one
snapshot. Reporting the first for the second is how a volatility figure becomes a
covenant nobody can hold to. Same discipline v0.19.5 applies to a zero
denominator.

## 7. `Financial KPI History`, and why a live query is bounded

A five-year Monthly history is sixty TTM windows, each a full computation over
twelve months of GL. Recomputing that per query would make the tool unusable at
exactly the moment somebody is using it.

`Financial KPI History` holds one row per `(kpi_key, company, computation_step,
window_type, window_months, as_of)` — with the **components dict** as well as the
figure. That is not an optimisation, it is the same rule the KPI is built on: a
cached number with no ingredients is one an auditor cannot test, and the
historical figures are exactly the ones nobody can recompute from memory.

**It is a cache and nothing in it is the only copy of anything.** Every row is
derivable by rerunning the computer that wrote it, which is what makes deletion a
safe invalidation strategy and what puts it on `install.py`'s *regenerated* list
rather than its *precious* one.

- **The overnight sweep**, `0 2 * * *`, is the sixth scheduled job in this app
  and the first that does arbitrary arithmetic over a site's whole ledger. It is
  **one job that iterates** every registered report and every company — a
  scheduler with a cron per KPI is one nobody can read, and the Financial KPI
  Framework is going to add KPIs as data. `daily` would be tidier and is wrong:
  Frappe's `daily` fires on the day's first tick, which on a farm bench is during
  the morning. It is the only scheduled job here with a kill switch of its own,
  because it is the only one whose cost scales with the size of somebody's books.
- **A live query computes at most 24 missing snapshots** and then stops,
  returning a shorter series with a warning naming the tool that fills the rest.
  A read that runs for four minutes is a read somebody kills and then distrusts;
  a short series that says it is short is one they can act on.
- **Invalidation is active on approval and passive on read.**
  `approve_normalization_adjustment` now **deletes** every cached snapshot whose
  window overlaps the adjustment's period, and the next read or the next sweep
  rebuilds them. Deleted rather than flagged, because a cached row carries the
  components list as well as the figure, and a stale components list is worse
  than a missing one — it is a set of ingredients that does not produce the
  number printed above it. The approval's result says how many went and names
  `recompute_kpi_history` for when the pack goes out this afternoon.

## 8. The chart

`Sustainable CF/Acre TTM Monthly` is the new default view: twenty-four rolling
points, each a full twelve months, with a **dashed reference rule** at the
prior-window mean.

Twenty-four is chosen rather than inherited. Twelve would show a rolling figure
whose first and last points share no month at all; sixty is a five-year chart at
monthly resolution nobody can see recent movement in. Two years is where a
deferred-maintenance drift becomes visible while the current season is still
legible.

The mean is a frappe-charts `yMarker` rather than a second dataset, and that is
the reason the chart supplies its own config (`use_report_chart: 1`, the one
mechanical difference from the quarterly chart). A second solid line invites the
reader to compare its *shape* with the first, which is meaningless because it has
none. A `yMarker` is a dashed labelled horizontal rule — what a reference level
is, and how every chart that has ever drawn one draws it.

**The components are columns, not a tooltip**, and that is a choice rather than
an oversight. The interesting question about a month where the figure fell is
always which of the three moved — normalized OCF, maintenance capex, or the acres
— and the ratio alone cannot answer it. Putting four figures in a frappe-charts
tooltip means either four more datasets on a chart about one number, or a custom
formatter this app would then own across every Frappe version it supports. The
columns are where a reader actually inspects a figure: they sort, they export,
and they are already how the quarterly report presents the same ingredients.

**The quarterly chart stays and is not renamed.** It is demoted to a secondary
view in the documentation and in the `why` text — a lender's pack is laid out in
their quarters and lining up with it is worth having, as long as nobody reads the
January cliff as an event. It is not *renamed* because a Dashboard Chart's
docname is what a Dashboard, a workspace and anybody's saved link point at, and
renaming the record would silently empty the dashboards of every site that
installed v0.19.5.

## 9. The retrofit, and why the old call still answers

`get_sustainable_cf_per_acre` now defaults to TTM. A caller that passes
`period_start` and `period_end` — the v0.19.5 signature — gets v0.19.5's payload
back, **exactly**, with a deprecation sentence at the head of
`computation_warnings`.

That compatibility is not politeness. This figure is quoted in lender packs,
saved in scripts and pasted into spreadsheets, and a release that changed what an
unchanged call returned would silently alter a number somebody had already sent
to a bank. One of the two arguments without the other is refused rather than
guessed at. The v0.19.5 end-to-end test passes unmodified.

The KPI's *arithmetic* did not change and must not appear to have: the same
period through the new machinery gives the old answer, and there is a test that
says so.

  mean.

**Tools** (+3, and one retrofit)

| Tool | What it does | What it cannot do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `get_windowed_report` | Any registered report over a window, TTM by default, with `point_in_time`, `window` and `historical_averages`. | Touch your ledger. It warms `Financial KPI History` and writes nothing else — no Account, no GL Entry, no Asset, no Field, no adjustment. There is a test that asserts exactly that. |
| `list_financial_kpi_history` | The cache as a plain series, for drawing or exporting a line. | Hide a gap. A missing window is reported as missing, because plotting it as a continuous line draws a trend that did not happen. |

  `allow_recompute_kpi_history` (off), `enable_kpi_history_sweep` (on).

**Scheduler**

  `erpnext_mcp.services.windowed_reports.recompute_kpi_history_incremental`. The

**Documentation**

## 11. Judgement calls, stated

- **A read tool writes the cache.** `get_windowed_report` is annotated read-only
  and persists snapshots it had to compute. The alternative — never warming the
  cache from a read — leaves a cold cache cold until 2 AM and makes the first
  report of the day take minutes. The claim `readOnlyHint` makes is about the
  domain, and it holds: a test asserts that a windowed read changes no table
  except `Financial KPI History` and the audit log.
- **The `ttm` key is present only for TTM windows.** The standard's documented
  shape uses `ttm`, and that holds for the default. A YTD block under a key
  called `ttm` is the kind of thing somebody quotes, so non-TTM windows carry
  `window` alone.
- **Prior-year comparison walks the series, not the calendar.** The comparator is
  the entry one full lap back (12 entries on a Monthly step), not the date twelve
  months before `as_of` — those differ whenever twelve months back lands inside a
  bucket, and the reader means "the same point last year".
- **The series stops at the start of the ledger** rather than emitting rows
  saying "no data". Those rows are a slower way of saying nothing and would drag
  every average toward zero if anybody forgot to filter them.
- **Invalidation deletes rather than flags.** Argued in §7.
- **The quarterly chart is not renamed.** Argued in §8.
