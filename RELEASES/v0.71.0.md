# v0.71.0 — the bank's version of the same week

Ten tools, closing Sprint 6 of the Gap Closure Plan and the plan itself: the
bridge from everything this app has been collecting since Sprint 2 to the bank's
own record of the same money.

---

## The gap, restated

Sprints 2 to 5 built the paper.

| The paper | The register | Since |
|---|---|---|
| a slip from the fuel pump | Expense Receipt | v0.31.0 |
| a bill from a supplier | Purchase Invoice | v0.68.0 |
| a load off a truck | Scale Ticket | v0.67.0 |
| the packer's statement | Settlement Statement | v0.67.0 |
| a cheque received | Payment Entry | v0.70.0 |
| a pay period | Farm Payroll Entry | v0.45.0 |

Every one of those is a **claim** that money moved. Not one of them is evidence
that it did. The evidence is the bank statement, and until this release nothing
in the app compared the two — a farm could have a season of immaculate receipts
and no way to answer *which of these actually cleared*, or the reverse and far
worse: a statement that ties out perfectly with a third of its withdrawals
having no paper behind them at all.

Everything new lives in `erpnext_mcp/tools/banking_bridge.py`.

---

## Three questions, deliberately not one feature

They get confused constantly and they have different answers.

| | Question | Answered by | Closed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Allocation** | Is this transaction settled against a Payment Entry or a Journal Entry, so the ledger balance is the statement balance? | ERPNext itself | `reconcile_bank_transaction` (v0.1.0) |
| **Evidence** | Is there a *receipt* behind this line? | Sprint 6 | `match_receipt_to_bank_transaction` |
| **Categorisation** | What *kind* of expense was it? | Sprint 6 | `apply_categorization_rules` |

`get_bank_reconciliation_status` answers all three side by side and **never adds
them together**. A single "reconciled: 84%" would be wrong in whichever of the
three senses the reader had in mind, and the case that matters most — allocated,
categorised, and with nothing on file to show an auditor — is the one a merged
number hides best.

This release does **not** rebuild Bank Bridge, the separate app that handles
Plaid connections and statement parsing. Bank Bridge puts Bank Transactions on
the site; these ten tools are what the farm's own paper has to say about them.

## Matching is proposed. It is never committed in bulk

`auto_match_receipts` is a **read tool**, and that is the most load-bearing
decision in the sprint.

It scores every unmatched receipt against every unmatched withdrawal, ranks the
pairs, and hands back the exact `match_receipt_to_bank_transaction` call that
would commit each one. Then it stops.

The reason is not timidity about a hard problem. A wrong receipt-to-bank link is
**invisible afterwards**. Both documents exist. Both amounts are right. The only
thing that is wrong is which slip is filed against which withdrawal, and there is
nothing to see in a review — no imbalance, no orphan, no error. A batch matcher
that committed its own proposals would produce a season of quietly mis-filed
evidence that surfaces, if ever, in an audit.

So a person accepts each one, and `bank_match_method` records whether a human
named both documents (`Manual`) or accepted a machine's proposal (`Proposed`).
An auditor can ask how much of a season's reconciliation a machine suggested,
and get a number.

**Confidence is amount 0.5, date 0.3, merchant 0.2, capped at 0.95.** The amount
carries half of it because it is the only signal that is nearly impossible to
coincide by accident at farm transaction volumes. The merchant carries least,
because a bank memo line is a mangled version of a vendor name at best and a
terminal ID at worst — weighting it heavily would produce zero matches for every
card reader that prints `POS PURCHASE 887342`. The weights are tuned so an exact
amount within a day clears the threshold with an unreadable memo, and an exact
amount a week later with no name agreement does not.

**The direction is the one thing a person cannot overrule.** An amount two cents
out or a date eight days late is a judgement, and a human naming both documents
outranks an algorithm: the link is made, the objections come back in `blockers`,
and the stored confidence is `0.0` so the pair surfaces in any later review.
Money *arriving* is not an expense under any judgement, so a receipt against a
deposit is refused outright.

**Contested proposals are reported, not resolved.** Two slips for $47.83 at the
same station on the same day are a real thing on a farm with two trucks, and
nothing can say which slip is which withdrawal. The higher scorer is proposed;
the other comes back under `contested` with the transaction named. Dropping it
silently would leave a bookkeeper hunting for a charge that is sitting on the
statement in front of them.

## Categorisation is different, and is allowed to write

`apply_categorization_rules` writes without a per-row review. The difference from
matching is not confidence, it is **inspectability**: a rule is deterministic,
its output names the rule that produced it, and an operator who disagrees reads
the rule, fixes it and runs again. A fuzzy amount-and-date match has none of
those properties.

**The rules are data, not code** — the same argument the configurable compliance
framework makes, applied to the chart of accounts. A farm that changes fuel
suppliers adds a row; it does not wait for a release. `Bank Categorization Rule`
is an ordinary doctype: pattern, match field, match type, direction, amount
band, priority, category, account, party.

- **Priority order, first match wins.** `CHEVRON` at 10 beats `FUEL` at 100.
  `list_bank_categorization_rules` returns them in evaluation order, because
  reading the list top to bottom is how somebody works out why a transaction got
  the category it did — and `never_fired` names the rules an earlier one is
  swallowing.
- **A bad regex is refused on save**, not in the middle of a run over a month of
  statement.
- **A category typed by hand is never overwritten** unless `overwrite=true`, in
  which case the result says what was there before.
- **`dry_run=true` does the whole run and writes nothing.** It is the sensible
  first call after seeding, because what it does *not* categorise is the list of
  rules the farm still needs.

`seed_farm_categorization_rules` puts a starting book on a site — Fuel,
Chemicals/Spray, Equipment Parts, Labor Services, Irrigation, Insurance,
Utilities, Feed, Supplies, Professional Services, Owner Draw — with merchant
patterns at priority 10–40 and generic words at 100+. Every seeded rule is
`Withdrawal`-only: a refund from Chevron is not a tank of diesel.

**Nothing in the seed matches a bare transfer or an ATM withdrawal.** `TRANSFER`
is an owner draw on one farm and a sweep between the operating and payroll
accounts on the next, and a seed that guessed would file a year of internal
movements as equity leaving the company. Only the phrases that *say* it —
`OWNER DRAW`, `MEMBER DRAW`, `MEMBER DISTRIBUTION` — are in there.

**Accounts are mapped, never guessed.** Pass `account_map` and those categories
get rules that can tell a Journal Entry where to post; the rest get rules with a
category and no account, and the result names them. There is no keyword search of
the chart of accounts anywhere in this release — a seeder that picked an expense
account by name would put a season of spraying wherever the fuzziest match
landed.

## The cash flow summary refuses to produce one number

A settlement, a sales invoice and a bank deposit can all be **the same money**,
arriving three times in three doctypes at three different moments. A summary that
summed them would triple a season's revenue and look entirely reasonable.

So `get_cash_flow_summary` reports in blocks that are never combined:

- **`cash`** — the bank statement. Deposits, withdrawals, net. The only section
  where the money actually moved, and the only total in the response.
- **`inflows` / `outflows`** — the documents. Payments received and made,
  invoices out and in, settlements, payroll, expense receipts. Each says its
  doctype, its count, its amount and its **basis**, or why it is absent on this
  site.
- **`by_category`** — the one place the two touch, and it **deduplicates**. A
  receipt matched to a withdrawal is one purchase: the withdrawal is dropped and
  the receipt kept, because a receipt carries the category somebody chose off the
  paper while a bank line carries one a pattern guessed from a memo field.
  `deduplicated_transactions` says how many were dropped.

That last number is what the whole sprint is for. Without the matching, a farm's
fuel total is its receipts plus its fuel withdrawals — roughly double, with
nothing visibly wrong.

## Nothing here posts to the ledger

Not one of the ten writes a GL Entry, a Journal Entry, a Payment Entry or an
allocation. Matching a receipt records **evidence**; categorising records what a
transaction **was**. Turning either into a posting is `create_journal_entry`,
which has its own switch and its own review.

The line is deliberate. A tool that could both decide what a statement line means
and post it would be a tool that writes a farm's books from a memo field.

## Schema

**Expense Receipt** gains four columns in this app's own DocType JSON — run
`bench migrate`:

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `bank_transaction` | the statement line this slip is the paper for |
| `bank_match_method` | `Manual` or `Proposed` |
| `bank_match_confidence` | 0–1, evidence for the link rather than a gate on it |
| `bank_matched_on` | when |

**Bank Transaction** gains three Custom Fields — created at install and again
lazily on first use, so a bench that upgraded without running the installer still
works:

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `farm_category` | Fuel, Chemicals/Spray, Irrigation — editable by hand |
| `farm_expense_account` | where the rule says it books. Not a posting |
| `categorization_rule` | which rule decided, read-only |

All three are **`allow_on_submit`**, and that is load-bearing: a bank feed's
transactions are submitted, and a field without the flag cannot be changed on one
without cancelling it — which would detach every allocation already made against
it. A bookkeeper correcting a category must never have to do that.

This is the third place this app adds a Custom Field to a doctype it does not
own. `compliance_fields.py` argues the general case;
`sales.ensure_settlement_link_field` is the narrow precedent. The argument here
is that the alternative — a parallel record with one row per bank transaction —
is a shadow of a thing that already exists, and shadow records drift.

**Bank Categorization Rule** is new, named from a `BCR-.#####` series with
`(company, rule_name)` unique. The docname is a series rather than the rule name
because two farms on one site both have a rule called "Fuel — Chevron", and
naming the document after the rule would make the second one a duplicate of the
first.

## The ten

**Read (on by default)**

| Tool | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `auto_match_receipts` | which slips look like which withdrawals — proposals only |
| `get_bank_reconciliation_status` | the three questions, side by side |
| `list_unmatched_receipts` | the evidence worklist, oldest first |
| `list_unmatched_bank_transactions` | statement lines with nothing behind them, and which kind of nothing |
| `list_bank_categorization_rules` | the book, in evaluation order |
| `get_cash_flow_summary` | cash apart from documents, categories deduplicated |

**Mutating (off by default)**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `match_receipt_to_bank_transaction` | links one slip to one line, or ranks candidates and writes nothing |
| `create_bank_categorization_rule` | one rule; the account is vetted or absent, never guessed |
| `apply_categorization_rules` | runs the book; posts nothing |
| `seed_farm_categorization_rules` | the starting book, idempotent |

`test_auto_match_writes_nothing_at_all` — every other property of this module is
