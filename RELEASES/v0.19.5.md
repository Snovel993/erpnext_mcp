# v0.19.5 — what the year actually earned per acre

**Released 2026-08-03. `Normalization Adjustment`, the Asset capex split, the
Field productive dates, a direct-method cash flow service, a quarterly dashboard
chart and six new tools.**

Every release from v0.19.0 onward answered somebody holding a citation — WPS,
OAR 437-004-1131, FSMA Subpart C, the Produce Safety Rule. This one answers an
owner, a lender and a buyer, who ask a different question about the same season:
what did the ground actually earn?

$$\text{Sustainable CF/Acre} = \frac{\text{Normalized OCF} - \text{Maintenance Capex}}{\text{Productive Acres}}$$

Suite: 3,835 → **3,895 passing**. Tool surface: 229 → **235** (104 read, 131
mutating).

---

## 1. The line this release draws

> A normalized figure nobody can inspect is indistinguishable from an arranged one.

Everything else here follows from that sentence.

Headline operating cash flow lies in two directions at once. It is **flattered**
by money that came in and will not come in again — an insurance recovery, a
settlement, a gain on a tractor sale that landed in the operating section — and
it is flattered **again** by maintenance that was not done. A farm running its
irrigation to failure to make a year look good is destroying the thing the year
was earned with, and the headline number goes *up* while it happens.

Both corrections are judgements, and a judgement is only worth as much as the
argument behind it. So `get_sustainable_cf_per_acre` returns each approved
adjustment with its justification and the name that signed it, each
maintenance-capex asset with its purchase date and portion, and each productive
block with the days it was in service — and the figure is the **last** key in the
payload rather than the only one.

That is the same instinct the compliance side of this app runs on. A spray record
whose REI is one click from the crew's schedule survives an inspection; one whose
evidence is in a binder somewhere does not. A per-acre figure is read by people
whose job is to find the soft spot in it, and it needs the same property.

## 2. `Normalization Adjustment`

One add-back or subtraction on operating cash flow, for one company and one
period, with the sentence that says why it will not recur and the signature of
whoever accepted that sentence.

**The justification has a forty-character floor**, and it is not a quality bar —
no character count is one. It is a floor under *"one-time"* and *"per Tim"*,
which are what gets typed when a field is merely required and both of which an
auditor reads as an admission that nobody thought about it. What the sentence has
to answer is the question every buyer asks: **why will this not happen again?** A
hailstorm in a region that hails every third year is not non-recurring, and this
is where that gets settled before the number reaches a lender.

**The amount is always positive; the sign lives in `direction`.** A negative
amount beside a `Subtract from OCF` is a double negative, and a double negative
is how an adjustment ends up moving the number the wrong way in a pack somebody
is borrowing against.

**Only `Approved` counts.** Draft and Pending Approval are somebody's proposal —
including a model's, which is the ordinary case. Rejected is the record that the
proposal was looked at and refused, kept for exactly the reason a rejected
insurance claim is kept. Superseded is what a correction leaves behind.

**Not submittable, deliberately.** A submittable doctype has one irreversible
transition, and this workflow has two terminal states that are not the same thing
— Approved and Rejected — plus a third that a correction produces years later.
Modelling that as `docstatus` would make a rejection either indistinguishable
from an approval or impossible to record at all.

**One approved adjustment per company, period and category.** Enforced in the
controller rather than by a unique index, because the constraint is on the tuple
*among approved rows*: three rejected proposals and one approved one for the same
quarter's insurance is the register working, and two approved ones are two
answers to one question. A correction **supersedes** rather than duplicates,
which leaves the trail of what was believed before — the half of a restatement
anybody actually wants.

## 3. AI proposes, human approves

`create_normalization_adjustment` produces a **Draft** and there is no argument
that makes it produce anything else. `approve_normalization_adjustment` is a
separate tool, behind a separate switch, and it takes a signature.

The split is the whole compliance posture of the release, and it is the same one
`record_training` makes between filing a record and signing the supervisor's
review. Finding a non-recurring item scattered through a ledger nobody reads line
by line is genuinely valuable and is something a model is good at — an insurance
recovery three months after the hailstorm, a settlement booked to a
miscellaneous income account, a working-capital swing that is really a customer
paying two days either side of a quarter end. Deciding that it will not recur is
a judgement with a lender on the other end of it.

`approved_on` is **written at approval, never taken as input.** An approval date
somebody can set is an approval date somebody can set to before the quarter
closed, which is exactly the thing the timestamp exists to prove.

`approver_employee` defaults to the Employee linked to the acting user and is
empty where the app runs as a service principal — the ordinary configuration, and
not a failure. The signature is the identity that matters.

## 4. Maintenance capex is actual spend

The common shortcut is to model maintenance capex as some fraction of revenue. It
destroys the only interesting thing the metric measures. An operation that spent
nothing on replacement this year did not have a cheap year: it borrowed the year
from the orchard, and **under-investment is the signal** rather than noise to be
smoothed. A formula substituting 3 % of revenue reports a well-maintained farm
every time, including in the years it matters.

Four Custom Fields land on ERPNext's `Asset` — `capex_type`,
`maintenance_portion`, `growth_portion`, `capex_justification` — and they are the
fifth doctype this app grafts a column onto. `compliance_fields.py` carries the
argument; `docs/compliance_fields.md` prints it.

**Why not an `Asset Capex Profile` beside the Asset.** v0.7.0 put the cost split
in an `Asset Cost Profile` precisely so this app would touch nobody else's
schema, and the same move fails here. The maintenance/growth call is made
**once**, by the person raising the purchase, at the moment they know why they
are buying the thing — the old pump failed, or the new block needs a pump it
never had. A profile row written afterwards by somebody reconciling the quarter
is a person reconstructing an intention from an invoice, and they will get it
wrong in the direction that makes the quarter look better.

**`capex_type` is not `reqd`.** Frappe enforces `reqd` on save rather than
retroactively, so marking it required would leave every existing Asset readable
and unsaveable — a farm with two hundred assets would find that editing a
location on a tractor bought in 2019 now demands a classification nobody present
can make. The gate is in `create_asset` instead, where the person raising the
purchase is standing, and it does not bite until the column exists.

**Mixed must add up to the invoice, within a cent.** A split that does not is not
a split, it is two numbers: some of the money is classified as neither and drops
out of every figure computed from it. A rounded cent is not a disagreement; a
dollar is, because a dollar is what a transposed figure looks like.

**Growth and Mixed need a justification, Maintenance does not.** The asymmetry is
deliberate: classifying spend as growth takes it out of the maintenance figure
and *raises* sustainable cash flow, which is the one direction a misclassification
flatters the operation.

`backfill_asset_capex_type` classifies history in bulk, **dry-run by default**,
on one sentence of heuristic — everything bought before the operation started
tracking is generally maintenance, because it is the existing productive plant
carrying on. It only fills nulls, so a second run finds nothing to do. It is a
starting position rather than an answer, and the result says so in those words:
the new block planted in year six was growth and will read as maintenance until
somebody fixes it, which *understates* the KPI, where a register of nulls
*overstates* it.

## 5. The denominator is what is productive

`Field` gains three declared fields — `productive_from_date`,
`productive_through_date`, `pre_yield_end_date`. Declared rather than Custom,
because `Field` is this app's own doctype and `compliance_fields.py` refuses to
paper over a missing declared field with a Custom one.

**Nothing already carried these dates.** v0.19.5 checked: there is no
`PlantingSeason` junction in this app or reachable from it, and no field on
`Field`, `Parcel` or `Irrigation Zone` answering the question at a per-period
grain. `planting_year` is the closest thing and is not the same fact — a block
planted in 2019 may have come into bearing in 2022 or 2023 depending on variety
and rootstock, and inferring one from the other is exactly the invention this
release exists to stop.

Fallow ground has acreage, a cost centre and a water right and earns nothing. A
perennial in its pre-yield years is capital under construction wearing the
costume of an orchard. Both are out of the denominator and **counted separately
in the output**, because pre-yield acres are next year's denominator and a reader
who cannot see them coming cannot read the trend.

Every block is weighted by the fraction of the period it was actually productive,
**inclusive at both ends** — `period_end` is an inclusive date everywhere else in
this app, so a Q1 window is ninety days, and a denominator counting one fewer day
than the numerator's period would understate every partial year by a little, in
the same direction, for ever.

**A block with no `productive_from_date` is excluded and named.** This is the
most consequential refusal in the release and the easiest one to get wrong the
kind way. Assuming an undated block is productive puts acres in the denominator
that may be a three-year-old planting, which makes the KPI *lower* and looks
conservative — and quietly converts a data gap into a number somebody acts on.
Saying "eleven blocks have no productive-from date" is the answer; inventing
their dates is not.

## 6. Raw OCF, by the direct method

`services/sustainable_cf_per_acre.py` computes the operating section from
`GL Entry` rather than reading ERPNext's built-in Cash Flow report. Two reasons,
and the second decided it:

- The report needs the Frappe report engine, a filter dict shaped to whichever
  version the site is running, and a return shape that has changed across the
  versions this app supports. `run_report` exists for a caller who wants exactly
  that; a KPI is not that caller.
- **A report's output cannot be traced back to rows.** The whole argument of this
  release is that the figure has to be inspectable.

For each posted voucher that touched a cash or bank account in the period, the
net cash movement is the sum of its cash rows, and the activity it belongs to is
read off its **other** rows: Income and Expense counterparts are operating, fixed
assets are investing, equity and non-trade liabilities are financing. A voucher
whose other side spans more than one section is **split proportionally** rather
than assigned to whichever line is biggest, because a journal entry that settles
a supplier invoice and a loan instalment out of one payment is genuinely both.

The two rows anybody will argue about are written out in
`ACCOUNT_TYPE_ACTIVITY` with the reason: a trade payable is **operating** (paying
a supplier is the operation paying for what it consumed) and a note payable is
**financing** (the money came from a lender, not from selling fruit). ERPNext
types both as `Liability`, and only `account_type` tells them apart.

Transfers between two of the operation's own cash accounts fall out at zero
without a special case: the two cash rows net inside the voucher. A voucher with
cash rows and nothing else is **reported as unclassified** rather than swept into
operating — which is the direction that would flatter the figure.

`GL Entry` exists only for submitted vouchers, so a draft journal entry cannot
move this number. That is the same property `generate_quarterly_investment_report`
relies on and for the same reason.

## 7. The six tools

| Tool | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `create_normalization_adjustment` | **mutating, off** | Proposes one. Creates a Draft, always. |
| `approve_normalization_adjustment` | **mutating, off** | The human half. Signature required, timestamp written. |
| `reject_normalization_adjustment` | **mutating, off** | Refuses one on the record, with the reason kept. |
| `backfill_asset_capex_type` | **mutating, off** | Classifies history in bulk. Dry by default, idempotent. |
| `list_normalization_adjustments` | read, on | The register, and which rows actually count. |
| `get_sustainable_cf_per_acre` | read, on | The KPI, with every ingredient itemized. |

**The role gate is not the HR one.** `employee.require_hr_role` gates the
personnel register on HR Manager, HR User and Farm Manager, and none of those is
who signs off a normalization. This gate is **Accounts Manager, Farm Manager,
System Manager**: the accountant who has to defend the add-back, the manager who
knows whether the pump was a replacement, and the operator. An HR User who can
file a training record has no business moving the number a lender reads, and the
two lists are separate so that stays true when either is widened.

Company scope is the shared one, and every tool checks it: a normalization on the
wrong entity flatters one set of books with another entity's insurance claim.

## 8. The chart

`Sustainable CF/Acre by Quarter` — a core Frappe **Dashboard Chart** whose source
is a standard **Script Report** in this app's own module, installed idempotently
on every migrate by `dashboard.install_kpi_charts()`.

A Script Report and not a Query Report, because there is no SQL that produces
this: the figure needs the approved adjustments summed with their directions
applied, the maintenance portion of each classified asset, and a denominator
weighted by days in service. A query that tried would be a reimplementation of
the service in SQL, and the two would disagree within a release.

**The components travel with the figure, in columns rather than as a tooltip.**
Normalized OCF, maintenance capex and time-weighted acres are each their own
column, because the interesting question about a quarter where the number fell is
always *which of the three moved* — and a chart of the ratio alone cannot answer
it. The unclassified-asset count rides along for the same reason: a quarter whose
capex was never classified reports a flatteringly high figure, and the column
says so on the face of the report.

**Not Frappe Insights**, and that is a deliberate deferral rather than an
oversight. Insights would draw this more handsomely and is a separate app with
its own install, permissions model and upgrade path; a KPI that only renders on
sites which happen to have it is a KPI half the operation cannot see. An Insights
view over the same service is a later release and loses nothing by waiting.

## 9. Judgement calls, stated

- **Raw OCF sourcing.** Direct method from GL Entry, not the Cash Flow report.
  Traceability decided it. Documented in `raw_ocf.computation_note` on every
  result rather than only here.
- **Float, not `Decimal`.** The spec sketched `Decimal`; every monetary figure in
  this app is a float rounded at the boundary, and introducing a second
  convention in one module would mean a conversion at every seam with the other
  two hundred and thirty-four tools. Rounded to two places at each boundary, and
  the time-weighted acreage to four.
- **Field, not PlantingSeason.** There is no PlantingSeason junction in this app.
  The dates are declared fields on `Field`, which is this app's own doctype.
- **Mixed capex tolerance: one cent.** A rounded cent is not a disagreement and a
  dollar is.
- **Adjustments are read INSIDE the window, not overlapping it.** An adjustment
  spanning a quarter is about that quarter; letting it overlap would count one
  insurance recovery in two quarters and in the year, and the year would then
  disagree with the sum of its quarters by the amount of the double count.
- **`backfill_asset_capex_type` writes with `db_set`.** Most of the register it
  exists to classify is submitted, and a submitted Asset cannot be re-saved
  through the ordinary path. Classifying a purchase is metadata about a decision
  already made — no amount, no account, no schedule, no docstatus changes — so
  writing the columns directly is proportionate, and going through `save` would
  refuse the rows that most need it.
- **The KPI is `None`, not `0`, at zero acres.** A division nobody performed is
  not an answer, and a zero would be read as one.
- **Calendar quarters in the report.** The chart is read beside a lender's own
  quarters, and a fiscal year starting in July would otherwise produce a "Q1"
  nobody outside the operation can line up with anything.

## 10. What is not here

- **No `update_normalization_adjustment`.** An adjustment is edited in the Desk
  while it is a draft and superseded once it is approved. A tool that could
  rewrite an approved add-back would undo the point of the signature.
- **No automatic proposal from the ledger.** A sweep that scanned for likely
  non-recurring items and filed drafts would be useful and is a later release;
  what it must never do is skip the approval step, and building the second
  before the first is settled is how that happens.
- **No `Financial KPI Definition` doctype.** Phase 2, parallel to the
  Configurable Compliance Framework: formula, inputs, cadence and thresholds as a
  record, with DSCR, ROIC and EBITDA/acre added as data rather than as releases.
  `docs/kpi_sustainable_cf_per_acre.md` sketches it.

## 11. Upgrading

`bench --site <site> migrate`. It creates the `Normalization Adjustment` doctype,
adds the three productive-date columns to `Field` and the four capex columns to
`Asset`, seeds the six new switches, installs the report and builds the chart.

**Two things to do afterwards**, in this order:

1. `backfill_asset_capex_type(cutoff_purchase_date=<when you started tracking>,
   dry_run=true)` — read what it would do, then run it for real. Until the
   history is classified, every past period's KPI is overstated, because
   unclassified purchases are excluded from maintenance capex entirely.
2. Fill in `productive_from_date` on the blocks. Every field without one is out
   of the denominator and named in `computation_warnings`, which is the worklist.

Nothing existing changes behaviour. The four Asset columns are nullable and the
`create_asset` gate only engages once they exist; every read tool that touched a
Field or an Asset before this release returns the same shape with three or four
more keys on it.
