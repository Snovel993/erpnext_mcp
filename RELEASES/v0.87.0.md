# v0.87.0 — a fruit farm has two volumes

The breakeven calculator. Five tools, four doctypes, three Custom Fields on
Account, eighty-two tests.

*What price do I need to break even?* is the question a grower asks in July with
the crop hanging and a broker on the phone, and it is the question this release
answers.

---

## The one idea

A textbook breakeven has one volume. A fruit farm has two, and everything here
follows from that.

Picking, hauling and field bins are bought for **everything that comes off the
trees**. Cartons, packing labour, freight and commission are bought only for
**what packs out**. When packout falls from 85% to 60%, the second pile falls
with it and the first does not — it simply lands on fewer sellable boxes.

So each cost line carries a `volume_basis`, and the arithmetic is:

```
variable cost per sellable unit  =  vh / p  +  vs
cull credit per sellable unit    =  c · (1 − p) / p
contribution margin per unit     =  P + cull credit − variable cost
```

Every packed box carries `1/p` harvested units of picking with it and brings
`(1−p)/p` culls' worth of juice money along. On this release's own fixture —
60,000 fixed, 40,000 of picking, 30,000 of packing, 10,000 boxes at 80% — the
breakeven price is 16.00 a box. Slide the packout to 60% and it is **19.75**.

A single-pile model says 21.00. A model that forgot to move anything says 16.00.
Both are wrong, both are within shouting distance, and **both look exactly like
an answer** — which is why the split is the design rather than a refinement.

The **cull credit is not decoration**. Thirty percent culls at juice price is not
thirty percent of the crop earning nothing, and a model that treated it that way
would make every light-packout scenario look worse than it is. That is the
direction that leaves fruit on the tree which should have been picked.

## The bug this release nearly shipped

Turning ledger *totals* into model *rates* means dividing by the volume those
totals correspond to. Get that volume wrong — re-derive both rates at whatever
the packout slider currently says — and:

```
vh/p + vs   →   Vh/S + Vs/S   →   (Vh + Vs) / S
```

The harvest/sellable split **cancels straight out of the arithmetic**. Every
number still computes, every figure still looks plausible, and the entire reason
for the module has quietly evaporated.

So the volume the rates are derived at is stored on the analysis, as
`baseline_harvest_units` and `baseline_packout_pct`. Both are set on the first
computation and are **not moved by the slider**. Sliding the packout to 62%
therefore means *"the same money per carton, over fewer cartons"* — which is what
a grower means — and `get_breakeven_sensitivity` at −22.5% packout returns
exactly what `compute_breakeven(packout_pct=62)` stores.

Two tools answering one question with two numbers would be worse than either of
them being slightly wrong. There is a test that asserts they agree to six places.

`rebase_costs` moves the baseline deliberately, once the season's real crop and
real packout are known. It is an argument rather than an automatic behaviour
because re-basing changes every historical comparison the record is part of, and
that is a decision rather than a side effect.

## It says what it guessed

Three classification sources, best evidence first, stored on **every line**:

* **Account** — an operator classified the account itself with
  `breakeven_cost_behavior`, one of three Custom Fields this release installs on
  `Account`. Said once, true for every analysis afterwards.
* **Override** — this analysis was told to treat one account differently, once,
  and nothing was written to the Account.
* **Heuristic** — nobody has said, so the account's name and ERPNext type were
  read and a guess was made.

The heuristic is genuinely useful *and* genuinely a guess, and the design turns
on holding both at once. A first run against a real chart of accounts classifies
most of it correctly and returns a number in a minute rather than an afternoon —
and reports how many lines it guessed at, in the result, in
`computation_warnings`, and again on every read.

**A breakeven resting on forty guessed classifications is a different object from
one resting on none**, and the person about to quote it to a lender is entitled
to know which of the two they are holding.

An override naming an account that is not an expense account of the company is
**refused**, never ignored: a dropped override leaves the account being guessed
at while the caller believes they classified it, and the result would be
indistinguishable from one where it took.

**Income tax is excluded by rule, not by heuristic.** At breakeven there is no
pre-tax income to tax, so a model carrying the tax line would demand the farm
cover a liability it does not have.

## What the reads refuse to do

**No breakeven quantity where the contribution margin is not positive.** There is
no such quantity — every additional box loses money. The arithmetic limit is an
enormous number that reads as a hard target, so `breakeven_units` comes back
`null` and the reason is stated. The breakeven **price** is still reported, and
there it is the number that matters: it says how far the price has to come up
before selling more helps at all.

**No conversion between packages on the market overlay.** A breakeven per 40-lb
box compared against a USDA quotation per 20-lb carton is out by a factor of two
and looks entirely plausible. Both packages are reported next to the spread and
neither is converted, because pack style is a judgement this app has no basis to
make.

**No writing from a read.** `get_breakeven_sensitivity` answers whatever band it
is asked for and stores nothing; `compute_breakeven` stores a standard ±10/±20%
band across all five variables. What is in the register depends on who ran a
computation, not on who was browsing.

**An edited input goes stale rather than recomputing.** Change the price in the
Desk and the record flips to `Stale` with its old results intact and the changed
field named. Recomputing on save would be easy and wrong: a breakeven that
changed underneath the person reading it, carrying the same `computed_on` it had
a moment ago, is worse than one that admits it is out of date.

## Maximal data science, doing actual work

Every intermediate is a column: both variable piles, both rates, the baseline,
the per-unit variable cost, the contribution margin and ratio, the breakeven in
sellable units *and* in fruit on the tree, the breakeven price, the breakeven
packout, the projected result and the margin of safety — plus one cost line per
account and twenty stored scenarios.

That is not tidiness. The interesting quantity is almost never this season's
breakeven on its own; it is how the breakeven moved between seasons and **which
term moved it**, and that comparison exists only if the terms were kept. A cost
line stores the account name as it read at the time, too, so a two-season
comparison whose account labels were silently renamed underneath it is a
comparison somebody can still check.

`breakeven_packout_pct` is the same question read backwards. *"We need 59% out of
this block"* is a target a packhouse can be given; a breakeven revenue is not.

## The market overlay reads a register, not the internet

USDA AMS Market News publishes shipping point prices — what a district was asking
f.o.b. — and every quotation this app sees is kept as a `USDA Price Quote`.

The obvious design is a call to a price API at the moment somebody opens a
report. It fails in the two conditions a farm office is actually in: the internet
is down, or nobody has an API key yet. Both are normal, neither is an outage, and
a breakeven that stopped answering because a third party was unreachable would
teach its user not to rely on it. So the overlay reads **the register**, which
also lets it answer *"has this crop cleared its breakeven in any of the last
three weeks"* — a question one live number never could.

A grower with a broker's bid in hand has a better number than any district
average. `compute_breakeven(market_price=…)` stores it as its own labelled kind
of source, so the overlay reads one register and nobody mistakes a bid for a
published average.

The band matters, not just the midpoint: AMS publishes a narrower *mostly* band
inside the low-to-high range because the extremes include the distressed load and
the one specialty buyer, and the overlay prefers it where there is one.

Fetching is opt-in twice over — a MARS API key in settings, and either
`refresh_usda_prices` with a report slug or the nightly sweep, which **ships
off**. That is the only scheduled job in this app that ships disabled, and the
reason is specific: the weather and regulation feeds work against keyless public
sources, this needs a key, and an always-on entry would log an authentication
failure every night on every site that never wanted a market overlay.

**No report slugs are shipped.** AMS identifies reports by slugs an operator
looks up for the districts they actually ship into; a list invented here would be
right for one region and a nightly 404 everywhere else. An unconfigured sweep
does nothing and says so.

A quotation **belongs to no company**. A shipping point price is a fact about a
market, not about anybody's operation; the records carrying the operation are the
analyses that read it, and every one of those links to Company and is scoped by
Frappe exactly as before.

## What it is not

It is a **perspective on the chart of accounts**, not a second set of books.
Nothing posts, nothing is a journal entry, no number changes what the financial
statements say. What it adds is the one thing a chart of accounts cannot hold:
which accounts stop mattering when the crop gets bigger.

An operation wanting costs elastic per unit already has that — `Standard Cost`
holds a rate per bin, per acre or per hour, and `get_cost_variance_report` is
where a rate meets an actual.

## The switches

Three reads on, two writes off, as every release here ships them.

Both writes are "mutating" because they **store**, not because they post.
`create_breakeven_analysis` deliberately does not compute: the two tools have
separate switches, and a create that ran the computation would hand an operator a
tool they had not enabled.

| Switch | Default |
| --- | --- |
| `allow_get_breakeven_analysis` | on |
| `allow_list_breakeven_analyses` | on |
| `allow_get_breakeven_sensitivity` | on |
| `allow_create_breakeven_analysis` | off |
| `allow_compute_breakeven` | off |
| `enable_usda_price_sweep` | off |

## Upgrading

`bench --site <site> migrate`. Four new doctypes, and three Custom Fields added
to `Account` by the installer — created lazily on first use as well, so a bench
that pulled the code without running the installer still classifies an account
the first time somebody says so. A site that will not take them loses only the
ability to make a classification *stick* between analyses; every breakeven still
computes, every heuristic still runs, overrides still work, and the result says
`account_classification_available: false` rather than pretending otherwise.
