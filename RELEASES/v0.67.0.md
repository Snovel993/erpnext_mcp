# v0.67.0 — the two numbers that are supposed to disagree

Nine tools, two new registers, and one deliberate refusal to make a sum come out.

---

## The gap

A load of fruit leaves the orchard on somebody else's truck, crosses somebody
else's scale, and the grower is handed a slip of thermal paper. Two to four
months later a settlement statement arrives saying how much of that fruit packed
out, what it sold for, and what was taken off the top before the cheque.

Between those two documents, this app had nothing. The settlement was the only
record of the transaction it could see — which is to say the counterparty's own
account of it, unchecked, because the thing that would check it was a slip of
paper in a truck door pocket that had faded to blank by November.

Sprint 2 of the gap-closure plan puts both documents in the ledger. `Scale
Ticket` is the grower's copy of what was delivered. `Settlement Statement` is the
packer's account of what happened to it. And the single most important line of
code in this release is the one that is not there: **nothing reconciles them.**

## The receipt is the financial atom

The design principle, and the reason `classify_receipt` exists.

A foreman photographs a piece of paper. Everything after that is identical —
extract, file, review — and the *only* branch is which piece of paper it was:

| The paper | The register |
|---|---|
| a fuel or parts slip | Expense Receipt (v0.31.0) |
| a scale ticket | **Scale Ticket** |
| a packout settlement | **Settlement Statement** |
| a vendor invoice | Purchase Invoice — a later sprint |

One capture flow, four destinations. `classify_receipt` is that branch published
as a tool, and it is the only read in this release that touches no doctype at
all.

## Why a Scale Ticket is not a Delivery Note

ERPNext has a Delivery Note. It is the wrong shape, and the reason is worth
having in writing because it will be asked again.

A Delivery Note is the **seller's** document about goods leaving on the seller's
terms: priced, against a Sales Order, in Items and UOMs the seller controls. A
scale ticket is a **third party's weight record**. The packer owns the scale. The
packer prints the slip. The variety is whatever the packer's clerk wrote in biro.
And there is no price on it at all — the price arrives months later, on the
settlement.

Forcing that onto a Delivery Note would mean inventing an Item per variety per
grade before a foreman could photograph a slip at a tailgate. **A capture that
requires master data to exist first is a capture that does not happen**, and the
ticket ends up in a glovebox, which is where this whole problem started.

So the ticket is stored as what it is. The connection to priced, Item-shaped
documents is made later by the settlement, once the packer has finally said what
the fruit was worth.

## The arithmetic is shown, not asserted

`net_weight` is `gross_weight - tare_weight`, computed on every save and
read-only in the Desk. It cannot be passed to `create_scale_ticket`.

That is not caution about writes. It is that **the net weight is the exact number
a settlement dispute turns on**, and a net weight somebody typed is a number with
no arithmetic behind it. Where the paper ticket's own printed net disagrees with
the subtraction — and it will, occasionally — that disagreement is the *finding*.
It belongs in `notes` beside the photograph, not silently in the field the grower
will later argue from.

`get_scale_ticket` returns the sum rather than only its answer:

```json
{"weight_check": {"gross_weight": 18400.0, "tare_weight": 6200.0,
                  "net_weight": 12200.0, "weight_uom": "Lb",
                  "computed_as": "18400.0 - 6200.0 = 12200.0"}}
```

A tool that returned only the answer would be asking to be trusted about the
arithmetic the whole document exists to settle.

A tare above the gross is refused rather than netting negative. It is two numbers
off different tickets, or a gross in pounds against a tare in kilos — and a
negative net propagating into a settlement check would make the check say the
packer overpaid.

## The docname carries the company, not the year

`ST-OML-0001`. Every other dated register this app ships names itself by year,
because the question asked of a shift or a heat record is *which season*. The
question asked of a scale ticket is *whose fruit* — two entities delivering to
one packer under one bench is the ordinary case here, and a shared `ST-2026-…`
run would interleave them.

`ticket_number` is the **packer's** number, kept as a separate field, and it is
deliberately **not** unique on this site. Two packers will both have a ticket
4471 sooner or later, and a uniqueness constraint would refuse the second
grower's perfectly real ticket.

## The release's one real idea

**`gross_delivered_weight` on a settlement is what the packer says arrived. The
sum of the matched Scale Tickets is what the grower's own copies say arrived.
Nothing in this release agrees them.**

Three things were possible and two of them destroy the document:

- *Sum the tickets into the settlement's delivered weight.* The packer's figure
  is gone. The comparison that was the entire point of keeping stubs no longer
  exists, and the settlement now agrees with itself by construction.
- *Refuse a settlement whose figure disagrees.* Most real settlements disagree by
  a little — shrink, a load weighed at a second scale, a ticket filed late — and
  a register that refused them would simply not be used.
- *Report both, name the difference, correct neither.*

The third is what shipped:

```json
{"delivery_reconciliation": {"packer_gross_delivered_weight": 48000.0,
                             "matched_ticket_net_weight": 49850.0,
                             "weight_uom": "Lb", "variance": -1850.0,
                             "matched_ticket_count": 4,
                             "tickets_in_other_units_excluded": 0}}
```

−1,850 lb is not an error in this record. It is 1,850 lb of fruit the grower's
own tickets say crossed a scale and the packer's statement did not pay for, and
it is a conversation with the packer. The `note` on that object says so in as
many words, because somebody will eventually try to make it zero.

Tickets in a different weight unit are **counted and excluded**, never converted.
There is no bins-to-kilos conversion this app knows, and a fabricated one would
put a fabricated variance on the answer — which is worse than no answer, because
it looks like one.

The same principle runs through the smaller decisions. The line items are never
reconciled against `packed_weight` (fruit still in storage and fruit repacked
into a later pool live in that gap). `cull_weight` is never derived from
delivered minus packed (juice and shrink live in *that* gap). Deriving either
would manufacture a cull percentage out of a storage lag, and cull percentage is
a number growers renegotiate contracts over.

## What the settlement does compute

Five numbers, and none of them can be passed in:

| Field | From |
|---|---|
| `packout_pct` | packed ÷ delivered |
| `cull_pct` | culled ÷ delivered |
| `total_gross_revenue` | sum of the line items |
| `total_deductions` | sum of the deduction rows |
| `net_proceeds` | gross less deductions |

The two percentages are how one packer is compared with another, and **a
percentage nobody recomputed is a percentage nobody checked**. A statement is
free to state its own; this app states the one it derived from the weights on
the record, computed the same way every time.

`gross_amount` on a line is `packed_weight × price_per_unit` **where the
statement left it blank**, and left alone where the statement gave one — the same
rule, for the same reason, as the line totals on an Expense Receipt. A packer who
applied a promotion or a partial-pool adjustment to one line is telling the
truth, and the multiplication is not.

`price_uom` is its own field rather than an assumption that the price is quoted
per the weight unit. A packer quotes per box and weighs in pounds often enough
that assuming they agree would misprice a line by a factor of forty.

Deductions are **rows**, not one netted-off number. "What did cold storage cost
me last season" is the question a grower asks a year later, and a single net
figure cannot answer it. A negative deduction is refused: a deduction is already
a subtraction, so a negative one would *add* to the proceeds — if the packer
credited something back, that is a line item.

## Matching, and the four things it refuses

Passing `scale_tickets` to `create_settlement_statement` claims them: each is
stamped `Matched` and drops off the `unmatched: true` list. Every check runs
**before** the insert, so every refusal below can honestly say nothing was
created:

1. **A draft ticket.** Its weights can still change after the settlement is
   checked against them, which makes the check meaningless.
2. **A ticket already matched to a different settlement.** Two statements paying
   for one load is the exact overpayment this register exists to surface;
   silently re-pointing the ticket would hide it.
3. **A ticket from another company.** Two entities under one bench is ordinary.
4. **A ticket delivered to another packer.** A packer does not settle another
   packer's deliveries.

Cancelling a settlement releases its tickets back to `Submitted`. A cancelled
settlement has not paid for anything, and a ticket left reading `Matched` would
sit in the register as paid-for for ever.

## Status is computed. Twice.

Neither register has a `status` an operator can type into. Both are derived from
`docstatus` and one other fact on the record:

| Scale Ticket | | Settlement Statement | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Draft` | docstatus 0 | `Draft` | docstatus 0 |
| `Submitted` | docstatus 1, unclaimed | `Submitted` | docstatus 1, no JE |
| `Matched` | docstatus 1, settlement set | `Posted` | docstatus 1, JE set |
| `Cancelled` | docstatus 2 | `Cancelled` | docstatus 2 |

A status column somebody could edit would eventually disagree with the docstatus
beside it, and the question these registers exist to answer — *which delivered
loads has nobody paid for yet* — is a query on exactly that column.

**Nothing in v0.67.0 sets `posted_journal_entry`, so nothing reaches `Posted`.**
Said here rather than left to be discovered: the tool that books settlement
proceeds to the ledger is a later sprint. The column and the state exist now so
that sprint does not have to migrate every statement captured before it.

## Create is a draft; submit is a different permission

Both registers are submittable, and both `create_*` tools leave the document at
docstatus 0.

That is not timidity about writes. **Submitting a scale ticket makes a third
party's weight record immutable**, and an operator who wants a phone able to
*capture* tickets does not necessarily want the same phone able to *freeze* them.
One switch cannot express that. Two can, and there are two.

## `classify_receipt`, and why it is a keyword table

The classifier runs while somebody is standing in front of the thing they
photographed. It is *allowed* to be wrong — the app shows its answer as a
pre-selected tab a person can change. What it is not allowed to be is unarguable.

"Why did it file my ticket as an expense" has to have an answer, and
`matched_signals` is that answer. A keyword table is auditable by reading it,
ships with nothing to host, and gives the same result on a bench with no Python
beyond what Frappe already has.

```json
{"receipt_type": "scale_ticket", "confidence": 0.71,
 "matched_signals": ["scale ticket", "gross wt", "tare wt", "net wt", "bins"],
 "scores": {"scale_ticket": 10, "settlement": 1, "bill": 0, "expense": 0},
 "suggested_tool": "Scale Ticket (create_scale_ticket)"}
```

Three decisions in the scoring are worth stating:

**It never returns 1.0.** The ceiling is 0.95, because a keyword rule is never
certain and a confidence of 1.0 is an instruction to the client to stop asking.

**Confidence is share × saturation.** The winner's share of all matched evidence,
scaled down when there was little evidence to share. One weak keyword out of one
is not 100% certainty; it is one weak keyword.

**Nothing matching is a stated fallback, not a guess.** `expense`, confidence 0,
`default_applied: true`. Expense is where an unrecognised slip does least harm,
because it is the register a person reviews anyway.

Ties break toward the more specific document — settlement, then scale ticket,
then bill, then expense. A settlement quotes weights and so always picks up
scale-ticket words; a scale ticket almost never says "packout". `expense` is last
because it is also the fallback, and a fallback that could win a tie would
swallow the two registers this release exists to fill.

`amount` is echoed back and **never used to classify**. A $9,000 fuel bill and a
$9,000 settlement are the same number, and a rule on it would be a rule on farm
size.

## The expense receipt grows two links

Sprint 1 gave this app the ability to create a `Supplier` and an `Item`. This
release lets a receipt point at them — `supplier` on the header, `item` on each
line. Both optional. Both **additive**.

That last word is the whole design. `merchant` and `description` keep saying
exactly what the paper said. A slip printed `VALLEY CO-OP #14` and a Supplier
record called `Valley Co-operative` are the same vendor, and a capture that
replaced the first with the second would **lose the evidence in the act of
improving the data**. Keeping both is what lets a bookkeeper total a year of fuel
per vendor *and* still show an auditor the string the machine read.

Neither is ever inferred. No fuzzy match from merchant to Supplier, no lookup
from an OCR'd line to an Item. `HYD HOSE 1/2` matches four items in a real
catalogue, and a guess would put a fabricated consumption figure somewhere a
person later reads as a measurement. A link is set when a human says so, or it
stays empty.

`list_expense_receipts` gains a `supplier` filter that finds only receipts
somebody **linked**. The merchant text is not searched: a vendor total built out
of string matches is not a vendor total.

## Four mobile routes, and three deliberate absences

`POST /farmops/api/mobile/` — `classify_receipt`, `create_expense_receipt`,
`create_scale_ticket`, `list_scale_tickets`.

`submit_scale_ticket`, `create_settlement_statement` and
`submit_settlement_statement` have **no route**, each for its own reason:
submitting freezes a third party's weight record and belongs to somebody who can
see the settlement it will be checked against; a settlement is a multi-page
document that arrives at an office by post, not a thing anybody photographs at a
tailgate. A method with no route 404s, which is the entire design of that table
— and `test_farmops_api` asserts it in both directions, so the absence stays a
decision rather than becoming an omission.

`classify_receipt` **is** published despite touching nothing, and that is the
interesting one. It could have shipped as a keyword table compiled into the app.
It is a route so the table exists **once** rather than once per platform: the
classification a phone shows and the classification the catalogue makes are the
same function call, or they are two answers to one question that drift apart the
first time somebody adds a keyword on one side.

On both create routes the company comes from the caller's scope and
`submitted_by` from the authenticated account. An account that can name somebody
else in a request body is not scoped to anything — and an expense filed against
another worker's name is a reimbursement claim with the wrong signature on it.

## Fixed: every `create_customer` call on a stock site

`create_customer` defaulted `customer_group` to `All Customer Groups`. That is
the **root** of the tree, ERPNext puts an `is_group = 0` link filter on
`Customer.customer_group`, and the framework refused the insert. On a stock site
that was every call which did not name a group — which is every call the tool
exists to make easy. `create_supplier` had the identical bug against
`All Supplier Groups`.

The default is now the site's alphabetically first **non-group** node
(`Commercial` on a stock ERPNext install). Alphabetical rather than
first-created, because a default that depends on insertion order differs between
two sites nobody can tell apart. A site with only group nodes is refused with
*that* reason rather than with "no Customer Group called X", which reads as a
typo and sends somebody looking for a record that could never have been there.

`territory` is deliberately unchanged, and the asymmetry is the point: ERPNext
accepts a group Territory on a Customer, and its own Selling Settings default is
`All Territories`. Changing it would be fixing a bug nobody has.

**How this shipped in v0.66.0 with a green suite** is worth recording. The
standalone double does not reproduce ERPNext's link filters, so a group node is a
perfectly valid docname to it. The tests asserted the behaviour that existed
rather than the behaviour ERPNext would accept — which is the failure mode of
every test double, and the reason the FrappeTestCase suite exists.

Four new doctypes: `Scale Ticket`, `Settlement Statement`, and the settlement's
two child tables `Settlement Line Item` and `Settlement Deduction`. Both parent
registers are on `install._PRECIOUS_DOCTYPES` — a scale ticket is the grower's
**only** copy, and losing the register makes every settlement that follows
unauditable.

`bench migrate` after upgrading. The eight register tools carry a predicate
requiring both the new doctypes **and** the ERPNext app: both link to `Customer`,
which exists nowhere else, so on a Frappe-only bench they go unavailable by name
rather than failing inside a query. `classify_receipt` needs neither and is
available everywhere.
