# v0.53.0 — the badge in the wallet the worker already carries

`generate_employee_badge_qr` has minted `CFL-0001` and drawn its QR since
v0.50.0, and what came back was **a PNG somebody has to print**. In practice that
means a trip back to an office in the middle of a hire day, a laminator, and a
card that goes through a wash cycle in August and stops scanning at a bin
trailer.

Every worker in the orchard is already carrying a phone with a wallet on it.

`generate_employee_badge_pass` is the same badge with a different delivery: an
**Apple Wallet `.pkpass`** the foreman AirDrops straight off the handset — it
opens into Wallet on the worker's device with **nothing installed there** — and a
**Google Wallet save link** for the Android half.

---

## 1. It is the same badge, not a second credential

The identifier, the minting, the Bucket Log Badge Map row and the retirement of a
replaced card are `badges.py`'s, called through the same two functions
`generate_employee_badge_qr` uses (`_choose_badge_id`, then `_record_badge`).

That is not code-sharing for its own sake. **A worker with a printed card and a
wallet pass is holding two copies of one badge**, and the moment those could
diverge is the moment a bucket scanned off the phone pays somebody the card does
not. There is one identifier and both artefacts render it — a bin trailer
scanning a phone screen and one scanning a laminated card produce the identical
string and resolve through the identical `resolve_badge` call.

The consequence is that the tool inherits the whole contract:

- **Idempotent without `regenerate`.** Somebody who already holds a live badge
  gets a pass for *that* badge rather than a second identifier — which is what
  makes the wizard's button safe to press twice on a bad connection.
- **`regenerate: true` is the lost-card path.** New ID, old one retired in the
  same call.
- Refuses somebody who has left, somebody employed by another entity, and an
  entity the caller cannot reach, each by name.

---

## 2. A `.pkpass` is a signed zip, and the signature is the hard part

The archive is `pass.json`, the images, `manifest.json` (a SHA-1 per member) and
`signature` — a detached PKCS#7 over the manifest, made with a certificate Apple
issues against one Pass Type ID. Wallet verifies the signature, then verifies
every member against the manifest, so changing one pixel of the photograph after
signing invalidates the whole pass.

**The SHA-1 is Apple's format, not a security choice this app made.** A stronger
digest in the manifest produces a pass Wallet refuses. The strength is in the
SHA-256 signature over it. `usedforsecurity=False` says so to anything auditing
digest use, and keeps the call working on a FIPS build.

The archive is **deterministic** — fixed member order, fixed timestamps — so the
same badge produces the same bytes twice, which is what lets the tool say whether
a regenerated pass actually differs from the one already attached.

---

## 3. An unsigned pass is built and *said to be* unsigned

Tim has no Apple Pass Type ID certificate yet. A site without one gets a
**complete and correct** `.pkpass` here — right `pass.json`, right images, right
manifest — with **no `signature` member**, and every caller is told
`apple.signed: false` in the same breath, with the `site_config.json` keys it
needs named in the sentence.

Apple Wallet will refuse to open that file, and that refusal is the honest
outcome. The alternative considered and rejected was a self-signed blob with a
`signature` file in it: Wallet refuses it just as hard, while looking to
everybody upstream like it should have worked.

**Nothing in the app changes the day the certificate arrives.** The same call
starts signing. `docs/wallet-passes.md` is the list of what to obtain from Apple
(Pass Type ID, WWDR G4, a `.p12` exported from Keychain) and from Google (issuer
account, service account key), the exact `site_config.json` keys, and a
self-signed `openssl` path for exercising the plumbing first.

Credentials live in `site_config.json` rather than the settings form, for the
same reason `farm_ops_mobile_enabled` can: this is a private signing key and the
path to it, and a Single doctype is editable by anybody who reaches the Desk with
the right role.

---

## 4. Google fetches images; it is not sent them

Android has no equivalent of the AirDropped file. A Google Wallet pass is a JSON
object signed into an RS256 JWT that becomes a `pay.google.com/gp/v/save/…` link
— the worker taps it and **Google builds the pass server-side**, fetching the
images itself over the public internet.

So a photograph at `/private/files/…` — which is what `set_employee_photo`
correctly writes, because it is a picture of a person — **cannot be on a Google
Wallet pass**. Every image left out for that reason is named in
`google.warnings` rather than written as a URL that renders as a grey box on a
worker's phone. The Apple pass carries the same photograph inside the archive and
is unaffected.

The asymmetry is real and is not worth hiding. It is written down in the module
header, in the tool's result, and in `docs/wallet-passes.md`.

An unconfigured site still gets the **pass object** — it is what an operator
pastes into Google's Pass Builder to check the shape before committing to a
service account.

---

## 5. Images

The employee photograph becomes Apple's `thumbnail.png` (90px) and
`thumbnail@2x.png` (180px); the company's `badge_logo` becomes `logo.png` /
`logo@2x.png` and the three icon sizes. Cropped to **cover** rather than
letterboxed, because the square sizes are a headshot and the thing a foreman
looks at across a bin trailer is the face.

- A JPEG is converted; a pass member has to be a PNG and a phone uploads JPEG.
- An unreadable image becomes the **initials tile** — the same fallback the
  printed card has had since v0.50.0 — plus a warning. A badge with initials
  where a face should be is a working badge.
- `icon.png` is present whatever happens. A `.pkpass` without one is not a pass,
  Wallet rejects the archive, and a bench with no Pillow would otherwise be a
  bench that cannot issue a wallet badge at all — so there is a thirty-line
  `zlib` PNG writer for the fallback, the same trade `render/qr.py` makes with
  the same writer.
- Anything that will not come under 512 KB after processing is left out and
  named. A pass is AirDropped over a yard's wifi.

---

## 6. The route

`POST /farmops/api/mobile/get_employee_badge_pass`

The bytes travel **in the answer** as `apple.pkpass_base64`, not as a `file_url`.
That is the whole reason the route exists rather than the tool being enough: the
handset authenticates against this door with `X-FarmOps-Token`, not with a Frappe
session, so a private file URL is a login page to it — and a foreman cannot
AirDrop a login page. `include_base64` is set by the wrapper and is not a body
key.

`badge_id` is not accepted, for the reason it is not accepted on
`generate_employee_badge_qr`: minting a payroll key is the server's job, not
whatever a foreman typed. `attach` is not accepted either — the pass is always
filed against the Employee, so a reissue has a record and a Desk operator can
hand the same file to somebody who lost their phone rather than issuing a new
badge for it.

**The iOS side is separate, tracked work.** Nothing in `fafo_ios` calls this
route yet; it is listed in `PENDING_IOS_INTEGRATION` alongside v0.52.0's
model-serving pair, so the contract tests do not claim a Swift mirror that does
not exist. `docs/wallet-passes.md` §3 has what the handset needs to do with the
answer.

---

## 7. What this does not do

**There is no push update service.** A pass already on a phone stays there; there
is no `webServiceURL`, no per-pass `authenticationToken` and no registration
endpoint. What makes that safe is that the pass carries only `badge_id` —
revocation happens in the register, and a worker holding a retired pass scans a
badge that resolves to nobody, exactly as with a retired laminated card. Adding
push is a real feature and it is not this one.

**`sharingProhibited` is set.** A badge attributes piece work to a person, so a
pass a picker can AirDrop onward is a pass two people can scan buckets with. The
foreman AirDrops it once, at onboarding; after that it does not travel.
