# v0.68.0 — the fourth branch, and the one that was never an expense

Twenty-three tools, closing Sprint 3 of the Gap Closure Plan from both ends.

---

## The gap, restated

v0.67.0 left `classify_receipt` naming four kinds of paper and only building
registers for two of them:

| The paper | The register |
|---|---|
| a fuel or parts slip | Expense Receipt (v0.31.0) |
| a scale ticket | Scale Ticket (v0.67.0) |
| a packout settlement | Settlement Statement (v0.67.0) |
| a vendor invoice | Purchase Invoice — *"a later sprint"* |

This is that sprint. It has two halves, built and shipped together because
the second cannot work without the first: a general purchasing pipeline
(Purchase Order → Purchase Receipt → Purchase Invoice → Payment Entry, plus AP
ageing) is what a Purchase Invoice actually *is* on this site, and the
receipt-to-invoice tool this release adds is a thin, receipt-specific layer
that hands that pipeline a resolved Supplier, account and line — it does not
duplicate it.

## Half one: the purchasing pipeline

Sixteen tools over four ERPNext documents. `list_purchase_orders` and
`get_outstanding_invoices` already existed in `tools/trade.py`; this is
everything else a purchase actually walks through:

- **Purchase Order** — `create_purchase_order`, `get_purchase_order`,
  `submit_purchase_order`.
- **Purchase Receipt** — `create_purchase_receipt`, `get_purchase_receipt`,
  `list_purchase_receipts`, `submit_purchase_receipt`. Goods received against
  a Supplier, optionally checked against a submitted Purchase Order for the
  same supplier.
- **Purchase Invoice** — `create_purchase_invoice`, `get_purchase_invoice`,
  `list_purchase_invoices`, `submit_purchase_invoice`. The bill.
- **Payment Entry** — `create_payment_entry`, `get_payment_entry`,
  `list_payment_entries`, `submit_payment_entry`. `payment_type="Pay"` and
  `party_type="Supplier"` only — this is the AP side, and a Receive-type entry
  belongs with the receivables tools instead. Allocates across one or more
  submitted invoices, partial amounts allowed, or on-account with none.
- **`get_ap_aging`** — a supplier's true balance from GL Entry against every
  account typed Payable, aged per open invoice the same way
  `get_outstanding_invoices` ages receivables. A per-supplier `drift` field
  fires when the ledger total and the open-invoice total disagree, which
  usually means a manual Journal Entry against the Payable account outside the
  normal invoice/payment flow — reported, not corrected, the same principle
  v0.67.0's settlement reconciliation runs on.

Every create is draft-only; every submit is its own separately-switched tool.
Same split `create_journal_entry` / `submit_journal_entry` established:
enabling "propose" is not enabling "post."

## Half two: what happens to a receipt afterward

Seven tools, and one idea behind all of them: capture is fast and rough,
review is slow and careful, and this release is what a bookkeeper does in
between.

### The correction nobody had a tool for

`submit_expense_receipt` writes `category`, `supplier`, and everything else at
the moment of capture — a foreman standing at a fuel pump, one hand on the
phone. That is exactly the wrong moment to get a cost center right. Until this
release, the only way to recode a receipt after the fact was the Desk.

**`update_expense_receipt`** fixes `cost_center`, `supplier`, `category` and
`notes` — nothing else — on a receipt in *any* status, Approved included. It
does not touch `merchant`, `amount`, `receipt_date` or the review trail,
because none of those four fields is the thing that got approved or rejected;
recoding which bucket a $184 fuel stop landed in does not reopen whether $184
was a reasonable fuel stop. A call naming no field, or naming values the
record already has, is refused rather than silently accepted — the caller's
next question after either is "did that do anything," and the answer should
not need a second call.

### Reading the pile

**`get_expense_summary`** totals by category and buckets a trend by week,
month or quarter. Rejected receipts are excluded from the totals by default —
a rejected receipt was decided *not* to be a real expense, and a dashboard
that counted it anyway would overstate spend by exactly what somebody already
refused — and the excluded count is reported rather than hidden, in the same
spirit as every other honesty check this app makes about what a number
includes.

**`get_expense_report`** is the opposite instinct: nothing is excluded by
default, because a detailed export is where somebody checks what happened to
*one specific* receipt, and a rejected one that silently disappeared would
look like it was never captured at all. `csv:true` returns the same rows as a
ready-to-save string.

### Merchant, meet Supplier

`submit_expense_receipt`'s `supplier` link has been optional and *never
inferred* since v0.67.0 — no fuzzy match from an OCR'd merchant string to a
Supplier record, because a wrong guess there is a fabricated vendor total. That
rule does not change. What this release adds is the suggestion a human would
have made by hand:

**`normalize_merchant`** scores a merchant string against every Supplier with
plain text similarity — corporate suffixes (Co, LLC, Inc, Corp, Ltd …) and
punctuation stripped from both sides first, so `WILBUR ELLIS CO` and
`Wilbur-Ellis Company LLC` normalize to the same six words and score high. No
model, no training data — a table of legal-form words and `difflib`, auditable
by reading it. It never writes anything.

**`list_merchant_aliases`** is not a table of its own. It is every Expense
Receipt whose `supplier` is already set, read back grouped by which Supplier —
the alias register this app keeps is exactly the data already on hand, and it
changes for free the day a link changes.

### The fourth branch, built

**`create_purchase_invoice_from_receipt`** is what `classify_receipt`'s `bill`
branch has been waiting for. Given an APPROVED Expense Receipt, it resolves a
Supplier (the receipt's own link, an explicit argument, a confident
`normalize_merchant` match, or — failing all three — a brand-new Supplier
created from the merchant string) and a category-matched expense account, then
hands both to `purchasing.create_purchase_invoice` — the tool this release's
other half ships — rather than writing the Purchase Invoice itself. The line
bills against a shared, non-stock Item per category, created once and reused
by every later receipt in it, never one Item per receipt.

Two refusals do the real work here. A receipt that is not yet Approved is
refused: billing an unreviewed number would put it straight into the payables
ledger. And a receipt categorised `Owner Draw` is refused *by name* — because
that money was never a bill from a vendor.

### The one that was never an expense

An owner's draw against the company's equity has always looked, to this app,
exactly like a distribution — `record_member_event` has matched "owner draw"
and "member draw" in its equity-keyword table since it was written. What it
required was a Cap Table Entry and a Member accounting dimension, which is
real infrastructure for a real holding-company structure, and not every
receipt tagged `Owner Draw` arrives with either.

**`create_owner_draw`** is the narrower door: a draft Journal Entry — debit
the matched equity account, credit bank or cash — independent of whether a
site has adopted the cap table machinery at all. It **requires the Member
Manager role** (or System Manager), checked before anything else runs; an
operator creates that Role in the Desk and grants it to whoever is actually
authorised to move the company's equity. Optionally linked to the Expense
Receipt that prompted it, via the same `linked_doctype`/`linked_document` pair
`create_purchase_invoice_from_receipt` uses — one generic Dynamic Link on the
receipt, reused by whichever downstream document it becomes, rather than a
separate Link field per document type.

## What shipped where

- `erpnext_mcp/tools/purchasing.py` — the sixteen-tool pipeline.
- `erpnext_mcp/tools/expenses.py` — `update_expense_receipt`,
  `get_expense_summary`, `get_expense_report`; `Owner Draw` added to
  `CATEGORIES`.
- `erpnext_mcp/tools/receipts.py` — `normalize_merchant`,
  `list_merchant_aliases`, `create_purchase_invoice_from_receipt`.
- `erpnext_mcp/tools/governance.py` — `create_owner_draw` and the
  `Member Manager` role gate.
- Expense Receipt gains `cost_center`, `linked_doctype` and `linked_document`.
- All twenty-three tools registered with their own switches, defaulting the
  same way every tool in this app defaults: reads on, writes off.
