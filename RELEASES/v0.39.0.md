# v0.39.0 — the KPI was a function, and the farm that needed it could not write one

**v0.19.5 shipped one KPI. v0.19.6 put it under a window standard and showed the
standard generalized by registering two more beside it. All three are Python
functions, and adding a fourth is six lines, a test, a review, a release and a
deploy — which is a perfectly good process for a KPI this app's authors chose,
and no process at all for a KPI somebody's lender asked about on a Tuesday.**

Seven new MCP tools (346 total: 160 read, 186 mutating). One new DocType,
`Financial KPI Definition`. One new service, `erpnext_mcp/services/kpi_engine.py`.
One new compliance rule, the twenty-first, and the first in that file that is
about money rather than about a regulator. One new scheduled job, the eighth.

---

## The problem, stated the way an operation states it

Every farm has two or three ratios that are genuinely its own.

A packing house watches **cost per bin**, and the number only means anything
against its own bin count. An operation carrying an equipment note watches **debt
service coverage on the covenant's definition** — which is the lender's
arithmetic, written into a document, and is not the same as anybody else's DSCR.
A family office watches **distributions against normalized cash flow**, because
that is the conversation it has with its members every December.

None of those belong in a shipped app. All of them belong on the dashboard of the
farm that needs them. Before this release the only way to get one was to write
Python, and the people who know what the ratio should be are not the people who
can write it.

So a KPI is now a **record**.

---

## What did not move, and why that is the release rather than a caveat

`formula_type` has exactly two values and both are deterministic.

**`Built-in`** delegates to a computer that ships with this app — registered in
`services/financial_reports.py`, reviewed and tested like any other code. The
record still owns everything an operator would legitimately change: the window,
the step, the lookback, the four thresholds, the dashboard position, the switch.
It owns nothing about the arithmetic.

**`Expression`** evaluates the text in `expression` over the variables
`expression_inputs` names. It is parsed to an AST, every node is checked against
an allowlist of arithmetic, and the surviving tree is walked by an evaluator in
`kpi_engine.py` that has no builtins, no attribute access, no subscripts, no
statements, no name binding, and no calls except to four bound numeric functions.

**There is no third value, and there is no field anywhere on the record that
holds Python.**

That is a deliberate difference from `Compliance Rule.custom_python`, and the
reason is what the two things have to express. A compliance rule can genuinely
need to say *"this record is stale unless that other record supersedes it"* —
a shape no set of declarative fields captures, which is why `alerts/sandbox.py`
is a restricted interpreter with loops, statements and comprehensions in it. A
financial KPI is a number divided by another number. Nothing about a ratio needs
a `for` loop, so the sandbox here does not have one — and a sandbox without
loops, without statements and without name binding is a very much smaller thing
to be confident about than one with them.

Two sandboxes, on purpose. Merging them would mean the finance side inherited
every node the compliance side needs.

### What the grammar allows

`+ - * / // % **` on numbers and variable names, unary minus, parentheses,
comparisons inside a conditional, and calls to `min`, `max`, `abs`, `round`.

### What it refuses, by name, at save time

`import` and `__import__` (one import is `os`, and `os` is the filesystem);
**every attribute access**, because `x.__class__.__bases__[0].__subclasses__()`
is the standard escape from every sandbox that forgot it and needs no imports at
all; subscripts, lists, dicts, sets, tuples, f-strings, starred arguments,
lambdas, every comprehension, assignment expressions, string constants, and any
call to anything not on the four-name list. Underscore-leading names are refused
whatever they turn out to mean. An exponent above 64 is refused at evaluation,
because `9 ** 9 ** 9` is nine characters and will hold a worker until somebody
restarts it.

Refused **at save time** rather than at compute time, which matters more than it
sounds: an operator finds out that `import` is not available while they are
looking at the form, rather than at three in the morning when the refresh runs
and a KPI quietly stops producing values.

## A definition holds the question. It never holds an answer

Nothing on a `Financial KPI Definition` is a number that came out of the ledger.
The thresholds are lines somebody drew and the window fields say which period to
ask about; that is the whole of it.

Every figure is computed from the ledger at the moment it is asked for, cached in
`Financial KPI History` **with the components that produced it**, and derivable
again by rerunning the same computation over the same window. That is what makes
the cache safe to delete and what makes a historical figure defensible a year
later, and it is the rule the whole reporting side of this app has been built on
since v0.19.6.

An **Expression** KPI is as defensible as a built-in one, and this is why: the
components dict carries every input with what it matched — how many accounts, how
many GL entries, whether it was read as credits-less-debits or the other way,
whether it was a balance or a movement. A reader sees not just that
`current_assets` was 412,000 but where the 412,000 came from.

## Every KPI goes through the window standard. None of them gets its own

`compute_kpi` does not compute a window. It builds a computer — a callable taking
`(company, period_start, period_end)` — and hands it to
`windowed_reports.compute_windowed`, which is the same function
`get_windowed_report` has gone through since v0.19.6.

The boundaries, the fiscal-year anchoring, the last-completed-step rule, the
cache, the prior-window history, the statistics and the partial-window warnings
therefore behave **identically** for a KPI somebody typed into a form this
morning and for the one that shipped in v0.19.5. There is a test asserting the
two produce the same figure for the same window, because they go through the same
code.

That is not tidiness. A framework whose new KPIs get a second, simpler window
implementation is a framework where the new KPIs are quietly wrong at the fiscal
year boundary, and nobody finds out until a lender does.

---

## `kpi_id` is the cache key, which is why it is unique and why it cannot move

A Compliance Rule is versioned by copy: an edit writes v+1 and the old row stays,
so an alert raised in April can still be read against the definition that raised
it. **A KPI is not**, and the difference is what the two records produce.

An alert is an **event** with a definition behind it. A KPI is a **line**, and a
line assembled from two definitions of one number is a chart with an unmarked
join in it — which is the single most misleading object a finance dashboard can
contain.

So `kpi_id` is unique, `update_financial_kpi_definition` refuses to rename it at
all, and changing the arithmetic of a live KPI is reported as a decision with the
cached row count in front of the caller. The usual right move is a new `kpi_id`
beside the old one so both series stay readable; where it is genuinely a
correction, `refresh_kpi_cache(force=true)` rebuilds the whole series under the
new formula. `source_version` on each cached row is the other half of the same
guarantee, for the case where the app itself changes a built-in computer.

## The four input sources

```json
{
  "current_assets":      {"source": "gl", "root_type": "Asset", "balance": true},
  "current_liabilities": {"source": "gl", "root_type": "Liability", "balance": true},
  "sales":               {"source": "report", "report_name": "revenue", "path": "total"},
  "cf_per_acre":         {"source": "kpi", "kpi_id": "sustainable_cf_per_acre"},
  "sqft_per_acre":       {"source": "constant", "value": 43560}
}
```

**`gl`** reads GL Entry directly, for the reason `sustainable_cf_per_acre`
computes OCF from GL rather than off the Cash Flow report: every figure traces
back to rows somebody can open, which is what makes it defensible to a lender.
Narrow with `root_type`, `account_type`, `accounts` or `account_number_prefix`; an
input that narrows nothing is refused, because an unnarrowed query sums the whole
chart of accounts to approximately zero and that is a number nobody would
question.

**`"balance": true` is the flag the whole source turns on.** A balance is a
POSITION at the window's end; a movement is what crossed it. A current ratio built
from twelve months of movement in a cash account is not a current ratio — it is a
cash flow with a ratio's name on it, and it will be wrong by whatever the opening
balance was.

**The sign is `natural` by default**, which reads credit-balance roots (Income,
Liability, Equity) as credits less debits and debit-balance roots (Asset, Expense)
the other way, so every figure comes out positive on a well-kept set of books.
Revenue read the wrong way round comes out negative everywhere, and a figure that
is negative everywhere is one everybody misreads as a loss. Where one input
matches accounts across two roots, the mixture is warned about rather than
silently netted.

**`report`** reads a component off a shipped computer, so an Expression KPI can be
built on top of one. **`kpi`** is another definition's value, with cycles refused
at save time for the two shapes people build by hand (a definition naming itself,
and a mutual pair) and caught at compute time with a named chain for everything
else. **`constant`** is a number with a name, which is what a magic constant in a
formula should always have been.

## Thresholds go to the compliance calendar, not to a second alerting system

A KPI past its threshold raises a `Compliance Alert` through the existing hourly
sweep, under a new `Finance` category, with the same dismissal, the same snooze,
and the same auto-clear when the value comes back inside. Nobody closes it,
because there was never a task — there was a number.

An operation with two alerting systems reads neither, and a covenant about to be
breached is exactly as much a Monday-morning problem as a cabin with a dead
carbon monoxide detector.

`financial_kpi_threshold_breach` is the twenty-first shipped rule and the third
permanent built-in. It is built-in for a reason neither of the other two has:
**its thresholds are not on its own row.** They live on each Financial KPI
Definition, because they are per-KPI and per-unit — a ratio's warning line and a
dollar figure's warning line are not values that can share a column. The rule
record still owns the two severities, the scope filters and the switch.

**It reads the cache and never computes.** The alert sweep runs hourly beside
somebody's real work; a scan that recomputed every KPI for every company would
put minutes of GL arithmetic on that path, every hour, for a figure that moves
once a month. The overnight refresh fills the cache and the scan reads the newest
snapshot it finds — which is at most a day old and is the *same* figure the
dashboard is showing, so an alert and a dashboard can never disagree about the
number.

**A KPI with no cached value raises nothing AND DISMISSES NOTHING**, which is the
reading every rule in that file gives to an absent record. A definition created
this morning has no history until the job reaches it, and treating that silence
as a pass would be as wrong as treating it as a breach.

### Four thresholds, and empty is not zero

`threshold_warning_low`, `threshold_critical_low`, `threshold_warning_high`,
`threshold_critical_high`. An empty one means that direction is not bad for this
KPI; a zero means a negative value is a warning. On a cash-flow KPI those are two
very different operations, so the two are kept distinguishable everywhere.

A crossed pair — a critical floor above its warning floor, a critical ceiling
below its warning ceiling, or low and high thresholds that overlap — is refused at
save time. The first two can never be read the way they are written; the third
puts the KPI permanently in alert.

**A KPI with no thresholds reports `No thresholds`, not `OK`.** "Nobody drew a
line" and "inside the line" are different statements, and a dashboard showing them
the same green would be claiming something nobody checked. `compute_all_kpis`
names the unwatched KPIs in a note, because an empty `breached` list is not by
itself a healthy operation.

## The seeder writes exactly one definition, and that is the argument

`Sustainable CF/Acre`, as a Built-in KPI on the computer that has shipped since
v0.19.5, adopting the `kpi_key` the cache has been using since v0.19.6 — so the
existing series continues rather than a second one starting beside it under a new
name.

A seeded KPI is a claim that this app knows what an operation should watch, and it
can only honestly make that claim about a metric it also ships the computer for.
A current ratio does not qualify — not because it is a bad metric, but because the
accounts that make it up are named differently on every chart, and a seeded
definition pointing at the wrong ones would put a confident, precise, **wrong**
number on somebody's dashboard from the day they installed the app.

**No thresholds are seeded either**, for the same reason. A defensible floor under
cash flow per acre is a number about one operation's own cost structure and debt
service, and a seeded one would be a line somebody had not drawn being enforced on
a compliance calendar.

Idempotent and checked by `kpi_id`, like every seeder in `install.py`: an operator
who disabled it stays disabled, one who set thresholds keeps them, one who moved
it down the dashboard does not find it back at the top after every migrate.

## The eighth scheduled job

`services.kpi_engine.refresh_all_kpi_caches` at `0 3 * * *` — between the
shipped-report sweep at two and the regulation feed at four. It is the two
o'clock job's counterpart for KPIs that are records, and it exists separately
because a shipped report has no definition to read its window type, window length
and step from, so the older job assumes a monthly TTM.

**One job that iterates, and here that is load-bearing rather than tidy.** The
whole point of this release is that an operator adds a KPI without a code
release, and a KPI that needed its own scheduler entry would be one they could
not add. Adding a KPI adds no line to `hooks.py`.

It shares `enable_kpi_history_sweep` with the two o'clock job rather than getting
a second checkbox — they cache the same doctype for the same reason, and a second
setting called something almost identical is how a setting stops being read.

It never raises, takes no arguments, is incremental against what is already
cached, and writes only this app's own `Financial KPI History`.

## The seven tools

| Tool | Kind | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `create_financial_kpi_definition` | mutating | Define one KPI as a record |
| `update_financial_kpi_definition` | mutating | Edit one in place, with the diff and the previous values |
| `list_financial_kpi_definitions` | read | The register, with `thresholded_count` |
| `get_financial_kpi_definition` | read | One in full, with `problems` and the cached row count |
| `compute_kpi` | read | One KPI over its own window, with history and threshold verdict |
| `compute_all_kpis` | read | The whole dashboard in one call |
| `refresh_kpi_cache` | mutating | Fill or rebuild the cached history |

**Enabled on creation**, unlike a Compliance Rule, and the difference is what the
two do when wrong. A rule that fires wrongly accuses somebody of a compliance
failure, so nothing goes live unapproved. A KPI that is wrong reports a number, on
a dashboard, beside its own ingredients and its own computation warnings — which
is a thing a reader can check, and an accusation is not. `enabled=false` authors
one quietly.

**The role gate is the KPI one**: System Manager, Accounts Manager, Farm Manager
— the same three that hold the normalization register. A KPI definition decides
what number a lender is shown and what threshold raises an alert about it, which
is the same class of authority as approving an add-back to operating cash flow,
and it is a smaller list than the one that reads the calendar those alerts land
on.

**The two compute tools warm a cache and write nothing else** — no Account, no GL
Entry, no Journal Entry, no Asset, no Field — and there is a test asserting it.

## What the existing tools got out of it

`recompute_kpi_history` now accepts a `kpi_key` that names a **definition** and
delegates to the framework, which reads that definition's own window type, window
length and step rather than assuming a monthly TTM. A caller who already knows
that tool does not have to learn a second one, and it refuses a name that is
neither a shipped report nor a definition — naming both registers.

`list_financial_kpi_history` now returns a `definitions` block joining each
`kpi_key` in the series to the record that owns it: title, unit, category,
thresholds. A cached series is a column of numbers and a date, and the thing a
reader needs beside it is what the number *is* — 0.42 is a catastrophe as a
current ratio, a fine margin as a percentage and a rounding error as dollars.
It also reports orphaned keys: rows whose `kpi_key` has no definition, which on a
site that renamed one are the half nothing will ever extend.

Sites that have not migrated the definition DocType get exactly the payload
v0.38.0 gave.

## Tests

82 new standalone tests (5,377 total), in eight groups:

1. **The sandbox is arithmetic and nothing else** — every refused shape checked
   by name, arithmetic evaluated, a division by zero producing a null that names
   the empty denominator.
2. **The inputs resolve to queries** — the four sources, and the balance-versus-
   movement distinction asserted as a delta against the seeded ledger.
3. **A definition is refused before it lies** — the duplicate key, the
   unusable key, the missing computer, the undefined variable, the three crossed
   threshold pairs, the self-reference and the mutual pair.
4. **Computing goes through the window standard** — a defined KPI and
   `get_windowed_report` asserted equal for the same window.
5. **The threshold verdict** — worst-first, inclusive boundaries, and
   `No thresholds` distinguished from `OK`.
6. **The alert is on the one calendar** — the rule seeds, lands under `Finance`,
   reads the cache without writing anything, carries no due date, and reads each
   definition's own window.
7. **The cache and the overnight job** — incremental, `force`, the delegation
   from `recompute_kpi_history`, the kill switch, and the no-arguments contract.
8. **The guards** — the role gate, the seven switches, the company scope, the
   seeder's idempotence, and the read-warms-a-cache-and-nothing-else promise.
