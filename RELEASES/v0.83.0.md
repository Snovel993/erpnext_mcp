# v0.83.0 — the button that was always missing

Three Desk surfaces and one delete that stops being refused. No new MCP tools:
everything here connects machinery that already worked to a person who could not
reach it.

---

## The one idea

This release adds almost no capability. `generate_asset_qr` has drawn asset
symbols since v0.17.0. `generate_employee_badge_qr` has drawn badges since
v0.50.0. The Employee form has had an **ID Card** button since v0.56.0 and the
Employee list a **Print Badge Sheet** action since the same release. Every step
of onboarding worked.

What none of it had was a door a person could walk through without an AI client
in the middle. An MCP tool answers JSON-RPC to a token; a mechanic standing at a
bench with a valve open on screen has a session, a role and a mouse. Three of the
four surfaces below are that door, and the fourth is a page saying what order to
use them in.

**That is a real release and not a cosmetic one.** A capability nobody can reach
is not a capability, and "ask the model for base64 and paste it somewhere" was
the documented way to print an asset tag for four months.

## 1. Asset QR tags, from the Desk

Two Client Script rows and two whitelisted methods.

| Surface | Row | Calls |
|---|---|---|
| Asset Register **form** | `Asset Register — QR Tag` | `erpnext_mcp.api.asset_tags.asset_qr_tag` |
| Asset Register **list** | `Asset Register — Generate QR Sheet` | `erpnext_mcp.asset_tag_sheet.render_asset_qr_sheet` |

The form button shows the symbol large — a QR about to be stuck on a pump is
worth looking at first — and prints one label. The list action takes the ticked
rows and prints a sheet.

### The label sheet is measured, not approximated

`asset_tag_sheet.py` knows the geometry of Avery 5160 (30 up) and 5163 (10 up)
from the manufacturer's own template, and positions every label **absolutely**
rather than letting an inline-block flow accumulate rounding error down the page.
On stock with no vertical gutter — 5160 rows butt up against each other — a third
of a millimetre per row is a label straddling a perforation by row ten.

A template name the app has never heard of **falls back and says so on the page**
rather than throwing at somebody holding a roll of labels. The sheet also says
"print at 100%", because "fit to page" rescales the grid and every label lands off
its die cut.

### The tag carries the docname in text as well as in the symbol

A QR on a pump housing gets sun, dust, hydraulic fluid and a pressure washer. The
failure mode is not "the label falls off" — it is "the symbol no longer decodes
and the label is still firmly attached". A tag whose docname is printed underneath
degrades into something somebody can still type into the search bar, and it is
printed **even when the symbol failed to render at all**.

The symbol encodes `qr_url` (`<site>/scan/<docname>`) and not the bare docname,
which is what makes a phone's own camera do something useful with it.
`universal_scan` unwraps it back.

### What gates it

`frappe.has_permission("Asset Register", "read", doc=<name>, throw=True)` on the
**specific record**, so a User Permission scoping somebody to one company scopes
their labels too.

The `allow_<tool>` switches are deliberately **not** consulted — the call
`api/badges.py`, `api/gis.py` and `api/__init__.py` all made before. Those
switches are the AI's leash: `allow_generate_asset_qr` off means "the model may
not do this", and reading it here would mean an operator who distrusts the model
also loses the button on their own form.

`read` and not `write`, which is the whole character of the button: printing a
label writes nothing and stamps no `last_scan_at`. Generating a tag is not a
sighting of the machine.

## 2. Onboard Worker

A Workspace at `/app/onboard-worker`, and the content of it is the **order**.

```
Hire Somebody  →  Badge & Print  →  Phone Enrolment      (+ Badge Register)
   New Employee     Employee list      Mobile Access Grant
```

Underneath, the paperwork that follows somebody through their first fortnight
rather than their first hour: I-9 and W-4, training and certification, housing.
Separated on purpose — putting them in the same row would make the sequence
unreadable, which is the one thing the page is for.

**Not a wizard**, and not merely because a wizard is more work. Real onboarding
does not complete in one sitting: somebody is hired Monday, their I-9 is verified
when their documents turn up Wednesday, their phone is enrolled when they
remember to bring it. A wizard models a transaction that finishes or is
abandoned; this is a checklist somebody comes back to.

The page is **never rebuilt over somebody's arrangement**. Only an empty
`content` — this app's own half-built page, or a fresh install — is written.

## 3. Client Scripts, not hooks — including where the hook was allowed

`badge_form_action.py` is a Client Script row because it *had* to be: Employee is
ERPNext's doctype and `test_hooks.TheFormScripts` enforces that everything in
`doctype_js` is one this app created.

Asset Register is **ours**, and already in `doctype_js` carrying the map widget.
The hook was open and was not taken:

1. `test_the_shared_widget_is_listed_first_everywhere` asserts `len(files) == 2`
   for every entry. A third file does not break the form; it breaks the assertion
   that keeps the map widget's load order honest, and loosening a load-bearing
   test to hang a button off it is the wrong trade.
2. **An operator can see and decline a Client Script row.** It is in the Desk with
   the source in front of them; untick `enabled` or delete it and the app will not
   put it back. A `doctype_js` file reappears at every `bench migrate`.
3. Same three-state seeder the badge buttons use, so a fix reaches sites that have
   already migrated: absent is written, this app's own unedited copy is updated,
   and a copy somebody has edited is left alone **and said out loud**.

`before_uninstall` names all three new rows.

## 4. Deleting an archived card stops being refused

`generate_mobile_login_qr` can file the card it draws in the governance archive
and record where it went on `Mobile Access Grant.qr_document`. That link made the
archived copy **undeletable**: Frappe refuses to delete a document any Link field
points at, so a card filed by mistake, or one belonging to somebody who left two
years ago, could not be cleared out without first finding a read-only field on a
register most people have never opened.

`GovernanceDocument.on_trash` now nulls that link. Order is the mechanism:
`delete_doc` runs `on_trash` and only then `check_if_doc_is_linked`, so by the
time the check looks there is nothing left to find. The grant survives with
everything else intact — this is set-null, not cascade.

### It is deliberately one field

Seven doctypes link to Governance Document. Three of the others describe
themselves as archive copies too — `Lease.governance_document`,
`Parcel.appraisal_document`, `Advisory Agreement.document_reference` — and none
of them is released.

**The test is not whether the label says "archive". It is whether this app can
redraw what was deleted.** A mobile card is regenerated from data the grant still
holds, so the filed PNG is a convenience. A lease's archive entry holds a signed
instrument that exists nowhere else on the site, and releasing its link would turn
a delete into a one-click destruction of the only copy — the precise opposite of
what an archive is for.

`test_archive_link_release.EveryOtherLinkStillRefuses` holds the tuple to that
rule, so widening it is a line somebody changes on purpose.

## 5. The harness learned to refuse a linked delete

`frappe.delete_doc` in `tests_standalone/harness.py` used to pop the row out of
the table and check nothing at all. Real Frappe runs `check_if_doc_is_linked` and
raises `LinkExistsError`.

This is the same class of gap as v0.12.0's missing link *validation*, and it cut
both ways: the suite could certify "the archived card was deleted" against a
framework that would have refused, and — worse for this release — the fix for that
refusal was untestable, because there was no refusal to fix.

The double now runs `on_trash`, then the link check, and honours `force=True` and
`ignore_links` as Frappe does. **It can only make tests stricter.** Two existing
tests were modelling deletes a real bench refuses and now say `force=True`
explicitly:

- `test_receipt_intelligence` — a Merchant Alias *is* the link to the supplier it
  points at.
- `test_task_templates` — a Farm Task naming its template keeps the template alive.

Both were asserting what happens *after* the row is gone. Forcing is how that
state is reached, and saying so is more honest than a double that pretended the
delete was free.

Two narrowings are stated rather than hidden: a document linking to itself does
not block its own delete (Frappe agrees, and a chain being torn down would
otherwise deadlock), and child-table links are not walked — this double stores
child rows inside their parent, so there is nothing to scan. That gap makes the
double **permissive**, not strict.

## Not in this release

**`rotate_token` was already there, and already stronger than a switch.**
`generate_mobile_login_qr` has taken `rotate_token` since v0.17.1 and it
**defaults to true** — a new login QR already invalidates the previous token
unless somebody explicitly passes `rotate_token=false` to reprint a card without
breaking a phone already in the field. It is not opt-in and should not become so:
a settings toggle gating rotation would only add a way to turn the protection off
site-wide, and the field description on `allow_generate_mobile_login_qr` has
described the current behaviour accurately since it was written.
