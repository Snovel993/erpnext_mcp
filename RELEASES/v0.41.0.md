# v0.41.0 — the job had a shape and three places kept it

**The template concept has been in this app since v0.16.0. What it has never had
is a record.**

The shape of a recurring job — what type of work it is, what skill it needs, how
long it takes, whether a foreman sends somebody or a worker picks it up, what
evidence closing it requires, what compliance record completing it produces, and
what a worker is actually meant to check — lived in three places that could not
be edited together:

* **`ALERT_TASK_MAP`**, a Python dict in `tools/dispatch.py`, holding thirteen
  recipes. Moving a habitability walk from forty-five minutes to sixty was a code
  release.
* **Three loose fields on every Compliance Rule** — `producer_farm_task_type`,
  `producer_skill_required`, `evidence_contract_json` — added in v0.22.0 so a
  rule an operator wrote could ask for work. They worked. What they could not do
  is be shared: two rules asking for the same job stated it twice, in full, and
  drifted the first time one of them was edited.
* **Whatever a foreman typed into `create_farm_task` that morning.**

A Farm Task Template is the missing record. Five tools, two DocTypes, one field
repointed, five seeded templates, and nothing wired.

---

## What it is not

It is not an Inspection Template, and the difference is load-bearing rather than
taxonomic.

| | Inspection Template (v0.21.0) | Farm Task Template (v0.41.0) |
|---|---|---|
| What it defines | a **multi-section visit** | the shape of **one task** |
| What one produces | several compliance records, at their own regulated cadences | at most one |
| Versioned | by copy — a session submits against its pinned version weeks later | **edited in place** |
| Raised by | `_bundle_into_sessions`, matching sections against pending alerts | `create_task_from_template`, or a rule's `producer_task_template` |

A Cabin Opening is one trip producing a Housing Inspection, two Detector Tests
and a Water Test, out of one claim, at four separate cadences. A smoke detector
test is one job. Collapsing the two would make every detector test carry a
sections table with a single row in it, and make `sections[0].produces_record_doctype`
the answer to a question the Farm Task already has a field for.

They share a vocabulary — the evidence contract keys, the skill strings, the
regime tags — and nothing else.

## The load-bearing rule: a task snapshots its template

`create_task_from_template` **copies** onto the task: the task type, the skill,
the estimated duration, the dispatch mode, the evidence contract, the record the
completion produces and its default field values, the instructions, and the whole
checklist with a `done` flag beside each item. After that the task is
self-contained. It can be claimed, worked, evidenced and closed with the template
deleted — and there is a test that deletes it.

Three consequences, and they are why this design is worth having:

**Editing a template changes what FUTURE tasks look like and nothing else.** A
worker halfway through a five-item habitability walk whose template lost an item
does not find their evidence attached to a list that no longer contains it.
Nobody's evidence contract tightens under them mid-job. `update_farm_task_template`
reports how many tasks the edit cannot reach, and says so in the note.

**The template needs no versioning by copy.** An Inspection Template and a
Compliance Rule are both versioned that way because a live document reads back
from them — a session submits against its template's sections weeks after
starting, an alert is read against the rule that raised it, so the old row has to
survive intact. Nothing reads back from this one. Versioning it would buy an
audit trail Frappe's own `track_changes` already keeps, at the cost of a register
in which the eight templates an operation actually runs are lost among forty
superseded rows.

**`Farm Task.template` is provenance, never a lookup.** It answers "where did
this shape come from", and nothing else reads it.

## The checklist, and why most templates should not have one

`Farm Task Template Checklist Item` is a child table: an item name, a `required`
flag, an evidence hint, a sort order.

It is **optional and usually right to omit**. A detector test is a checklist;
"renew the certificate before it lapses" is not. A one-item list saying *do the
task* is a form people learn to tick without reading — and once that habit is
formed, the required flag stops meaning anything on every template. The tool
says so in a warning when you author a template without one.

Where there is one, it is snapshotted onto the task as `checklist_status` and
**`complete_farm_task` refuses a completion with a required item unticked**, by
name, before any compliance record is written:

```
FT-2026-08-00014 cannot be completed: 2 required checklist item(s) are
not marked done.
  - Every smoke detector pressed and heard
  - Every CO detector pressed and heard
```

The checklist is checked **before** the evidence contract, and the order is
deliberate: an unticked required item is a statement that part of the *work* was
not done, and the evidence contract is about what the work *produced*. Telling
somebody their photograph is missing when the real answer is that they never
tested the CO detector sends them back for the wrong thing.

An **optional** item left undone does not refuse. That is what optional means,
and it is what keeps a template that covers more than today needs usable: the
propane check on a cabin with no propane is left rather than falsely ticked.

A tick naming an item the task does not hold is **refused rather than ignored**.
A typo that silently marks nothing looks exactly like a tick right up until the
completion is refused for an item the worker believes they ticked — and the
second refusal names a different item from the one they got wrong, which is the
worst possible place to spend somebody's afternoon.

## `producer_task_template` was repointed, and it had no behaviour to lose

`Compliance Rule.producer_task_template` linked to Inspection Template from
v0.22.0 to v0.40.0. In all that time **nothing on the dispatch side ever read
it**: multi-section visits are raised by `_bundle_into_sessions`, which matches a
template's sections against the compliance records a place's pending alerts are
asking for, deterministically, and never consults this column. It was a field
with a description and no behaviour.

It now names a Farm Task Template, and it has behaviour for the first time. Where
a rule names one, **the template is the whole recipe** and the rule's inline
`producer_farm_task_type`, `producer_skill_required` and `evidence_contract_json`
are not read at all. Those three stay as the fallback for a rule with no
template, which is most of them.

`generate_tasks_from_compliance_alerts` therefore resolves a recipe in three
steps, in this order:

1. **The rule's Farm Task Template**, where it has one. An explicit statement by
   whoever authored the rule that this work has a defined shape somewhere else
   has to outrank a table this app wrote before the operation existed.
2. **`ALERT_TASK_MAP`.** The thirteen shipped rules produce exactly the tasks
   they produced in v0.22.1.
3. **The rule's own inline producer fields.** The case the table cannot cover,
   because the rule did not exist when the table was written.

The alert still supplies what only it knows: the severity that becomes urgency,
the place the work happens, and its own message — which goes on the task **after**
the template's standing instructions, because a worker needs "how this job is
done" before "what is wrong with this particular cabin".

Resolving step 1 costs **one query for the whole sweep**, not four per alert. On
a site where no rule names a template it is an empty dict and every alert takes
exactly the path it took before this release.

### The patch clears rather than converts

`repoint_producer_task_template` finds every rule still holding an Inspection
Template docname, clears the column, and prints each one by name with what it
held. It has to: a Link column whose target DocType changed will refuse the next
save of any row holding an old value, so a rule an operator set — and the value
never did anything — would become a rule nobody can edit, with an error naming a
DocType they never mentioned.

It clears rather than converts because there is no honest automatic translation
from a four-section visit to a single task shape. Picking one section would be
this app inventing an operator's intent and then generating work from the
invention.

Every Inspection Template stays on the site, every session worked from one stays
readable, and the bundling that raises them is unchanged.

## Five seeded templates, and nothing wired

`bench migrate` seeds:

| Template | Produces | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin Habitability Inspection | Housing Inspection | `housing_inspection_overdue` |
| Smoke Detector Test | Detector Test | `housing_detector_test_stale` |
| Water Quality Test | Water Test | `water_test_stale` |
| Certification Renewal | — | `certification_expiring` |
| Training Record | — | `training_expiring` |

Every task type, skill, duration, dispatch mode and evidence contract on the
first three matches `ALERT_TASK_MAP` **to the letter**, and a test asserts the
equality. That is the backward-compatibility guarantee stated as an assertion:
pointing a shipped rule at its template raises exactly the task it raised in
v0.16.0, plus a checklist. The day somebody edits one table and not the other,
the build says so — otherwise wiring a rule up would quietly change what a worker
is asked to bring back.

**Seeding wires no rule.** `producer_task_template` is left exactly as it was on
every rule, so an upgrade changes no task any sweep produces. Pointing a rule at
a template is a deliberate act by somebody who has read what the template asks
for:

```
update_compliance_rule
  {"rule": "housing_detector_test_stale",
   "producer_task_template": "Smoke Detector Test"}
```

An upgrade that silently changed the shape of the work a compliance calendar
dispatches is the one thing this app will not do.

**Seeded, not fixtured.** `test_hooks.py` forbids the word by name, for the
reason it always has: a Frappe fixture is imported by `bench migrate` with no
ability to skip what a site has since edited. The seeder checks by template name
across every row and creates only what is not there — an operator who added an
item to the detector checklist keeps it, and one who disabled a template their
operation does not run keeps it disabled.

## The five tools

| Tool | Switch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `create_farm_task_template` | OFF | Define the shape of one recurring job |
| `update_farm_task_template` | OFF | Edit it — in place, reaching future tasks only |
| `list_farm_task_templates` | ON | The register |
| `get_farm_task_template` | ON | One in full, with its checklist and what it has raised |
| `create_task_from_template` | OFF | Raise a task pre-filled from one |

`evidence_required` is **mandatory** on a template, for exactly the reason it is
mandatory on a task: a template with no contract raises tasks with no contract,
`create_farm_task` refuses those, and the failure would land in front of whoever
is stood in the cabin rather than in front of whoever wrote the template.

A checklist of bare strings is accepted and means *required, no evidence, in this
order* — the commonest checklist anybody writes through a chat client, and making
them type four keys per item to get the obvious defaults is how a checklist ends
up not being written at all.

`create_task_from_template` takes three overrides — `location`, `assigned_to` and
`urgency` — because those are the three things that are true of the **case**
rather than of the job. Everything else comes off the template.

**There is no `delete_farm_task_template.`** A template that has raised work is
the answer to "what did this job ask for last season", and every task raised from
one links back to it. `enabled=false` retires one while keeping all of that
readable — the same doctrine `deactivate_inspection_template` and Training Type's
`active` flag follow.

## What is refused, and what deliberately is not

**Refused at authoring time**, while the person who can fix it is looking at what
they typed: a missing or empty evidence contract; a contract whose every
requirement is false; a misspelt evidence key, because `{"photo": true}` asks for
nothing and looks like it asks for something; a task type the Farm Task Select
does not hold; a template name already taken; two checklist items with one name,
because the name is the key a completion marks; an unnamed checklist item; a
negative duration.

**Not refused, on purpose:**

* A `creates_record` naming a DocType this site has not got. This app installs
  doctypes over time and an operation may template work against a record a later
  release adds; refusing here would make a template unsaveable today for a
  spelling that will be right in a month. The refusal that matters is at **task**
  creation, where somebody is about to be sent somewhere — and it is there.
* A template with no checklist. Most should have none.
* A checklist with no required item. A list of optional prompts is a legitimate
  aide-mémoire, and refusing it would push operators into marking everything
  required to get the record saved, which is how a required flag stops meaning
  anything.
