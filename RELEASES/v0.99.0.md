# v0.99.0 — the break horn reaches the crew, not just the foreman

Wave 4 of `fafo_ios/SERVER_CHANGES.md` — item 16. One doctype, two mobile routes,
two tools, one service module, and a trigger on the two break methods.

---

## The one idea

`BreakAlarm` on the handset plays a tone the instant a foreman calls a break —
over an `AVAudioSession` in `.playback`, so it **rings through the silent
switch**, deliberately, because a break horn that respects a muted phone does not
work on most of a crew's phones.

That is exactly one phone.

```
  before                            after
  ─────────────────────────────     ─────────────────────────────
  foreman taps "Break"              foreman taps "Break"
    → tone on the foreman's phone     → tone on the foreman's phone
    → everyone else: a shout          → APNs push to every handset
                                        on the shift, same .caf,
                                        same code path, through Focus
```

---

## The device is the identity; the token is not

This is the whole shape of the doctype, and getting it the other way round is the
obvious-looking mistake.

Apple issues a **new** device token when the app is reinstalled, when a phone is
restored from a backup, when it migrates to new hardware, and periodically for no
reason the client is told. iOS calls `register_push_token` on every login and
every launch.

A register keyed on the token would hold one live row and five dead ones per
handset by August, and every crew push would be five wasted requests and one
delivery — with nothing in the register to say which was which.

So **Mobile Push Token** is keyed on `platform::device_id`:

| field | why |
|---|---|
| `device_key` | `platform::device_id`, **unique**, written by the controller. One column because a Frappe DocType cannot declare a unique index over two; controller-written because a key a caller could spell differently is not a key. |
| `token` | mutable. The upsert writes whatever the phone is presenting today. |
| `employee` | what a crew push joins on. Never blanked by a re-registration — a launch arriving before the Employee link existed would otherwise erase the link a previous launch established. |
| `is_active` | retired on logout, and by Apple's word. **Nothing is deleted.** |
| `last_error` | Apple's own reason. "This phone stopped receiving, on this date, for this reason" is the fact somebody needs when a worker says the horn never reaches them. |

## Neither route takes a subject from the body

A phone enrols **itself**. `user` and `employee` are resolved from the caller's
own login and are not arguments at all, so `routes.bind` has nothing to drop.

A body that could name an employee would be a way to have a colleague's break
horns, heat alerts and dispatch pings delivered to a handset of your choosing.
The same shape the three direct-deposit routes have, and asserted the same way —
by **inspecting the signature**, which is the only form of that claim a later
edit cannot quietly falsify.

`unregister_push_token` does not accept a `token` either, and that absence is
load-bearing rather than tidy: a logout that had to present the current token
would fail exactly when it matters most, and a phone whose token Apple rotated
between login and logout would go on receiving another shift's break horns
forever.

## A crew break pushes; an individual break does not

`log_shift_break` and `end_shift_break` push when `applies_to` is `Crew`, to
every worker **still on** the shift.

* A break covering one named worker is not news to the other nineteen.
* A worker who has clocked out is excluded — their phone is elsewhere, and a tone
  that rings through a silent switch is not a thing to send to somebody who went
  home.
* The foreman may hear the end-of-break bell twice: the app schedules its own
  local `UNNotificationRequest` and the push arrives as well. That is the right
  way round, since the alternative is nineteen workers hearing it never.

```json
{"aps": {"alert": {"title": "Break time", "body": "Shade Break starting now — 15 minutes."},
         "sound": "break_start.caf",
         "interruption-level": "time-sensitive",
         "category": "FARM_BREAK"},
 "break_kind": "Shade Break", "phase": "start",
 "shift": "SHIFT-2026-0001", "event": "fd62d6c548001"}
```

The custom keys sit **beside** `aps` and not inside it: Apple owns that object and
will one day add a key this app has already used for something else.

`interruption-level: time-sensitive` is the point of the payload. A break horn is
worthless if Focus or a Scheduled Summary holds it until lunchtime.

## It degrades to nothing, and that is the design

The p8 signing key is an operator artefact that does not exist on this bench yet.
The break record is the compliance evidence under OAR 437-004-1131.

**The first must never be able to cost the second.** So:

* Nothing in the send path raises. Every entry point returns a report.
* An unconfigured site is a **named skip** — `reason: "not_configured"`, with the
  four missing `site_config.json` keys in the Error Log. Not an exception, not a
  hang, not a silent zero. "No key on this bench" and "sent to nobody because the
  crew has no tokens" are different problems with different people to go and see.
* `httpx` and `cryptography` are imported defensively. APNs is HTTP/2-only and
  `requests` speaks HTTP/1.1; this app does not add a dependency to ship a
  feature the site cannot use yet, so their absence is a named skip too.
* The trigger reports rather than throws, and `crew` and `tokens` are separate
  numbers — eight workers with two tokens is six people who never enrolled a
  phone, which is a different conversation from a push that failed.

```json
{"shift": "SHIFT-2026-0001", "crew": 8, "tokens": 2,
 "sent": 0, "failed": 0, "skipped": 2, "reason": "not_configured"}
```

## Apple's word retires a token, and nothing else does

| Apple says | what happens |
|---|---|
| `Unregistered`, `BadDeviceToken`, `DeviceTokenNotForTopic` | the row is deactivated on the spot |
| `TopicDisallowed`, `TooManyRequests`, the 5xxs | **failed, not deactivated** |

The second row is the one that matters. `TopicDisallowed` says the bundle id in
`site_config.json` is wrong — it is true of **every** handset on the farm, and
deactivating on it would silence the whole crew over one line an operator can fix
in a minute.

---

## The signature is 64 raw bytes and not DER

`cryptography` signs ES256 to DER. Handing Apple the DER blob produces a
perfectly well-formed JWT that it rejects with `InvalidProviderToken` — every
push failing, with nothing on this side to show for it.

The suite signs a real provider token rather than stubbing one, and
`len(signature) == 64` is the only place that difference is visible without an
Apple developer account.

## What an operator does about it

```bash
bench --site <site> migrate          # creates Mobile Push Token
bench restart                        # the farmops sidecar picks up the routes
```

Then, in the site's `site_config.json` — **not** on a Settings doctype, which any
Desk role can read and a dozen Frappe debug paths dump in full:

```json
{"apns_key": "/home/frappe/keys/AuthKey_ABC1234567.p8",
 "apns_key_id": "ABC1234567",
 "apns_team_id": "TEAM123456",
 "apns_topic": "farm.fafo.scanclock",
 "apns_environment": "production"}
```

All four are required together — a push missing any one is rejected with a 403
that says nothing useful, which is a worse failure than not sending. `apns_key`
takes either the PEM text or a path to it.

`apns_environment` is explicit rather than inferred because a token minted against
sandbox comes back `BadDeviceToken` from production, which reads exactly like a
stale token and is not one.

To prove it: switch `allow_send_test_push` on, and call it at a worker who has
logged into the app once.

**Until the key is there, every break is logged exactly as it was before.** A site
that never configures APNs behaves in every respect as it did in v0.98.0.

## The tests

68 new, in `tests_standalone/test_push.py`. Ten guards were each proved by
**removing them and watching exactly their own test go red** — no cascades, one
mutation to one failure:

| removed | caught by |
|---|---|
| the upsert (always insert) | `test_the_same_device_launching_again_updates_the_row_it_already_has` |
| the `configured` gate | `test_an_unconfigured_site_never_reaches_the_transport` |
| `reason in DEAD_TOKEN_REASONS` | `test_a_configuration_failure_does_not_unsubscribe_the_crew` |
| the empty-crew guard | `test_an_empty_crew_issues_no_query_at_all` |
| `applies_to == "Crew"` | `test_an_individual_break_rings_nobody` |
| the `left_at` exclusion | `test_a_worker_who_clocked_out_is_not_sent_to` |
| raw r\|\|s (DER instead) | `test_the_headers_carry_the_topic_a_provider_token_and_the_urgent_priority` |
| the logout ownership check | `test_one_login_cannot_retire_another_login_s_handset` |
| two distinct `.caf` names | `test_the_two_tones_are_never_the_same_file` |
| the wrapper's absent `employee` | `test_the_wrapper_declares_no_subject_at_all` |

`test_an_empty_crew_issues_no_query_at_all` is the one worth naming. Frappe reads
`{"in": []}` as no filter at all on some backends — which would turn a crew of
nobody into a push to the whole farm, the single worst outcome this module could
produce. It is asserted by patching `frappe.db.get_all` and requiring that it was
never called.

Full suite green: **10,961** in an isolated HEAD+this-change tree.

## What is outstanding

**The live half.** There is no bench on the machine this was written on, so
nothing here has sent a push to a real device. `bench migrate`, restart the
sidecar, install the p8 key, then `send_test_push`.

**The iOS half.** `MobileAPI.swift` does not name either route yet — both sit in
`PENDING_IOS_INTEGRATION` rather than in `MOBILE`, and `test_ios_contract`
transcribes no mirror for a Codable that does not exist. Writing one here would
invent the contract rather than copy it. The server half is published first so
the app side is a client change rather than a release of both.
