# v0.99.0 — the afternoon, and the people who were in it

Wave 3 of `fafo_ios/SERVER_CHANGES.md` — items 4, 15, 17 and 23, "Compliance &
Safety + Bin Sealing".

Wave 1 was seven instances of one failure: the phone and the server disagreeing
about a word. This is not that. Three of these four are places where the server
**knew something and threw it away at the moment it mattered**, and the fourth is
a register that did not exist at all.

---

## The one idea

Every item here is about an *afternoon* — a span of hours, in a place, with named
people in it — and about a fact that is only knowable while it is happening.

```
  the server knew            and lost it at             what that costs
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  date_of_birth on          every roster call          a fifteen-year-old on a
  Employee                                             ten-hour day, lawfully
                                                       recorded

  the shift's peak          the break row              "was relief provided in
  heat index                                           time" is a join over

                                                       performs

  eleven people need        eleven Farm Tasks          no cohort on the record
  one course                                           and no attendance sheet
                                                       at the end of it

  whose buckets are         the trailer leaving        a residue trace that
  in this bin               the orchard                stops at the tag
```

The last row is the one that cannot be recovered at all. The other three are
recoverable in principle and never in practice, which for a compliance record is
the same thing.

## 15 — minors

### `is_minor` is derived and stored nowhere

There is no `is_minor` column on Employee and there will not be one. A stored
flag is correct on the day somebody ticks it and wrong every day afterwards — a
fifteen-year-old hired in April is sixteen in July — and it goes wrong in the
direction that **permits more work**. `date_of_birth` is a fact that does not
move; the answer is computed from it against the day being asked about, which is
why every function in the new `erpnext_mcp/minors.py` takes an `on_date`.

The roster answers as of the **shift's own day**, not today. A shift read in
November is being read about the afternoon it happened, and a picker who turned
eighteen in September was seventeen when the crew was formed.

### It is three-valued

| answer | means |
|---|---|
| `true` | under eighteen on the day asked about |
| `false` | eighteen or over |
| `null` | **no date of birth on file** |

"We do not know" and "they are an adult" are different answers. A boolean column
could not have said so, and a module that collapsed them would clear a minor onto
a ten-hour shift because a column was empty. Every refusal path treats `null` as
a **finding about the record** rather than as a finding about the person — it is
reported to the foreman at the roster call, which is the only moment somebody is
looking at the right screen to fix it.

### Two bands, because the law has two

| band | day | week | clock | citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| under-16 | 8 h | 40 h | 07:00–19:00 | ORS 653.315 / OAR 839-021-0220; 29 CFR 570.35 |
| 16-17 | 10 h | 60 h | none | OAR 839-021-0104 |

Treating "minor" as one category would either forbid lawful work for the older
band or permit unlawful work for the younger. There is no time-of-day limit on a
sixteen-year-old in agriculture, and inventing one would be this app writing a
rule stricter than the regulation — which teaches a foreman to route around it.

The daily figures are **non-school-day** figures, deliberately. A school-day
limit is three hours, and applying it would need this app to know each worker's
school calendar. The refusal names the figure it used.

### Refuse where a refusal costs a name; report where it costs the record

`add_worker_to_shift` **refuses**. `start_shift` **reports**. Same arithmetic,
and the asymmetry is the argument rather than an inconsistency:

* `add_worker_to_shift` is about **one named person**, and the shift goes on
  existing without them. A refusal costs a name off a crew, and what it buys is
  the thing a note cannot — these are limits on what may lawfully be
  **scheduled**, and a foreman told afterwards that the afternoon was unlawful
  has been told too late.
* `start_shift` forms a crew that is **standing in the block**. A server that
  would not open a shift because one fifteen-year-old is over their weekly hours
  produces *no record of the afternoon at all*, which is worse evidence than a
  record carrying the finding.

### The hours come off the crew rows, not off Attendance

`bridge_to_attendance` writes one Attendance row per crew member **when the shift
closes**. So on the afternoon somebody is being rostered onto a second crew, the
Attendance register says nothing about the morning they have already worked —
which is exactly the moment a child-labour hour check is being asked. Crew rows
exist from the moment the shift is formed, so `shifts.hours_worked_by` reads
those, and counts an open shift **to now**.

### What a minor may not be sent to

```
  Spray     both bands    40 CFR §170.309(c) — the WPS minimum age of 18 for
                          pesticide handlers and early-entry workers. An AGE
                          BAR, not a training gap: no course closes it.

  Repair    under-16      29 CFR §570.71(a) — the Hazardous Occupations Orders
                          in Agriculture. A 16- or 17-year-old lawfully runs a
                          tractor on a farm in Oregon, which is why the older
                          band is not on this row.
```

Two entries out of eleven task types. It is short on purpose: a list that also
carried "probably unwise" would be a list a foreman learns to override, and
`assign_farm_task` has **no override argument** beside this one — unlike the
pre-harvest interval, which a licensed applicator may lawfully shorten. Nobody
can consent a sixteen-year-old into being a lawful pesticide handler.

### The break schedule a minor counts from

`Labor Break Policy` gains `minor_rest_schedule`, `minor_meal_schedule` and
`minor_max_hours_without_rest`. OAR 839-021-0072 owes a worker under eighteen a
rest every **two** hours and a meal every **four**, against four and six for an
adult — a different table rather than a multiplier.

**The fallback direction is the design.** A policy with no minor rows computes a
minor's entitlement from the **adult** schedule, which owes FEWER periods and
therefore shows up as a shortfall the moment anybody reads the shift. The
opposite fallback — "no minor rows, no extra obligation" — would report a
fifteen-year-old as fully rested on a policy nobody had finished filling in.

Nothing is ever seeded into a policy. `Labor Break Policy` carries
`human_approved_by` because a break schedule is a statement this operation makes
about what it owes its crew, and an app that wrote rows in on `bench migrate`
would move that statement with nobody's name on it. `get_break_policy` returns
the OAR rows **beside** a policy that lacks them, marked `approved: false`, so
the gap is visible on the handset and the operator has the table to paste.

`get_break_schedule` — wave 2's endpoint, landed hours before this one — now
takes an `employee` and computes that person's own instants. The whole argument
for computing a countdown on the server is that seven phones count to the same
second, and that fails immediately if one of the seven is a minor whose schedule
the server does not know about.

### `minor_hours_approaching`

The eighteenth shipped rule, and the first whose subject is a person's age. It
fires within four hours of the weekly ceiling (Warning) and past it (Critical),
counted off the crew rows of every shift including the one still running.

It ships with **no task recipe and an explicit rectification refusal**. What
answers it is a rostering decision about tomorrow — do not put this person on the
crew, or send them home — and a Farm Task reading "do not schedule somebody" is a
card nobody can complete and evidence of nothing. It clears by itself when the
workweek turns.

## 17 — a heat break carries the heat

The Farm Shift controller has stamped every compliance event with the reading
current **at its own instant** since v0.19.4, bounded to half an hour. That is
the conditions the foreman was *standing in*. What the record could not say is
the conditions the break was *called about*.

```
  a cool-down at 16:10, on an afternoon that peaked at 97 °F at 11:00

    weather_snapshot_heat_index_f   88.0   ← what it was at 16:10
    peak_heat_index_f               97.0   ← what the shift had done
    threshold_crossed_at            11:00  ← when the obligation attached
```

OAR 437-004-1131 attaches its obligations at the **crossing**, and the question
an inspector asks is whether relief was provided in time. A register carrying
only the snapshot cannot answer it, and the join that reconstructs it — ninety
readings, per break, per shift — is the one nobody performs.

So a break logged as Cool-Down, Water Break or Shade Break now carries
`peak_temp_f`, `peak_heat_index_f`, `threshold_crossed_at`, `weather_source` and
a derived `heat_obligation`. **All five are keyed on the break KIND**, not on the
presence of a timeline: a Paid Rest on the same shift carries none of them, which
is what makes the flag mean something.

`heat_obligation` is derived from `break_kind` and never taken from a body — the
same rule that keeps `event_type` off `log_shift_break`'s signature. A phone that
could set it directly could file an unpaid meal as a cool-down in the one
register that has to answer -1131 honestly.

The read side carries the temperature under **both** `temp_f` (what every
existing reader uses) and `ambient_temp_f` (what the handset's payload calls it),
from one column, so they cannot drift — which is the class of failure v0.96.0 was
seven instances of.

A shift with no weather timeline gets **blank** columns and a sentence saying so.
Nobody measured, which is not a temperature.

## 4 — one afternoon, not eleven cards

`training_expiring` raises one alert per Employee Training Record. A crew of
eleven whose heat-illness training all lapses in the same fortnight produced
eleven Compliance-Audit tasks, each reading "arrange and deliver the retraining"
— eleven cards for one delivery, no cohort anywhere on the record, and an
attendance sheet that has to be assembled afterwards from eleven separately
closed tasks. iOS has had `TrainingSessionRunner` and `AttendanceSignatureView`
since before this release with nothing raised for them to run.

A `group_training` Check on Training Type (and on Farm Task Template, for a
producer recipe pointed at a template rather than a curriculum) decides it. The
rule, in full:

1. `training_expiring` alerts only, whose source record names a Training Type.
2. Only where that Training Type is ticked. An applicator licence and a forklift
   certification are one-to-one items and stay on the unchanged per-alert path.
3. Grouped by `(company, training_type)` — two entities are two sessions.
4. **Two or more people.** One person needing a retraining is one task; a session
   for a single attendee is a heavier document answering a lighter question.
5. Anything already answered takes the whole curriculum out of the running, so a
   second delivery cannot overlap the first.

The session is created **Scheduled**, with everybody listed and nobody marked
`attended`. Listing somebody who is going to be retrained is a roster; ticking it
would be a claim they turned up, made before the afternoon happened.
`complete_training_session` is still the only call that writes a training record,
which is what makes it safe to raise one of these off a sweep at two in the
morning.

Idempotency is by `source_alerts` on the session — the mechanism
`Inspection Session` already uses, because a task carries one `source_alert` and
a session answers several.

`materialize_task_for_alert` does **not** bundle. It answers one docname by
design: a tap on a phone names the alert somebody is looking at, not a filter
that might catch a coworker's. What it owes them is the sentence, and it now
carries one.

## 23 — bin sealing

### What breaks without it

A bin leaves the orchard on a trailer carrying a **tag** and nothing else.

| the question at the packing house | what it needs |
|---|---|
| a residue detection | which block, and was that block inside a re-entry interval |
| a piece-rate dispute | whose buckets, and how many each |
| a food-safety hold | which *other* bins the same crew filled that afternoon |
| a heat-exposure question | which shift, and so which weather timeline and break log |

Every one of them is a join from a tag to a shift to a crew, and **none of them
is reconstructable once the crew has gone home**. The buckets are tipped and
mixed; the badge scans exist only on the handset; the tag is the single surviving
identifier and it points at nothing.

### The register

`Bin Seal` — standalone, not a child table of Bucket Session, for the reason
`Shift Location Log` is not a child table of the shift: a bin is sealed by a
**checker** whose phone is not editing anybody's session, several sessions tip
into one bin, and one session fills many bins over a day.

`Bin Seal Contributor` holds the workers whose badges were scanned since the last
seal, with the buckets attributed to each and the window they were tipping.
**This table is the feature**; everything else on the record could be
reconstructed from a shift and a tag, and this could not be reconstructed from
anything.

### The tag is not unique and is not made so

Bin tags are reused — between seasons, between growers, between the two hundred
bins a packing house owns and lends out. A uniqueness constraint would refuse the
**second true record** rather than the first false one: the second seal is a real
bin that was really closed, thrown away to protect an assumption about somebody
else's stickers.

So `trace_bin` answers with **every** seal carrying the tag, newest first, and
`matches` says how many. A packing house holding a bin whose tag traces to three
seals has a real problem and now knows it has one; the alternative was a
confident answer about the wrong afternoon.

What **is** unique is `client_event_id`, the handset's own identifier for one
sealing action. A phone that sealed a bin and did not hear the answer sends the
same call again — over a funnel, in a canyon, on a dying battery this is the
ordinary case — and a second record of one bin is a doubled count at the pack
line and a doubled piece rate on somebody's cheque. A retry gets the same seal
back with `already_sealed: true`.

### The two counts are allowed to disagree

`bucket_count` is what the checker's tally read when the bin was closed. The
contributors' `buckets_contributed` is what the badge scans attributed. This app
**never reconciles them**, because a bucket tipped by somebody whose badge did
not scan is *in the bin and not in the rows* — and that is precisely the fact a
piece-rate dispute turns on. Balancing them would delete it.
`unattributed_buckets` names the difference on every read.

A badge that resolves to nobody is **reported and the bin is still sealed**. A
record with a gap in it is worth more than no record, and a bin refused over an
unregistered card is a bin nothing can trace at all.

### The surface

| | |
|---|---|
| `POST /farmops/api/mobile/seal_bin` | what `PieceTallyViewModel.sealBin(tag:)` posts |
| `seal_bin` | MUTATING, default **off** |
| `get_bin_seal` | one seal in full, contributors included |
| `list_bin_seals` | by shift, field, block, tag, session, checker or day |
| `trace_bin` | **tag → the people who filled it** |

`company` and `source` are absent from the mobile wrapper's signature, so the
route table's argument filter makes them unreachable rather than merely refused.
The first would let a phone file another farm's harvest against this one's crew;
the second would let a handset pass a typed record off as a scanned one.

## Two live bugs, both found by writing the tests

**`get_shift` was unreachable from a handset.** It resolved its docname on
`("name", "name", "farm_shift")` — `shift` was never consulted, though the
registry advertises it as an alias and `api/mobile.get_shift` passes exactly that
key. Every call a phone made to the shift read answered `farm_shift is required`.
It is the identical failure v0.96.0 fixed on `end_shift` (item 1) at a different
door, which is why the spellings are now named once as `SHIFT_KEYS`.

**`minors_on_crew` was two keys of one name.** `shifts.describe` sets it to a
count and `start_shift` was setting it to a list of findings — a payload whose
shape depends on which line ran last, which is the shape wave 1 was seven
instances of. The detail is `minor_crew_findings` now.

## Tests

83 in `tests_standalone/test_wave3_compliance_binseal.py`, seven claims with a
negative control on each — the negative controls are load-bearing here rather
than decorative:

* an adult with the same nine hours **is** rostered, which is what proves the
  gate is about age rather than about hours;
* an unticked curriculum still raises one task per person, which is what proves
  the `group_training` flag does the work;
* a Paid Rest carries none of the heat columns, which is what proves the stamp is
  keyed on the kind rather than on the timeline;
* a second seal with no `client_event_id` **is** a second bin, which is what
  proves the idempotency is on the event and never on the tag.

Every one was checked by mutation: breaking the band selection, the heat stamp,
the training bundler, the task bar, the hour gate or `trace_bin`'s ambiguity
handling fails these tests and nothing else.

Full suite **10,976 green**.

## Migration

Four doctype JSONs change and every change is additive; two doctypes are new.
This is a `bench migrate` and nothing else. No patch ships and none is needed —
a break policy with no minor rows behaves exactly as it did, an existing break
row has blank heat columns and reads as it always has, and a Training Type with
`group_training` unticked takes the path it took before.

Four new tools means four new `allow_*` switches: `get_bin_seal`,
`list_bin_seals` and `trace_bin` ship **on**, `seal_bin` ships **off**. No
operator's stored value is stranded, because none of the four names existed
before.

**The live half is outstanding and belongs to a deploy.** There is no bench on
the machine this was written on, so nothing here was called over HTTP: `bench
migrate`, restart the farmops sidecar, then POST `seal_bin` with a tag and a
count and read a docname back, and POST `get_shift` with `shift` in the body and
read a shift back instead of `farm_shift is required`.
