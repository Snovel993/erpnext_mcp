# v0.104.0 — the whole Company Details section, on the card

Tim, expanding the badge brief: the Employee form's **Company Details** section
is where a farm already records where somebody sits, and the badge should show
it — designation, department, branch and who they report to — alongside the
cabin.

**This corrects v0.103.0 rather than extending it.**

---

## What v0.103.0 got wrong

That release read `reports_to`, `department` and `branch` as a **fallback
chain**: the first one set won, and it printed on one line as "the crew".

```
  v0.103.0                          v0.104.0
  ────────────────────────────      ──────────────────────────────
  reports_to  ─┐                    designation  → printed
  department  ─┼→ "Crew: …"         department   → printed
  branch      ─┘   (one line)       branch       → printed
                                    reports_to   → printed
```

It was a reasonable way to fill a single line and it is the wrong model of the
farm. Those are **three different facts about one person**, and collapsing them
meant a picker in Harvest at Mill Creek who reports to Ramirez printed one of the
three and hid the other two — silently, with nothing failing.

`_company_details` now reads the section whole and every field comes back
separately.

---

## The crew is a different question, and keeps a different answer

Tim, on his own org model: workers *"connect with foremen for enforcement (a
foreman may not be their direct report-to but aids in oversight)"*.

That is two relationships, not one:

| | what it is | where it lives | on the card? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reporting line** | the formal chain of command | `Employee.reports_to` | **yes** |
| **Oversight** | whoever is running the crew you are on today | the open `Farm Shift` | no |

So `_crew` was **narrowed**. It is now only ever the open shift's foreman, it no
longer stands in for `reports_to`, and it stays **off the printed card** — a
shift is one morning's roster and a card that laminated `SHIFT-2026-0114`'s
foreman would be wrong by Wednesday. It is still returned in the data, because a
handset asking "who is overseeing this badge right now" is asking a real question
with a today-shaped answer.

The chain *above* the supervisor is `shadow_log.raci_chain`, not a second walk.
That function already climbs `reports_to`, already caps the climb, already
survives a cycle by reporting it, and already names the rungs — *direct
supervisor, manager, owner*, which is the ladder Tim describes. It comes back as
`reports_to_chain` and is not printed: a CR-80 has room for the person somebody
escalates to, and the rest of the ladder is a thing a handset shows.

## The layout, and the eight millimetres that paid for it

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ▓▓ Example Trading Co             EMPLOYEE BADGE│
  │  ┌────────┐  Ana Reyes                    ┌─────┐│
  │  │        │  Picker                       │ ▞▚▞ ││
  │  │  photo │  Harvest                      │ ▚▞▚ ││
  │  │        │                               └─────┘│
  │  └────────┘  BADGE                        ETC-0001│
  │              ETC-0001                             │
  │  Reports to: Rosa Ramirez        Mill Creek Ranch │
  │  Camp: Mill Creek · Cabin 7                       │
  │  No. E-100                            HR-EMP-00001│
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The designation and the department stay in the column between the photograph and
the QR, where the job title already was. The reporting line, the branch and the
cabin go in a **full-width detail band** below both.

That band is dead space the card already had. The photograph ends at 38.5mm, the
QR caption at 38.5mm and the badge ID at 39mm; the footer rule is at 47.4mm —
**eight millimetres of full card width with nothing on it.**

Using it is what makes these lines legible. `Reports to: Rosa Ramirez` is
twenty-four characters and does not fit the 32mm column at a size anybody reads
across a bin trailer. Two such strings do not fit one line either, which is why
the branch takes the right half of the first row and the cabin — the longest
string on the card — takes the whole of the second.

**Only the two lines that need a label get one.** `Camp:` and `Reports to:` say
what the value *is*; a department and a branch read as themselves, and a label on
each would spend four millimetres saying what nobody was confused about.

Everything else about the geometry is unchanged and still holds: fixed slots
rather than a stack that closes up (the sheet lays out in Python and the Print
Format in Jinja, and a conditional stack would need the same arithmetic twice and
would drift), and every line clips rather than wraps.

## Grade and employment type

Returned in the data, **not printed**. Tim named them as fields on the section
and did not ask for them in the layout, and a CR-80 has no room for a fact nobody
requested. They are free to carry, so they are carried.

---

## Value Chain and Supply Chain

Tim describes the distinction — **Value Chain** as staff (management, admin),
**Supply Chain** as the front-line workers doing the job — as context for why the
reporting line matters.

**Nothing was built for it, deliberately.** No field on Employee carries that
classification, and deriving it from `designation`, `grade` or `department` would
be this app guessing at a distinction only Tim can make, then printing the guess
on an ID card. If it should appear on a badge it needs somewhere to live first —
most naturally an Employee Grade, or a field on the Company Details section — and
that is a decision rather than an implementation detail.

---

## Nothing may lose a badge

Unchanged from v0.103.0 and now covering five lookups instead of two. A site that
has not migrated the camp register, a worker who reports to nobody, an HR app
whose Employee has no `reports_to` — each is a card printed without that line,
never a badge nobody can issue.

`render/badge_card.py` calls all three resolvers inside a guard of their own and
filters its Employee column list through the doctype meta first, so a missing
column cannot cost the card its photograph. `test_badge_print_format.py` proves
it for each resolver in turn.

## Tests, and one that was not testing anything

`TheCardSaysWhereSomebodyBelongs` is rewritten around the corrected model: all
four Company Details on one card at once (the test v0.103.0's fallback chain
would have failed), the supervisor printed as a name and not a docname, the chain
walked and named, the crew narrowed to the shift, and the negative control that
`reports_to` no longer answers the crew question.

**A scoping bug in the print-format negative controls was found and fixed.** They
asserted against the whole rendered page — which includes the `<style>` block —
and `CARD_CSS` carries comments that quote the very labels the markup prints, as
well as every class name. `assertNotIn("Reports to:", html)` would have passed
only until somebody documented the line. The negative controls now read the
markup after `</style>`, which is what "the line was not drawn" actually means.

The geometry check is extended: the column stack, the detail band and the footer
rule are now one arithmetic chain, and the two cells that share a row are checked
for horizontal clearance separately, since the vertical arithmetic cannot see
them.
