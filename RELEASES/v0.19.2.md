# v0.19.2 — one audit at a time, and a curriculum that knows what it answers

**Released 2026-08-03. Small doctype pass on v0.19.1.**

Two items. Both close holes that v0.19.1's debrief named and left open, and both
turn a piece of prose into a record — which is the whole shape of this release:
nothing new is computed, and two facts the app already knew stop living only in
comments.

Item 1 is the one v0.19.1 wrote down and could not do: *"`Compliance Alert` still
has no `regime` field, which is debrief item 1 and remains open."*

Item 2 is a hole v0.19.0 opened on purpose, argued for at the time, and named in
its own docstring — `training_type` as free text.

Suite: 3,601 → **3,653 passing**.

---

## 1. A calendar that can be read one audit at a time

Twelve rules produced one undifferentiated list. That is the right list for a
Tuesday and the wrong one for the morning before an inspection, when the question
is not "what do we owe anybody" but "what will OR-OSHA ask about" — and the only
way to get the second was to already know which of twelve rule names belonged to
which agency. `certification_expiring` covers eleven kinds of certificate across
five regimes; nobody memorises that.

So `Compliance Alert` gained **`regime`**, a Table MultiSelect over a new
`Compliance Regime` master. Multi-select because one alert genuinely matters to
several: an untested block in spray rotation is an FSMA Subpart E finding **and**
an OR-OSHA one, and filing it under whichever came to mind first would hide it
from the other packet.

**It is written by the sweep, never typed.** A lapsed WPS card is WPS evidence
whoever happens to be looking, so `Rule` gained a `regimes` tuple and every one of
the twelve carries one:

| Rule | Regimes | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `certification_expiring` | *per row* | Eleven kinds of certificate. An applicator licence is WPS evidence; a GlobalGAP certificate is not. |
| `policy_review_overdue` | FSMA, GAP, GlobalGAP, PrimusGFS | The three schemes that ask for an annual document review, plus 21 CFR 112's written procedures. |
| `water_test_stale` | FSMA, OR-OSHA | Subpart E is the rule; the water through the sprayer is also the field-sanitation water. |
| `housing_inspection_overdue` | OR-OSHA | OAR 437-004-1120 is the habitability walk. FSMA Subpart L is about facilities, not the annual cycle. |
| `housing_detector_test_stale` | OR-OSHA, Internal | The fire code reaches it, and most years nobody comes to look. |
| `i9_expired` | Internal | IRCA is federal immigration law. There is no I-9 packet. |
| `flc_license_expiring` | Internal | ORS 658.405 is enforced by Oregon BOLI, which is not OR-OSHA. |
| `filing_response_due` | Internal | The deadline belongs to the agency on the filing, not to a regime. |
| `audit_action_overdue` | GAP, GlobalGAP, PrimusGFS, Internal | The first thing each scheme's auditor asks about next visit. |
| `housing_corrective_action_open` | OR-OSHA, FSMA | A finding on a cabin: the camp rule and Subpart L where it is a worker facility. |
| `water_test_contamination` | FSMA | §112.44(b) is the criterion the sample failed. Narrower than the stale rule on purpose. |
| `training_expiring` | *per row* | Copied off the record, which can carry any tag in the vocabulary. |

Two of the twelve could not carry a constant, and `Observation` gained its own
`regimes` for them. `certification_expiring` reads the certificate's **type**
(falling back to its name only when the type says nothing) through an ordered
heuristic whose order is the whole content: `globalgap` and `primus` are matched
before `gap`, because "GlobalGAP" contains "GAP" and tagging it GAP would put
another scheme's certificate in front of a USDA auditor — the same substring trap
`training.matches` has guarded since v0.19.0, one layer up.

`training_expiring` copies the tags off the training record itself, which is the
only honest source. The curriculum says what the course normally answers; the
record says what **that afternoon** covered, and a heat session that ran out of
time before the emergency-response topic did not satisfy OAR 437-004-1131 that
day. An untagged record produces an untagged alert, deliberately: the message
already says so and tells the reader how to fix it, and inventing a tag so the row
is not bare would make the calendar claim coverage the register does not have.

### `Internal` is a tag, not an absence

Six of the twelve answer to no outside agency. They could have been left untagged.
They are not, because an untagged alert is invisible to every regime filter, and
*silently invisible* is the one thing a compliance calendar must not be. `Internal`
says: real work, real due date, nobody coming to inspect it — which is a claim
somebody can disagree with, and "no tag" never is.

### The narrowed sweep is the sharpest edge in the release

`refresh_compliance_alerts(regime=...)` runs only the rules that raise that
audit's evidence. A rule it skips **raises nothing and dismisses nothing** — the
same treatment a rule gets when its doctypes are absent, and for a sharper version
of the same reason. A filtered sweep that auto-dismissed the rules it did not run
would clear most of the calendar and report it as progress. `rules_run` excludes
them, `rules_skipped` names each one with why, and the result says in as many
words that its counts are about one regime only.

The two per-row rules run whenever their **union** contains the regime, so a WPS
sweep rescans every certificate and keeps the applicator licences.

### The read side refuses rather than returning nothing

`get_compliance_calendar`, `list_compliance_calendar_for_me` and
`refresh_compliance_alerts` all take `regime`, and an unrecognised value is
**refused by name**. That is the whole argument for the check: filtering on a word
nobody understood returns an empty calendar, and an empty compliance calendar
reads as a clean one — the most expensive way for this tool to be wrong.

`list_compliance_calendar_for_me` validates it **before** its per-entity loop,
where every exception becomes a named failure for one entity. Right for a broken
register; exactly wrong for a typo, which would otherwise give a worker three
"failures" and an empty calendar instead of one sentence naming the mistake.

### The audit packet grew an open-items section

`generate_audit_packet(regime=...)` now narrows two sections rather than one, and
the second is new: **Compliance calendar — open items**, scoped to the same
regimes.

This is the part most likely to be questioned, so: it is a disclosure, not a
confession. The kairotic gate has **already refused the packet** if any corrective
action from inside the period is open. What is left is forward-looking work, on a
list the operation's own records generated rather than somebody's memory the night
before — and an auditor's question is never "do you have open items", it is "do
you know what they are". It is the same argument that put `allow_open_actions`'
disclosures at the FRONT of the document rather than at the back.

It is the one section **not** scoped to the period, because an expired licence is
expired now whatever quarter the packet covers; filtering it by the packet's dates
would produce a list of what was outstanding last March, which on a properly
closed period is empty every time and reads as an operation with nothing
outstanding. Snoozed and dismissed items are excluded: a snooze is "not this week"
and a dismissal is somebody's recorded judgement, and neither is a debt.

`generate_compliance_packet(regime=...)` gained the matching annex beside the
training one it has had since v0.19.0.

## 2. `Training Type`, and half a v0.19.0 decision reversed

v0.19.0 made `training_type` free text and defended it in the doctype's own
description: a Select would have to be edited every time a regulator renames a
curriculum, and an operator faced with a list that lacks the course somebody
actually attended will pick the nearest wrong one.

**That argument is still right, and this release does not make it a Select.** It
makes it a **Link to `Training Type`** — a master anybody can add a row to, and
which `record_training` creates from free text the first time somebody files a
course this site has not run. Nothing has to be configured before a training can
be filed, which was the whole of free text's advantage.

What the link buys is what free text could not: *"WPS Handler Training satisfies
WPS"* is a fact about the **curriculum**, not about one afternoon. Under free text
it had to be restated on every record, which made thirty records of one course
thirty chances to mistype a tag — and a near-miss files evidence where no packet
looks for it, which nobody finds out about until an inspector does.

### Auto-creating a curriculum is not the same decision as auto-creating a regime

The controller refuses an unknown **regime** twenty lines from where it creates an
unknown **training type**, and the two are not inconsistent. Regimes are a closed
legal vocabulary: `OSHA` for `OR-OSHA` is a typo with a silent, expensive
consequence. Curricula are open — regulators rename them, operations invent their
own — so a name this site has not seen is far more likely to be a real new course
than a mistake. The failure modes point opposite ways, so the answers do.

The creation happens in `validate`, not in the tool, because Frappe checks Links
**after** `validate`. Doing it only in `record_training` would have covered one
caller and left the Desk form, a data import, a patch and the iOS app throwing a
link error at somebody who typed the true name of a course that genuinely
happened.

### The new curriculum takes its NAME's regimes, not the session's

A heat session a crew leader also used to cover hygiene is tagged GAP on the
record. It must not make "Heat Illness Prevention" a GAP curriculum for every
future session of it. So an auto-created type takes what its name implies, the
record keeps what was actually covered, and `record_training` says so in the
result — including when an existing curriculum and the session disagree, which is
recorded as given (a session that ran short is entitled to say so) and flagged as
worth a second look.

### The migration rewrites almost nothing

`Training Type` names itself from `training_type_name`, so the docname **is** the
text already in the column. The migration therefore creates the masters those
values were already pointing at in spirit and touches a training record only where
the text needed normalising — trailing whitespace, or a casing that would
otherwise split one curriculum across two masters.

That is the whole reason for `field:training_type_name` over a `TT-00007` series.
A serial autoname would have meant rewriting every training record on the site
inside a migration, and a migration that rewrites compliance evidence is one
somebody has to be able to prove was correct afterwards.

It **does not touch `regimes` on any existing record**. The type's regimes are a
default for records filed from now on; the records already on the site carry what
somebody tagged them with at the time, and a patch that overwrote that would be
replacing evidence with a heuristic.

It is idempotent in all three steps, and listed in `patches.txt` **and** called
from `after_migrate` — the patch entry records in the Patch Log when the
conversion first happened, the hook catches a site that upgraded straight across
this version, so it runs at least twice on any real bench and is a no-op the
second time.

### Ten curricula seeded, and not as a fixture

`PSA Grower Training`, `WPS Handler Training`, `WPS Worker Training`, `Heat
Illness Prevention`, `OSHA 10`, `OSHA 30`, `Hazard Communication`, `Applicator
License Renewal`, `NOP Handler Training`, `Food Safety Refresher`.

**Not** a Frappe `fixtures` entry, which `test_hooks.py` forbids by name: a
fixture is imported by `bench migrate` with no ability to skip what a site already
has, so an operator who corrected the regimes on a curriculum would get them
silently corrected back on the next upgrade. Both seeders check before they write
and leave an edited row alone, including one somebody deactivated.

**Their retention is derived, not stated.** `retention_years` on a seeded or
auto-created type is `training.retention_years(regimes)` — the longest tag
governs — so a seed cannot contradict the doctrine. This is one deviation from the
v0.19.2 spec, which gave `Applicator License Renewal` a retention of 2: its
OR-OSHA tag demands 3, and shipping a seed that says "keep 2 years" for a record
whose tag demands 3 would be shipping a standing instruction to destroy evidence
early. A test asserts the two agree for every seeded curriculum.

## The vocabulary did not move, and that is the point

v0.19.0's `training.py` argued at length against a `Compliance Regime` doctype:
two extra doctypes plus a seeder, against the house rule "avoid table sprawl", for
a closed list that has changed twice in fifteen years.

**That argument was right about `Employee Training Record.regimes`, and it still
holds — that column is still a delimited tag list.** It was wrong about everything
else, and this release is where that showed: two new fields needed a regime tag on
records that module does not own, the Desk cannot offer a picker over a Small
Text, and an operator tagging an alert would have been typing `OR-OSHA` from
memory into a text box.

So there are two storage shapes now and still **one definition**. `REGIMES` in
`erpnext_mcp/training.py` is the list; `seed_regimes` writes the master from it on
every migrate; `canon`, `parse`, `require` and `matches` are still the only
readers; and the child rows are converted at the boundary (`to_rows` / `from_rows`)
so nothing downstream knows which shape a given field used. A child table and a
comma-separated column that disagreed about whether a row carries WPS is precisely
the failure that module was written to prevent.

Two tokens were added, both from the alert engine rather than the training
register:

- **`OTCO`** — Oregon Tilth Certified Organic, the certifier that actually holds
  the file. NOP is the federal rule it certifies against, and a Tilth inspection
  asks for Organic-System-Plan evidence a bare NOP tag does not distinguish.
- **`Internal`** — see above.

The `tilth` alias still resolves to **NOP**, deliberately. Records written through
it since v0.19.0 are stored as NOP, and repointing it would make one word mean a
different set of rows on the read path than it wrote on the write path — a filter
that quietly stops matching its own history. `otco` is the unambiguous spelling
and is what the seeder and the heuristics use.

Four places said "the eight are" against a list that had become ten. They now call
`training.vocabulary_note()`, because a count written in prose is a second copy of
a fact the tuple already holds.

## Tool surface

**Unchanged: 214 tools, 95 read, 119 mutating.** No tool was added. Three gained a
`regime` argument (`get_compliance_calendar`,
`list_compliance_calendar_for_me`, `refresh_compliance_alerts`), two had theirs
widened to scope a second section (`generate_audit_packet`,
`generate_compliance_packet`), and `record_training` gained free-text curriculum
creation on an argument it already had.

## Upgrade

`bench --site <site> migrate`. Three new DocTypes sync, the regimes and curricula
seed, and the training-type migration runs. Nothing else is required.

**Alerts already on the site are untagged until the next sweep**, which is at most
an hour and can be forced with `refresh_compliance_alerts`. Until then a
regime-filtered calendar returns fewer rows than it will — so the unfiltered
calendar names the untagged count and says exactly that, rather than letting a
short list read as a short list of problems.

The one behaviour change to know about: **every audit packet now contains an
open-items section.** It is a disclosure and the gate still refuses a packet over
open corrective actions from inside the period, but a packet produced after this
release is not byte-identical to one produced before it.
