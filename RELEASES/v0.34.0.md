# v0.34.0 — the arithmetic becomes a form

**Three releases put the numbers in place. This one puts them on the six forms an
agricultural employer in Oregon and Washington actually files.**

Five new MCP tools (326 total: 151 read, 175 mutating). One new DocType. One new
field on an existing one. A new pure module, `form_generators.py`, on the same
contract as `payroll_calc.py`.

---

## What this release had to decide first

v0.28.0 computed federal withholding. v0.29.0 computed Oregon and Washington.
v0.30.0 turned shifts into gross pay and gross pay into a payroll slip. Between
them, every number a W-2 needs was already in the database.

**So the interesting question was never the arithmetic. It was what happens when
the arithmetic changes after somebody has filed.**

A payroll slip is a live record. Hours get fixed, a rate gets corrected, a shift
that nobody logged gets added three weeks late. A tax form is not a live record:
it is a statement an employer made to an agency, on a date, and the agency has a
copy. If `get_tax_form` recomputed from today's payroll, it would return a
different W-2 than the one in the envelope — silently, with no way for the reader
to know which of the two the IRS is holding.

So **`form_data_json` is written once at generation and read back verbatim**, and
recomputation is a separate tool behind a separate switch that reports what
moved:

```
"changes": {
  "line2_wages_tips_other_compensation": {"was": 1000.0, "now": 1500.0, "delta": 500.0}
}
```

`regenerate_tax_form` refuses a `Filed` form unless `allow_filed` is passed, and
refuses an `Amended` one outright.

## The six forms

| Form | Scope | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **W-2** | one employee, one calendar year | IRS |
| **1099-NEC** | one contractor, one calendar year | IRS |
| **941** | one company, one quarter | IRS |
| **OR-WR** | one company, one year | Oregon DOR |
| **OQ** | one company, one quarter | Oregon DOR / OED |
| **WA-ESD** | one company, one quarter | WA Employment Security |

Q4's federal and Oregon deadlines fall on **31 January of the following year** —
the one entry in the due-date table that is not the month after its own quarter,
and the one a hand-written table gets wrong. There is a test for it.

## The generators are pure, and that is the whole point

`erpnext_mcp/form_generators.py` reads no database and has no side effects.
Everything a form needs arrives as an argument.

That is not stylistic. A W-2 is an arithmetic claim about how much money a named
person earned and how much of it was held back, and the only way such a claim is
worth making is if it can be computed from a fixture and checked against a number
somebody worked out on paper. Sixty of the 137 tests in this release do exactly
that, with no site, no database and no payroll entry in sight.

---

## `warnings` is the part to read first

Every generator returns one. It is where a form says which of its numbers is a
floor rather than a figure — and there are exactly three things a generator
cannot work out for itself, each of them real.

### Which state a dollar was earned in

A slip carries one `work_state`: the state the worker spent the most hours in
that period. A cross-state pay period allocates gross between two. The slip's
optional `state_wages` is that allocation where the caller has it; without it the
whole gross lands on `work_state`.

**The warning only fires where there is something to get wrong.** A slip that ran
two state engines and carried no allocation is ambiguous and says so. A
single-state slip is not ambiguous, and a form that warned about it on every W-2
would have taught its reader to skip the warnings — which is the failure mode a
warning list has.

### Year-to-date wages before the first slip in hand

The Social Security wage base and Washington's UI taxable wage base are **annual,
per-employee** caps. A quarterly form sees one quarter. Pass
`ytd_wages_by_employee` and the cap is applied correctly; without it the cap
applies to the quarter alone, which is exactly right for Q1 and an overstatement
in every quarter after it. The form says which it was.

### Additional Medicare, separately

A slip's `medicare` field is the ordinary 1.45% and the 0.9% surcharge **added
together**, because that is what comes out of a paycheck and that is what
`calculate_full_payroll` stores. Form 941 line 5d needs the surcharge on its own.
Where the two were never stored apart, the line is zero and the form says why,
rather than guessing at a split.

## The wage bases are consumed employee by employee

Two workers at $100,000 each are **$200,000** of Social Security wages on a 941 —
not $176,100.

Applying an annual per-person ceiling to a company's grand total would let one
high earner's remaining headroom absorb everybody else's excess. It produces a
number that looks entirely plausible, understates the liability, and arrives back
as an assessment letter. `_capped_wages_by_employee` sums per employee, applies
each one's own remaining headroom, and names in `warnings` who hit the ceiling.

The same function does Oregon's UI base and Washington's, which is why it takes
the state as an argument rather than existing three times.

---

## Line 7 is the fractions-of-cents adjustment, and it is computed

Form 941 line 5 is calculated at the **combined** rate — 12.4% and 2.9%, both
halves — against the quarter's total wages. What actually came out of the
paychecks is the sum of many per-period roundings, matched by the employer. The
two disagree by cents.

That difference is exactly what line 7 exists for, so it is computed rather than
left at zero:

```
line 7 = (what was withheld and matched) − (5a + 5b + 5c + 5d)
```

A slip withheld one cent over produces `line7 = 0.02`, because the employer
matched the cent.

## What none of this does

**File anything.** No transmission, no official scannable red-ink Copy A, no
deposit schedule, no e-file. The tools compute box and line values with the
inputs that made them; a person reads them onto the real form or into Frances
Online, EFTPS or the ESD portal.

`mark_tax_form_filed` records that a human did that — status, date, and whatever
confirmation number came back. Filing the same form twice is refused: it would
overwrite the date and confirmation of the filing that actually happened, which
is the one thing a filing record exists to preserve.

---

## Four digits, never nine

Every form prints `XXX-XX-1234`, read off the I-9's `ssn_last_four` — the one
record on this site that legitimately holds any part of a Social Security number,
and even there it holds four digits. The person filing completes the rest from
the paper.

This is the judgement `generate_1099_prefill` made in v0.11.0 and it has not
changed: storing nine digits so a form can print nine digits trades a real breach
risk for a saved minute. A W-2 with no SSN on file prints
`XXX-XX-____  (no SSN on file)`, and the WA ESD report — which cannot be filed
without them — reports how many are missing.

---

## Washington asks for hours, and that is not incidental

The ESD quarterly report is one of the few filings in the country that requires
**hours worked per employee**. Benefit eligibility is an hours test, so a report
filed with wages and no hours is incomplete rather than approximate — ESD rejects
it. `generate_wa_esd_data` names every employee who has Washington wages and no
hours.

Hours are reported as whole numbers, **rounded down**. An hour was either worked
or it was not, and rounding up manufactures eligibility.

---

## One field on somebody else's — well, our own — doctype

`State Tax Configuration` gains **`employer_account_number`**: Oregon's Business
Identification Number, Washington's ESD account number. It prints in W-2 box 15
and at the head of every state return.

It is a field rather than an argument because without it every state form prints
a blank where its account number goes, on every generation, forever. A `state_ids`
argument overrides it — a company filing under a second account for one quarter
should not have to edit its configuration to say so.

## The tools

| Tool | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `list_tax_forms` | **ON** | Filtered by type, year, quarter, employee, status. Returns `by_status` |
| `get_tax_form` | **ON** | One form, every box and line value as computed at generation time |
| `generate_tax_form` | OFF | Computes a form from payroll and records it as `Generated` |
| `regenerate_tax_form` | OFF | Recomputes and reports what moved |
| `mark_tax_form_filed` | OFF | Records a filing. Transmits nothing |

Only **`Calculated` and `Submitted`** payroll entries are counted. A `Draft`
payroll has not been paid and a `Cancelled` one was not.

A second form for the same period and recipient is refused while the first is not
`Amended`, and the error names the existing docname. The guard is **per
recipient**, so two employees get their own W-2s and each quarter gets its own
941.

## For the operator

```bash
bench --site <site> migrate
```

Then tick what you want in **ERPNext MCP Settings → Tax Form Generators**. The
two read tools are already on; the three write tools are off, as every mutating
tool in this app ships off.

Set **Employer Account Number** on each active State Tax Configuration before
generating a state return, or every one of them will warn that it is missing.
