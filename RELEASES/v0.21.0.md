# v0.21.0 — the shape of a visit is data

**Released 2026-08-03. A worker walks into a cabin once and does everything it
needs; the register still gets a Housing Inspection, a Detector Test and a Water
Test, separately, at their own cadences. What defines that visit is a ROW an
operator writes — not a release somebody ships.**

Tool surface **239 → 249** (111 read, 138 mutating). Suite **4,055 → 4,121
passing**. Five new doctypes, one additive column on Farm Task, four seeded
templates, one new audit-packet section, and the rule engine now bundles.
**Backend only.** The iOS sectioned-form renderer ships separately in v0.21.1
after v0.20.1 validates on TestFlight; nothing in this release requires it.

---

## 1. The tension this resolves, and the answer

A worker experiences **one visit**. They drive to the north block, walk into
MC-Cabin-01, do everything the cabin needs, and drive back.

Compliance sees **three regulated cadences that must stay separate**:

| Record | Cadence | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Housing Inspection | annual | 29 CFR 1910.142, OAR 437-004-1120 |
| Detector Test | the fire code's cycle | OAR 437-004-1120 |
| Water Test | ninety days | FSMA Subpart E |

Merging them into one record makes one record due on three schedules at once,
and the first quarterly detector test between two annual walks has nowhere to go.

**The answer: the UX is grouped and the records stay separate.** An *Inspection
Session* is the afternoon. The records it produces are the register, produced
exactly as they would have been from three separate trips — same doctypes, same
registers advanced, same alerts dismissed, same audit-packet rows. What changes
is that the photographs and the signature are captured once, and an auditor
holding a Housing Inspection can now ask **which visit produced it** and get an
answer.

## 2. Templates are data. This is the load-bearing claim.

An **Inspection Template** is a Frappe record. It says which sections a visit
consists of, what evidence each needs, which renderer draws it, and which
compliance record it produces.

`create_inspection_template` writes one and it is **live**: it reaches the
handset on the next fetch, the rule engine can match it on the next sweep, and no
line of code, no DocType JSON and no TestFlight build was involved.

That is the same argument the Configurable Compliance Framework makes about
*rules*, one layer up, and for the same reason: regulations move. OR-OSHA
renumbered the heat rule. OTCO added a Fraud Prevention Plan. A system whose
answer to "the close-down checklist now needs the propane logged" is a code
release is a system that answers in weeks.

### The five doctypes

| DocType | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `Inspection Template` | One shape of visit. `INSPT-2026-0001`. |
| `Inspection Template Section` | One part of it: name, renderer, contract, produced record. |
| `Inspection Session` | One worker's execution of one template at one place. `INSPS-2026-0001`. |
| `Inspection Session Evidence` | The shared tray — one file, tagged with the section it answered. |
| `Inspection Session Section Submission` | What was submitted per section, and **which record it produced**. |

`Farm Task` gains one nullable Link, `inspection_session`. **The task is still
the dispatch atom** — one card on the Kanban board, one claim, one entry against
the concurrent-claim limit. The session is the sectioned form behind that card,
never a second kind of card beside it. A board with two kinds of item on it is a
board somebody has to be taught to read.

## 3. Versions are pinned, and edits supersede

`update_inspection_template` does **not** edit the row. It writes a **new** row at
version+1 carrying the changes, deactivates the old one, and points the old one's
`superseded_by` at the new one.

Three consequences, and the third is the one that was actually asked about:

1. A template edited in July does not retroactively change how April's session
   reads. April's row is still on the site, with its sections as they were.
2. `template_version` on the session states the number, so a printed packet is
   self-contained without a second lookup.
3. **A session started against v1 while v2 is being authored is unaffected.**
   There is no window and no race, because v2 is a *different document* and v1 is
   never touched. The session pins the row, and the row is the version.

The cost: `template_name` cannot be unique at the schema level, since v1 and v2
share a name by construction. What is enforced instead, in the controller, is
that only one **live** row (active, not superseded) may hold a name — because two
of those are two answers to "start a Mid-season Habitability here" and the one a
worker gets would be whichever sorted first.

Deactivating destroys nothing: every session worked from a template stays
readable, every compliance record stays in the register and in the audit packet.
**There is deliberately no delete.**

## 4. Two sections that produce the same record produce ONE record

**The one design decision in this release that departs from the brief, and the
reason it does.**

`Detector Test` carries a smoke result *and* a CO result, and both are required
fields with a `Pass` default. A template that tests them as two sections — which
is the right shape for the worker, who walks to one detector and then to the
other — cannot file two Detector Test records for one cabin on one day without
**each of them asserting something it was never told about the other detector**.
Either they contradict each other, or they duplicate each other. Two compliance
records that disagree is precisely the failure this whole app exists to prevent,
and it would have arrived through the feature meant to make the evidence better.

So `submit_inspection_session` groups the submitted sections by *(produced
doctype, resolved subject)* and writes **one** record per group, layering the
sections' values in order. **Both section submissions link that one record**, so
the trail from either side of the walk is intact, and the unit's smoke *and* CO
dates both move — which is what the alert was actually asking about.

The brief's Mid-season walk-through therefore produces **two** compliance records
from three sections (1 Housing Inspection + 1 Detector Test covering both
detectors), not three. The seeded templates keep the two-section shape, because
the shape is right for the worker; only the filing is merged.

## 5. The rule engine bundles, deterministically

`generate_tasks_from_compliance_alerts` gained one pass in front of the existing
per-alert path. The rule, in full, because an auditor will ask why three things
became one job:

1. Alerts are grouped by **the place they point at**, and only where the source
   doctype is a register somebody can be sent to. An alert about a certificate is
   not about a place and is never bundled.
2. A place qualifies with **two or more alerts of different types**. One alert is
   one task; that path is completely unchanged and remains the common case.
3. Each alert type is translated into the compliance record answering it would
   produce, through the **same `ALERT_TASK_MAP`** the per-alert path uses. An
   alert type whose recipe produces no record (a licence renewal, a policy
   review) takes the place out of the running — a visit that silently answers
   three of four overdue things leaves the fourth answered by nothing.
4. A template matches when its sections produce a **superset** of those records.
   Superset and not equality: a template that also does a water test is still the
   right template for a cabin whose water is not due, and the worker marks that
   section skipped.
5. Ties break on `(extra sections beyond what was wanted, total sections,
   docname)`. Tightest fit first, and the docname makes it total — the choice is
   the same on every run and every site.
6. **No match is a first-class answer** and leaves every alert at that place on
   the unchanged per-alert path.

Set inclusion and two integers. **No model, no natural-language interpretation,
no probability anywhere in the trigger path.** For every session an auditor
questions, the answer traces to: these alerts were open at this place, this
template's sections produce a superset of what they asked for, it was the
tightest fit, here is its version.

A template whose `applies_to_asset_type` is a *label* rather than a register —
`Sprayer`, `Cabin`, `General` — is **never matched automatically**. A "Cabin"
template is almost certainly about a Housing Unit, and the engine still will not
assume it: automatically bundling somebody's compliance work is exactly the place
not to guess.

### Idempotence, by a different mechanism

A Farm Task carries one `source_alert`; a session answers several. So the session
stores all of them in `source_alerts`, and the next sweep reads them back —
**split on newlines and compared as whole docnames, never as a substring**, for
the reason `training.matches` gives about regimes. A second run finds the session
and raises nothing. Once the session is *submitted*, its records have moved the
registers, the alerts dismiss themselves on the next sweep, and none of this is
reached at all.

## 6. The four seeded templates

Installed on install and on every migrate, **idempotently, checked by name across
every row**. This is **not** a Frappe `fixtures` entry, and `test_hooks.py`
forbids the word by name for exactly this feature's reason: a fixture is imported
by `bench migrate` with no ability to skip what a site already has, so an
operator who added a section to their close-down would get it silently removed on
the next upgrade — and the first anybody would know is a winter with no propane
check.

| Template | Applies to | Sections | Citations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-season Cabin Opening | Housing Unit | 5 | OAR 437-004-1120; 29 CFR 1910.142; FSMA Subpart L |
| Mid-season Habitability | Housing Unit | 3 | OAR 437-004-1120; 29 CFR 1910.142 |
| Post-harvest Cabin Close-down | Housing Unit | 6 | OAR 437-004-1120; internal close-down SOP |
| Spray Day Inspection | Sprayer | 3 | 40 CFR 170.401; product-specific EPA labels |

A template somebody **edited** keeps the edit. One somebody **deactivated** stays
deactivated. One somebody **superseded** with their own version 2 does not get
version 1 seeded back beside it — which would put two live templates with one
name on the site and make the sweep's choice between them arbitrary.

### Spray Record does not exist yet — TODO for v0.21.1 or v0.22.0

The Spray Day template's **Product + Rate Recording** section has
`produces_record_doctype` empty. There is no `Spray Record` doctype on any site,
and a section pointing at a doctype that does not exist would refuse **every**
submission — while somebody is standing beside a sprayer trying to file one. The
product, EPA registration number, rate, REI and PHI are captured as findings text
and measurements in the meantime.

The controller refuses such a section **at authoring time**, which is how this was
caught rather than shipped: `create_inspection_template` will not accept a
`produces_record_doctype` this site does not have.

The day the doctype ships, one `update_inspection_template` call points the
section at it, and every session worked before then is still readable against the
version it was worked from. **That is the whole point of templates being data.**

## 7. The audit packet grows a chain of custody

`generate_audit_packet` gains a **`sessions`** section, on every packet type that
already carries `housing` or `water` — FSMA, GAP, GlobalGAP, OSHA, DOL, EPA and
Other.

**It adds no record to the packet.** Every compliance record a session produced is
already there, in the section that reads its own register — a Housing Inspection
is in `housing` whether a session wrote it or a camp manager typed it, which is
the point of producing the records separately.

What was missing is the **sentence joining them**. Three Housing Inspections, two
Detector Tests and a Water Test filed within a minute of each other read, to an
auditor, either as one afternoon's work or as somebody catching up on paperwork,
and nothing in the register said which. Now the packet says:

> *these records were captured in a single Mid-season Habitability session on
> 2026-07-24 by Ana Ramos, foreman Miguel Torres, worked from version 1 of the
> template, evidence timestamped and signed.*

Two counting decisions, both deliberate:

- **Records, not submissions.** Two sections that produced one Detector Test are
  one Detector Test. Counting them twice would tell an auditor there is a record
  they cannot find.
- **Distinct files, not evidence rows.** One signature filed against three
  sections is one photograph, and reporting three would overstate the evidence in
  a document somebody signs.

A session **started and never submitted** produced nothing and is not listed. It
is *counted*, in `open_sessions_in_period`, with a disclosure note — an abandoned
visit is not evidence of anything, and a place whose work was started and dropped
is a place whose alerts are still open.

`generate_compliance_packet` is **not** touched, and that is deliberate rather
than an omission: it builds the reconciliation and fiscal-year-audit packets,
which are financial documents containing no Housing Inspection and no Detector
Test. A sessions section there would be an empty appendix in a document a CPA
reads.

## 8. The ten tools

Six mutating, four read. Every one has the standard guard: the role gate, company
scope, the kill switch in `ERPNext MCP Settings`, an MCP Action Log row, and —
for the mutating six — **the switch ships OFF**.

| Tool | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `list_inspection_templates` | read | with what each produces and which version is live |
| `get_inspection_template` | read | **what a client renders a sectioned form from** |
| `list_inspection_sessions` | read | who went where, from which template and version |
| `get_inspection_session` | read | the pinned version, every submission, the tray, the records |
| `create_inspection_template` | write | live on the next fetch |
| `update_inspection_template` | write | supersedes; never edits |
| `deactivate_inspection_template` | write | withdraws with a reason; destroys nothing |
| `start_inspection_session` | write | pins the version; writes no compliance record |
| `submit_inspection_session` | write | **the one with teeth** |
| `propose_inspection_template_from_regulation` | write | **declared and refuses** |

### What `submit_inspection_session` refuses, and why nothing is written

In order: sections are read off the version the session **pinned**; a submission
naming a section that version does not have is refused; a **required** section
that is missing is refused *by name*; each submitted section is checked against
its own evidence contract and the shortfalls are named.

**Nothing is written if any of those refuses.** Half a visit is a set of
compliance records that look complete and are not — an auditor reading them has
no way to know the detector was never tested. That matters more here than on a
single completion, and it is why the refusals come before the first insert.

An **optional** section may be skipped, its produced-record link stays empty, and
the skip is recorded as *something somebody said* — because an empty space is not.

`notes` as an **empty string** records that nothing was wrong; leaving it out
records that nobody was asked. The two are different answers and the distinction
survives all the way into the produced record's `findings`.

### The declared surface

`propose_inspection_template_from_regulation` is registered, has a schema, has a
switch, and **refuses every call** with the sentence saying so. It is the surface
an AI template proposer will occupy — read a regulation, draft a template with its
sections, contracts and citations, leave it **inactive** for a human to enable —
declared now so the shape is fixed before anything fills it, and inert now
because **at runtime this app is deterministic**. AI belongs at authoring time
behind a human approval, never in the trigger path. Phase 2 of the CCF wires it.

It counts as a tool because it has everything a tool has except an
implementation, and a surface an operator cannot see in the settings form is a
surface nobody can refuse.

## 9. What is NOT in this release

- **No iOS changes.** The sectioned-form renderer is v0.21.1, after v0.20.1
  validates on TestFlight. Every template shipped here is renderable from
  `get_inspection_template` alone, which is the point: `renderer_hint` is a
  *hint*, and a client that does not know one falls back to a freeform form and
  the submission is still valid.
- **No Spray Record doctype.** See §6.
- **No change to any existing compliance record, register, alert rule or
  completion path.** A site that never creates a template behaves exactly as
  v0.20.1 did — including `generate_tasks_from_compliance_alerts`, which bundles
  nothing when there is nothing to bundle with.
