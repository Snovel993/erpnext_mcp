# v0.19.3 — the shift is the anchor

**Released 2026-08-03. Substantial doctype work: six new DocTypes, ten new tools,
the thirteenth compliance rule, and a one-way bridge into Frappe HR.**

Every compliance feature in this app so far has hung off a **document** or a
**moment**. A certificate has an expiry. A cabin has a last-inspected date. A
training record has a completion and a lapse. A task completion carries whatever
the weather was when somebody closed it.

Oregon OSHA does not ask any of those questions. OAR 437-004-1131 asks whether
**the July 15 shift** complied — water at one quart per worker per hour for the
whole exposure, shade within reach, the preventative rest cycle taken once the
heat index passed 80 °F, the supervisor observing for signs, everybody trained
before they set foot on a hot block. Every one of those is a statement about a
**period** and about a **crew**, and no record scoped to a single job can make it.

So v0.19.3 introduces the entity the exposure regimes actually turn on: the
**Farm Shift**. A crew, at a place, for a span of time, with a timeline of what
was done about the conditions and a signature on the end of it.

Suite: 3,653 → **3,734 passing**. Tool surface: 214 → **224** (99 read, 125
mutating).

---

## 1. The shift, and why it is the anchor

> A task completion carries a point-in-time reading. A shift carries a timeline.

That sentence is the whole release. A temperature at the moment one job closed is
a data point; ninety readings across nine hours, interleaved with four water
breaks and two supervisor observations, is a **defence**. The first invites the
question "and the rest of the day?"; the second answers it before it is asked.

The same shape serves the other exposure-based regimes without being redesigned:

| Shift type | The timeline that matters | The regime it feeds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harvest, Prune | heat index, water/shade/rest events | OAR 437-004-1131 |
| Spray | wind speed and direction, product, PPE | EPA WPS 40 CFR 170 |
| Irrigation | soil temperature, valve state changes | frost-protection documentation |
| Housing Work, Detector Test Round | the camp's own cycle | OAR 437-004-1120 |

**Four DocTypes**, because a shift is a parent with three timelines hanging off
it:

- **`Farm Shift`** — foreman, company, location, `farm_location_gps`, type,
  start, end, status, signature, notes.
- **`Farm Shift Crew Member`** — employee, role, `joined_at`, `left_at`, notes.
- **`Farm Shift Compliance Event`** — type, timestamp, who logged it, a
  description, a pointer back at the record that produced it, a denormalised
  weather snapshot and an optional photograph.
- **`Farm Shift Weather Reading`** — **ships empty on purpose.** See §6.

`status` is computed on every save from two facts, in this order: no end time
means **Active** whatever anybody ticked, because an open shift is what the
v0.19.4 weather sweep walks and a shift marked Closed with no end would drop out
of the fetch while still being worked.

Docnames are `SHIFT-2026-0001`, expanded by the controller rather than by
Frappe's `naming_series` hook, so a docname is the same string on a bench, in a
patch and in the standalone suite. The year is the year the shift **started** — a
night shift beginning on 31 December belongs to the year it began, and a series
keyed off `today()` would file it under the following one for ever.

## 2. The foreman is the sole actor, and that is a compliance decision

**There is no `clock_in` tool here and there will not be one.** Three reasons,
and the first decides it:

1. **-1131 puts the obligations on a named responsible person.** The one who
   calls the water break, who observes for signs, who decides a rest cycle is
   needed. The record has to carry that person's identity as a fact, not derive
   it from whoever happened to be rostered.
2. **FSMA §112.161(b) asks that person to review and sign.** Their identity must
   be *on* the shift.
3. **Worker self-clock is the failure mode where nobody's in charge.** A crew of
   thirty each clocking themselves in is a shift with thirty people responsible
   for the record, which is a shift with nobody responsible for it — and the
   observable failure is that nobody logged the water break because everybody
   assumed somebody else had.

There is a practical fourth: workers in the field often have no device at all, and
a foreman-driven flow works either way.

### Per-worker attendance survives inside the crew envelope

The obvious objection to a crew-shaped record is that payroll is person-shaped,
and it would be a real objection if the crew table were a list of names. It is
not. Every row carries `joined_at` and `left_at`, so *"the crew worked 06:00 to
15:00"* and *"Ana joined at 07:10 and left at 13:00"* are both true, both stored,
and neither derived from the other.

**`remove_worker_from_shift` sets `left_at`; it does not delete the row.** The
tool's name is the operational verb and the storage is the compliance one, and
the two are allowed to differ — the row is the only record that this person was
on the shift at all, which is what a wage claim turns on and what says who was
exposed on a hot afternoon before they were sent home.

## 3. Two defaults that point opposite ways, on purpose

`start_shift` stamps everybody it rosters with the **shift's own start**.
`add_worker_to_shift` stamps a late arrival with **now**.

That looks inconsistent and is not. Everybody rostered at the beginning *was*
there at the beginning, so stamping them with the moment the API call landed
would shave minutes off every one of their days. A worker added mid-shift
arrived when somebody said so.

The refusals follow the same logic. The same person cannot be on a crew twice —
two rows look deliberate on the form and become two Attendance days for one
person when the shift closes, and the payroll register carries the error rather
than the shift. `remove_worker_from_shift` called twice without an explicit time
is refused, because a silent second call would move a departure that has already
happened to now and lengthen a day that has already ended.

## 4. The timeline is the evidence; the heat record is only the claim

`log_shift_event` writes one row per thing the foreman did about the conditions,
at the moment it happened: Water Break, Shade Break, Rest Cycle, Supervisor
Observation, Heat Illness Signs Check, Cool-Down, Threshold Crossed,
Acclimatization Reminder, Other.

A closed vocabulary, because a timeline is only readable if the rows are
comparable: four Water Breaks across a shift are a **cadence**, and four
free-text sentences describing the same thing are four sentences somebody has to
read and compare by hand.

**An event timestamped slightly outside the shift is kept and reported, not
refused.** A clock five minutes out is not a false record, and refusing would
mean the break goes unlogged rather than logged approximately. The reader is
better served by an honest timestamp marginally outside the envelope than by no
event at all — same posture the training tools take towards a missing §112.161
element.

`producer_record_doctype` / `producer_record_name` are plain strings rather than
a Dynamic Link, deliberately: a producer can belong to an app this site does not
have, and a link that refuses to save is a compliance event that does not get
logged.

## 5. `end_shift` requires a signature, and that is why it is a tool

An unsigned close is an `UPDATE` setting a timestamp. The signature is what makes
the close an **attestation** — the supervisor saying, at the moment they say it,
that this is what happened — and §112.161(b) asks for a review that is dated
**and signed** rather than merely recorded. Without one the tool refuses, the
shift stays open, and nothing is written.

**What it does not refuse:** a shift with obligations unmet, or with an empty
event timeline. A day where the shade trailer broke down and the crew was sent
home at eleven is a real shift with a real gap. A system that would not let the
gap be recorded would produce either a false record or no record, and the result
says so instead:

> NOTHING WAS LOGGED ON THIS SHIFT'S TIMELINE. That is recorded rather than
> refused — a shift where nothing needed logging is a real shift — but on a hot
> day it is the absence an inspector reads as "no water breaks were called",
> because there is no other reading available.

### The Attendance bridge

Closing a shift writes **one submitted `Attendance` per crew member**, spanning
that person's own `joined_at` to their own `left_at` — or to the shift's end
where they stayed to it.

**Not the shift's span.** A worker who arrived an hour late and left two hours
early worked six hours of a nine-hour shift, and a row claiming nine is wrong in
the employer's favour, which is the direction that gets litigated.

Rows are **submitted**, not left as drafts, because `get_attendance_summary`
counts `docstatus 1` only — a bridge that left drafts would produce a payroll
register that read as empty for every shift-formed day.

`Attendance` gains a **`farm_shift`** Custom Field, declared in
`compliance_fields.py` beside the v0.15.0 columns and reported by
`before_uninstall`. It is the fourth column this app grafts onto a doctype it did
not create, and the argument is the same one: without it a shift-formed day is
indistinguishable from a hand-keyed one, so nobody reading the register can reach
the conditions the person worked in — and the bridge, unable to tell its own rows
from anybody else's, would pay somebody twice for one afternoon.

**The bridge runs one way only, and that is not an omission.** A shift is formed
by a foreman naming a crew, a location and a type; an Attendance row carries none
of those. Deriving shifts from attendance would invent a foreman, invent a
location and invent the boundary between two crews who worked the same day —
three fabrications on a record an inspector reads.

**The bridge never blocks the close.** A site without Frappe HR, an employee
archived since the shift ran, a day somebody already keyed in by hand: every one
is *reported* in the result and none of them refuses a signed shift. The
signature is the compliance act; the payroll row is the convenience, and refusing
the close would lose the first to save the second.

## 6. The weather table ships empty, on purpose

`Farm Shift Weather Reading` has no populate hook in this release. That is the
plan rather than an oversight: its shape is what the compliance-event snapshot
columns denormalise from and what the heat record's maxima will be computed over,
so fixing it **now** means v0.19.4 wires a fetch instead of migrating a schema
under live compliance records.

v0.19.4 ports `app/utils/weather.py` out of farm_app into
`erpnext_mcp/services/weather.py`, walks every shift with no `end_datetime` on a
fifteen-minute schedule, and backfills closed shifts from Open-Meteo's archive
API so a shift that ran before the service was switched on can still be
documented.

`start_shift` says so when a shift has no coordinates:

> No `farm_location_gps` on this shift, so it will get NO weather timeline when
> v0.19.4 wires the fetch — the service asks Open-Meteo what the conditions are
> at a place, and there is no place here.

## 7. `Heat Exposure Event` — the answer to "prove that shift complied"

One record, anchored to one shift, saying what was actually done. Docnames are
`HEAT-2026-0001`, keyed to the year of the **shift** rather than the year
somebody filed it.

**One per shift, and the second call is refused by name.** Two records about one
exposure period will disagree, and the one an inspector finds will be whichever
was filed second. The link carries a unique index, the controller refuses with a
sentence, and the tool refuses **before writing anything** and names the record
that already exists — because "amend HEAT-2026-0004" is an instruction somebody
can follow and a duplicate-key error is not.

### Three refusals, and each one is about a specific failure

**Unsigned.** Submitting is the attestation, and an unsigned heat record is a
claim with nobody behind it. This is the record a serious-injury investigation is
read from.

**Signs observed, no emergency response, no notes.** Signs seen and nothing done
is the sequence that kills people. There *are* legitimate versions — the worker
recovered in shade within minutes and declined further help; the signs were mild
and the person was moved to lighter work and watched — and every one of them is a
sentence somebody can write. **What is refused is the silence.**

**A `training_verified` claim the register contradicts.** -1131 requires heat
illness prevention training annually *and* before work at a site where the heat
index will reach 80 °F, so the training register knows the answer. The tool walks
the crew, looks for a current OR-OSHA-tagged record for each of them **as of the
day of the shift** — a card that expired last week was current in July, and
checking against today would report a July shift as non-compliant because of
something that happened after it — and refuses a `true` claim the register
disproves.

> THE SAME AUDIT PACKET CARRIES BOTH THIS RECORD AND THE TRAINING REGISTER, and a
> packet that contradicts itself is worse than a packet with a gap.

Claiming `false` is **accepted** and the missing names are reported. A shift that
ran with an untrained worker happened, and the record of it is what the operation
needs to have.

Everything else is recorded with the gap stated. `heat_gaps()` lists the
obligations the record does not claim were met, in the rule's own terms, on every
read of it.

The acclimatization plan is a Table MultiSelect naming the workers with fewer
than fourteen days in the heat, per -1131(g). Named individually rather than
counted, because these are the people most likely to be hospitalised and a plan
for "the new workers" is one an inspector cannot check. Somebody not on the
shift's crew is refused: a plan naming a person who was not there reads as filled
in from a list rather than from the block, which is exactly the impression a real
plan is fighting.

## 8. The thirteenth rule: `supervisor_review_lapsed`

Twelve rules watched documents and dates. This one watches **a signature that was
never put on a record** — the work was done, the record was written, and the
second pair of eyes never arrived.

FSMA 21 CFR 112.161(b) is one sentence and it is the most commonly cited finding
against farms whose actual practice is fine: records must be *"reviewed, dated,
and signed, within a reasonable time after the records are made, by a supervisor
or responsible party."* **USDA GAP does not ask for it.** So an operation with an
immaculate GAP binder — every training delivered, every trainee signature, every
certificate current — fails on the one element its own auditor never mentioned,
and finds out from an FDA-contracted inspector.

| Age of an unsigned record | Severity |
| --- | --- |
| under 14 days | nothing |
| 14–30 days | **Warning** |
| over 30 days | **Critical** |

Fourteen days is a lead time like every other number in `rules.py`. The rule
states no interval; the practice "reasonable time" is read against for records
generated daily is *weekly* review. A fortnight is one clear miss of that cadence
— two Fridays gone, and the afternoon still recent enough for the supervisor to
remember what they are signing off.

**The clock runs from `creation`, not from the activity date.** §112.161(b)'s own
phrase is "after the records are made". A training delivered last March and
recorded yesterday has a record that is one day old, and the supervisor has not
been late reviewing it — the operation was late *writing* it, which is a
§112.161(a)(2) problem and a different finding. Reading the activity date here
would raise a Critical on every record of a season somebody backfilled, and a
calendar full of alerts nobody can act on is a calendar nobody reads.

**It walks a table, not a doctype.** §112.161(b) is not about training; it reaches
every activity record under the part, and this app already ships four more that
will carry the same two columns:

```python
REVIEW_TARGETS = (
    ReviewTarget(doctype="Employee Training Record", ...),
    # Housing Inspection   — OAR 437-004-1120 asks the same of a habitability walk
    # Water Test           — §112.161(b) reaches Subpart E records directly
    # Heat Exposure Event  — a second pair of eyes on top of the foreman's
    # Farm Task Assignment — the completion record for everything dispatched
)
```

One row in v0.19.3. The alternative was discovering in v0.19.3.1 that a
training-shaped rule has to be rewritten to add the second doctype. A target
whose columns are absent is skipped **by name** rather than crashing the sweep,
which is what makes adding the next one a one-line change on a live site.

Regimes: `("FSMA",)`, and only FSMA. The other schemes ask for the record and none
of them asks for this signature — which is exactly why the gap exists, so tagging
it wider would put it in packets whose auditors never raise it and dilute the one
that does.

`Compliance Alert.category` gained **`Records`**. A supervisor review is not a
Workforce item once the rule reaches water tests and cabin walks.

## 9. The audit packet gains a heat section

`generate_audit_packet(audit_type="OSHA")` now carries **Heat exposure (OAR
437-004-1131)**: one row per documented hot shift, with the heat index, the four
obligation ticks, and a count of the gaps.

**On the OSHA packet alone.** A GAP auditor handed a heat register is being shown
evidence for a scheme they do not audit, which invites a question nobody wanted
to answer — exactly as a DOL packet containing a GlobalGAP certificate would.

**Drafts are excluded.** Submitting the record is the supervisor's attestation, so
a draft is a page of ticks nobody has signed, and presenting one as evidence would
be misleading in exactly the direction that matters.

**The gaps are disclosed in the packet** rather than left to be found. An
inspector who finds a gap themselves asks a much harder question than one who was
shown it, and the notes on each record say what happened instead.

## 10. Ten tools

Six mutating (all default OFF), four read-only (all default ON). Every one goes
through the standard guard pattern: role gate (System Manager / HR Manager / HR
User / Farm Manager), company scope via the foreman's own company, the kill
switch, and an MCP Action Log row.

The guards are `create_employee`'s, **imported rather than restated**. A shift is
a personnel record before it is a compliance record: it names who was at work and
for how long, it is read in a wage claim, and forming one for an entity you cannot
see would put a crew on a register you cannot read.

| Tool | |
| --- | --- |
| `start_shift` | mutating |
| `add_worker_to_shift` | mutating |
| `remove_worker_from_shift` | mutating |
| `log_shift_event` | mutating |
| `end_shift` | mutating |
| `create_heat_exposure_event` | mutating |
| `list_shifts` | read |
| `get_shift` | read |
| `list_heat_exposure_events` | read |
| `get_heat_exposure_event` | read |

`get_shift` is the one worth naming: it returns the crew with each person's own
span, the compliance-event timeline in **time order**, the weather timeline and
the heat record. That is the evidence chain an inspector is handed. Each crew row
reports `present_until`, the honest reading of an empty `left_at` — they were
there to the end — **computed** rather than written back, because writing it would
destroy the distinction between "left at 13:00" and "stayed to the end" the moment
the end time changed.

### Roles

A new group, `SHIFTS = ("Farm Shift", "Heat Exposure Event")`, rather than a line
in `COMPLIANCE_REGISTERS` — because the two halves belong to different people.
**Forming a shift is operational and it is the Foreman's**; a heat record is
compliance evidence and a Compliance Officer reads it. Folding them into the
register group would have given a Compliance Officer the power to form a crew,
which is the same separation `_grant(READ, DISPATCH)` on that role already exists
to keep.

| Role | |
| --- | --- |
| Foreman | **full** — the one register where the Foreman has more than the Compliance Officer |
| Farm Manager | full |
| Compliance Officer | read |
| Field Worker | read — the app can show a worker which crew they are on and what breaks were called |

## Judgment calls

**`naming_series` as a field, expanded by the controller.** The spec asked for
`SHIFT-.YYYY.-.####`. Both doctypes carry the series as a read-only field so the
Desk shows it, and `shifts.next_in_series` does the expansion — which means a
docname is the same string on a bench, in a patch and in the standalone suite,
and a test asserting `SHIFT-2026-0001` asserts the thing that ships. No other
doctype in this app uses Frappe's `naming_series` hook.

**Two fields the spec's status rule implied but did not list.** `cancelled`
(Check) and `cancellation_reason` (Small Text). The declared status computation
reads "end_datetime NOT NULL AND cancelled_flag → Cancelled", so the flag has to
exist. The reason is required once the flag is ticked, on the same argument
`revoke_mobile_user` makes: the flag is the part anybody could reconstruct and
the sentence is not.

**A sixth DocType for the acclimatization plan.** `Heat Acclimatization Worker`.
A Frappe Table MultiSelect needs a child doctype to hold the links; there was no
existing employee-link child to reuse, and a comma-separated column would have
lost the link the whole field exists to provide.

**Ten tools, not nine.** The spec's header said "9 new MCP tools (5 mutating, 4
read-only)" and the list beneath it named six mutating and four read-only. The
list is authoritative; the arithmetic was not.

**`Records` as a new `Compliance Alert.category`.** `supervisor_review_lapsed` is
generalizable across doctypes, so `Workforce` — which is what `training_expiring`
uses — would become wrong the moment the table grows a water test. Adding a Select
option invalidates nothing already stored.

**A URL is taken as given; a docname is checked.** `file_reference` refuses a File
docname this site has never heard of and accepts a path. A signature pointing at
nothing satisfies the contract and proves nothing — but refusing an unresolvable
*path* would leave a shift OPEN, and an open shift is a worse outcome, because
the timeline keeps growing against it. Same rule `inspections.normalise_evidence`
has applied since v0.16.0.

## What is not here

- **Weather.** The table exists and nothing writes to it. v0.19.4.
- **A `cancel_shift` tool.** The `cancelled` flag is settable in the Desk and
  the controller enforces the reason. No tool asked for one.
- **Shifts derived from existing Attendance.** Argued against in §5 — the bridge
  is one-way by design.
- **The other four `REVIEW_TARGETS`.** Each is a one-line addition the day its
  doctype grows the two §112.161(b) columns.
