# v0.79.0 — the day as it actually happens

Ten features. Six doctypes. Twenty tools, nineteen mobile routes. All of it is
one gap: between how this app modelled a day and how a day goes.

---

## Five things that happen, and what the app said about each

```
what happens                          what the app could say before this
────────────────────────────────────  ───────────────────────────────────
called away mid-job                   nothing. In-Progress, Completed or Rejected
two people report one valve           nothing. two tasks, forever
an investigation runs a week          nothing. a task is done in one visit
somebody is on the ground             nothing. no incident record at all
a written warning, then another       nothing. no discipline register
```

Each of those is a thing a farm does every week. None of them had a record.

---

## Interruption, and the hour that survives it

A worker sets an irrigation line at nine and is called to a broken valve at half
past. Three bad options existed: leave it In-Progress and lie about who is
working on what, complete it and lie about it being done, or reject it and throw
away the morning.

**`Paused` is a state, not a flag.** A boolean beside In-Progress would leave the
Kanban board showing two cards in one column with one of them not being worked,
which is the picture a dispatch board exists to prevent.

**The clock is a list of segments.**

```
09:00  start        ┐
09:30  pause        ┘ 30 min   "Called to the valve at Home-7"
11:00  resume       ┐
11:45  complete     ┘ 45 min

actual_duration_minutes = 75, not 165
```

The wall clock bills the valve repair to the irrigating, on exactly the
fragmented afternoons where an hour charged to a job matters most. Assignments
written before this release have no segments and fall back to the old
arithmetic — a duration that came back zero for a season of history would be the
worse bug by a wide margin.

**One task In-Progress per worker, enforced by pausing rather than by refusing.**

Somebody standing at a broken valve does not want to be told to go and tidy up
the job they walked away from. So starting a second task auto-pauses the first,
marks it `auto_paused`, and **says so**:

```
Irrigate Block 3 was in progress and has been paused so this one can run —
30 minute(s) were banked against it and nothing was lost. Resume it with
resume_farm_task.
```

A silent stand-down would leave a worker discovering at the end of the day that
their morning went to a task they thought they were still on. Refusing outright
would be defensible and routed around within a week.

Claiming does **not** auto-pause. Claiming is planning a morning; a worker may
hold three. Pausing somebody's running job because they picked up their next one
would stop the clock on work they are still doing.

A paused task can be rejected without resuming it first — *"I was called away and
the ladder is still broken"* is the sentence a board needs, and forcing a resume
would put a minute of clock on a job nobody touched.

## Two people, one broken valve

Two workers walk past the same leaking valve an hour apart and both do the right
thing: they report it.

**A board that silently deduplicated them would be guessing that two reports of a
valve are the same valve**, and on a farm with four hundred of them, sometimes
they are not.

So the system surfaces and the human decides. `claim_farm_task` and
`start_farm_task` return:

```json
{"duplicate_hint": {
  "message": "There is already an open task for this: Leaking valve at Home-7
              (FT-2026-08-00031) held by Ana Ramos. Link to it?",
  "actions": ["link_farm_tasks", "merge_farm_task"]}}
```

`link_farm_tasks` writes a row on **both** sides — a relationship stored on one
record only is invisible from whichever of the two somebody opens, and the whole
point is that the second person to walk up to a job finds the first person's.
The reverse relationship is the mirror: `duplicate_of` one way is `merged_from`
the other.

`merge_farm_task` folds a duplicate into the task actually being worked:

- **the primary keeps its state and its clock.** A merge is a statement about
  which record the work is under; it is not an event in the work, and a worker
  mid-job does not find their timer reset because a foreman tidied a board.
- **the duplicate is not deleted.** It goes to `Merged` with `merged_into`
  naming the primary. Its evidence is *copied* to the primary; its assignments
  and time segments stay exactly where they are, so `combined_minutes` is the
  effort both people put in and somebody opening the duplicate six weeks later
  is told where the work went.
- **merging a finished task is refused.** A completed job is a record of work
  that happened; link it instead.

## Work that does not finish today

An accident investigation is not a task. It is a set of them: interview the
witness, photograph the scene, pull the camera footage, write the root cause —
different days, different people.

`Farm Task.parent_task` is a **Dynamic Link**, because the thing with steps under
it is as often an `Accident Report` as another task.

**A parent does not close while a step is live.** Without that rule the first
person to finish their piece closes the investigation, and the camera footage
nobody pulled becomes a finding nobody made. The refusal names the steps, because
"it is not finished" is useless and "you are waiting on the witness interview" is
actionable.

One level of nesting only: a tree of sub-tasks is a project plan, and a dispatch
board that became one would stop being readable at a tailgate.

**Nothing auto-closes at the end of a shift.** `end_shift` ends a *shift*. A task
is not a shift, and a record left In Progress on Friday is In Progress on Monday.

## Narrative: what happened, in the words of whoever was there

`Task Note` is **one child table with three parents** — Farm Task, Accident
Report, Discipline Record. Appending an account is one act, and three
near-identical tables would drift the first time one of them grew a column.

**Entries are appended and never edited.** An investigation spanning four days is
four entries with four timestamps, and the reason a hearing believes any of it is
that Monday's account was written on Monday. A record where Thursday can rewrite
Monday is not a contemporaneous record; it is a document somebody prepared.

### `attach_audio_note`

A foreman at an accident scene has a phone in one hand, a worker on the ground,
and about ninety seconds of clear memory before the story starts to smooth
itself out. Every field in this app before now asked them to pick a category.

iOS's Speech framework transcribes **on-device** — so a foreman in a block with
no signal still gets text, and the audio does not have to reach a server for the
words to exist. This stores what the phone produced.

**The transcript is the required half and the audio is optional**, which is the
opposite of what the name suggests and the right way round. The text is what a
report, a search and a reviewer read; the recording is evidence *about* that
record, kept so a challenge to the wording has something to check against. A
recording with no words attached is a file nobody on a farm will ever open.

A failed file link comes back as `audio_error` with the words **already saved**.
Losing a foreman's account of an accident because a file link failed would be the
wrong trade by a wide margin.

`source_type` and `source_language` are on every entry. A transcript presented as
a verbatim quote is being presented as something it is not, and a Spanish account
tagged as English is a translation nobody knows is needed.

## Progressive discipline

**In a wrongful-termination claim the documentation is the case.** The question a
tribunal asks is almost never whether the final step was deserved. It is whether
the employer can produce a documented, escalating, acknowledged series of steps
that told this person what was wrong, what had to change, by when, and what would
happen if it did not — and then whether somebody went back and looked.

```
Verbal Warning → Written Warning → Final Warning → Suspension → Termination
```

**The prior record is found, not asked for.** Whoever is typing has an employee in
front of them and a thing that just happened; asking them which docname the last
warning was is asking them to go and look, which is how a chain acquires a
missing link — and the missing link is what the claim is about.

**A skip has to be explained.** More than one rung at a time is refused until
`supersedes_note` says why:

```
Ana Ramos's last step was a Verbal Warning and this is a Suspension — 3 rungs up.
That may be entirely right; a safety violation is not a progressive matter. But
"why was this person's step everyone else's fourth" is asked every time, and the
answer is worth more written today than reconstructed in two years.
```

The same rung twice needs no explanation (a second written warning for a second
incident is ordinary), and neither does a step *down* — somebody being generous
never has to be defended.

**A refusal to sign is an outcome, not a gap.** An employee may decline. What the
file may not contain is silence presented as agreement, so an acknowledgement
with neither a signature nor an explicit refusal is refused, and a refusal with
no witness named is refused: a declining nobody saw is the employer's word for it.

### The report names the holes

`get_discipline_report` is what an HR manager hands a lawyer — and it is the same
argument `export_insurance_schedule` makes about missing serial numbers. A report
that only listed the steps leaves the reader to find what is missing, and what is
missing is what decides the case.

```
4 gap(s) in this chain. Each is listed above with what is missing and why it
matters. THESE ARE FIXABLE NOW AND NOT LATER: an acknowledgement obtained today
for a warning issued in March is worth something; the same signature obtained
after a claim is filed is worth very little.
```

Each gap carries a sentence: *"an undelivered warning is a note in a file rather
than a step in a chain, and this is the first thing a claim looks for."*
"Unacknowledged" means nothing to somebody who has not run a hearing.

It states plainly that it is not legal advice and does not say whether any step
was warranted — only whether the documentation of it is complete.

**Nothing expires on a schedule.** The look-back window that discounts an old
warning differs by employer and by state; a server quietly ageing steps out would
be making that decision for somebody. `expire_discipline_record` is the call an
operator makes when their policy says so, and the row is kept — a rescinded
warning is itself something an employee may point to, and a chain that had one
deleted cannot explain the gap where it was.

## Accident investigation

29 CFR 1904 is the regulation. **The design problem is not the regulation.**

The design problem is the first ten minutes. This record is opened on a phone, in
a block, by a foreman whose attention is on somebody sitting on the ground. What
they can give is: what happened, where, who was hurt, who saw it, what we did.
What they cannot give is a root cause, a 1904.7 determination or a corrective
action. Asking for those at the scene produces exactly one outcome — nobody opens
the record until the evening — and the evening's account is worth a fraction of
the scene's.

```
create_accident_report          what is known now
update_accident_investigation   what was learned since, as many times as it takes
close_accident_investigation    the corrective action, and the date to check it

Open → In Progress → Corrective Actions Pending → Closed
```

**Witnesses are rows, not a string.** A witness is somebody an investigator has to
go back to, and *we still have not interviewed Miguel* is the most useful thing a
half-finished investigation knows. A comma-separated string is accepted and split
into rows, so the outstanding-statement flag works either way.

**Recordability is a person's determination and this app does not infer it.** It
would be easy to map severity onto the 300 log and wrong: 1904.7 turns on medical
treatment beyond first aid, first aid is a *defined list*, and the consequence of
being wrong is a citation. `osha_recordable` defaults to `Undetermined`, changing
it requires the **basis** — *"sutures — medical treatment beyond first aid, under
1904.7(b)(5)"* is a determination an inspector can check; "No" on its own is one
they will ask about — and an investigation cannot close while it stands.

A fatality, hospitalisation, amputation or loss of an eye returns the 1904.39
telephone obligation in `urgent_obligations`. This record does not make that call
and cannot; the clock runs from when the employer *learned*, which only a person
knows.

**The narrative appends and the conclusions overwrite.** `root_cause` is a
conclusion and there is one of it; a revised conclusion replaces the earlier one,
and the earlier one survives in the narrative where it was written. *What did we
think on Tuesday* is a question a hearing asks.

**Closing is checked, and everything outstanding is named at once.** A close that
fails four times, each naming one more missing field, is how somebody learns to
stop closing things. `outstanding` is on every read and not just the close: an
investigation that tells you on day three what it is waiting for is one somebody
finishes.

## Wizards as data

**The problem is the App Store.** An onboarding flow compiled into Swift needs a
release to change a question — and the questions change when the law does, which
is on somebody else's schedule. A farm that discovers in July that its state now
requires a heat-illness acknowledgement at hire cannot wait three weeks for a
build and a review.

So a wizard is a **row**. `get_wizard_definition` returns ordered steps, the
fields on each with their types and validation, and the conditional logic that
decides what comes next. Field types are the **handset's** — `photo`,
`signature`, `qr_scan`, `audio_note`, `employee_select` — things a phone does and
a Desk form does not. A spec written in Frappe fieldtypes would be a spec for the
wrong device.

Five ship: `accident_investigation`, `progressive_discipline`,
`asset_registration`, `employee_onboarding`, `inspection_session`. **A shipped
wizard is never overwritten** once an operator has edited it. A flow somebody
tuned for their own crew being reset by the next `bench migrate` is what would
make "config not code" a slogan rather than a property.

**The validation is a courtesy, not a control.** It is there so a worker finds out
about a malformed VIN at the machine rather than at submission. The endpoint in
`submit_method` applies its own refusals to whatever arrives, because a
client-side rule is a suggestion to anybody holding the credential.

## Bilingual, and loud about the gaps

A farm workforce in this region works in English and Spanish, and a system that
works well in one of them works badly for half the crew.

Every label, title, description, help text and select option carries `_en` and
`_es`, resolved server-side against the caller's own **`Employee.preferred_language`**
— a new compliance field, and a compliance field rather than a preference:
OSHA 1910.1200(h) and WPS 40 CFR 170.501 both require training and hazard
communication *in a manner the employee can understand*, and an employer who
cannot say which language they used cannot show they did.

**Never inferred from a device locale.** A phone set to English by whoever handed
it over says nothing about who is holding it now, and getting this wrong silently
is exactly the failure the column exists to prevent.

**A missing translation falls back to English and is listed** in `untranslated`:

```
3 string(s) have no es translation and fell back to en. The worker will see
English for those. They are listed above so they can be filled in — a wizard half
in one language is worse than one that is consistently in the wrong one.
```

Silently serving English means nobody finds out until a worker is standing in
front of a screen they cannot read. Refusing to serve an untranslated wizard
locks the crew out of a flow over a missing string. Fall back, and be loud.

## What a scan says now

Two sections joined the status report.

`paused_tasks` is keyed on the **worker**, not the asset — it is a fact about the
person holding the phone:

```
You have a paused task: Irrigate Block 3 (paused 22 min ago)
```

It belongs on a scan because the scan is the moment they have forgotten the line.
The sentence is not on the screen of the job they walked away from; it is on the
screen in front of them now. *"You have a paused task"* is a notification; *"you
paused Irrigate Block 3 twenty-two minutes ago"* is a worker turning round.

`subtasks_by_parent` shows the steps of any open investigation on the asset — so
scanning the machine an accident happened on shows what is still outstanding.

## The nineteen routes, and why the gates differ

| Group | Gate | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pause` / `resume` | enrolment | A worker's own work |
| `link_tasks` | enrolment | An **observation**. Noticing two jobs are one valve is what a worker in a block sees and a foreman at a desk does not |
| `merge_task` | Foreman+ | A **decision**. It takes somebody's work off the board under another name |
| narrative trio | enrolment | A worker's account of their own job |
| five discipline routes | **HR role** | A personnel document. A field credential has no business in one |
| `create_accident_report` | enrolment | **The person who finds somebody on the ground is whoever finds them.** A server that refused their report because they are not a foreman is one people work around at the exact moment that matters |
| update / close / list accidents | Foreman+ | The investigation is somebody's job |
| wizard reads | enrolment | Answered in the caller's own language |

`expire_discipline_record` has **no route**. Ageing a step out of a chain is a
policy decision made at a desk with the handbook open, and a method with no route
404s — which is the whole design of that table.

## Migration

```bash
bench --site <site> migrate
```

Six new doctypes: `Discipline Record`, `Accident Report`, `Accident Witness`,
`Wizard Definition`, `Wizard Step`, `Wizard Field` — plus the three shared child
tables `Task Note`, `Task Time Segment` and `Farm Task Link`.

`Farm Task` gains `Paused` and `Merged` states, `parent_task`/`parent_doctype`,
`merged_into`, `linked_tasks` and `task_notes`, and its `notes` becomes a `Text`.
`Farm Task Assignment` gains `Paused` and `Merged`, the pause columns and
`time_segments`. `Employee` gains the `preferred_language` compliance field.

**Nothing is backfilled and nothing needs to be.** Every existing assignment has
an empty segment table and falls back to the wall-clock duration it has always
reported; every existing task has no parent, no links and no narrative, which is
the correct answer for work that was done before any of this existed.

The five wizards are seeded on migrate and **never overwritten** afterwards.

Twenty new tools — eight read, twelve write. The eight reads default ON; the
twelve writes default off, as every write in this app does.

## The tests

168 new assertions across four modules, and each opens with the claim it is
about rather than the happy path.

`test_task_interruption.py` asserts the hour survives the interruption, that the
auto-pause is announced rather than silent, that nothing merges itself, and that
a parent will not close while a step is live. `test_discipline.py` opens with the
chain linking itself and closes with the report naming its own gaps.
`test_accidents.py` asserts the scene call is short, that recordability is never
inferred from severity, and that closing names everything outstanding at once.
`test_wizards.py` asserts that an operator's edit survives a migrate and that a
missing translation is reported rather than silently served in English.
