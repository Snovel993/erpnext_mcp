# v0.64.1 — the row that would not go, and the form nobody could find

Two things a farm found by using them. Neither announced itself: one made a
correct compliance calendar look like decoration, and the other made a complete
federal record look like a missing one.

---

## 1. The compliance item that stayed on the phone

v0.64.0 opened with the right argument and closed most of the way:

> Hourly closed that from a day to an hour. This closes it to nothing.

`complete_farm_task` gained a narrowed sweep, so the rule a completion could have
changed re-runs at the moment the work is filed. The narrowing picks rules two
ways — the rule that **raised** the task, and every rule whose target register is
the record the completion **produced** — and the release note said of the second:

> Nobody had to link the task to the rule — the **record** is the link.

It was not. The second half was written as `produced_doctype in rule.requires`,
and `requires` is the doctypes a rule cannot **run** without, which for a
built-in scanner is the doctype it **scans**:

| the work produces | the rule that clears it | that rule's `requires` | matched? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housing Inspection | `housing_inspection_overdue` | `Housing Unit` | no |
| Detector Test | `housing_detector_test_stale` | `Housing Unit` | no |
| Water Test | `water_test_stale` | `Field` | no |

Those three registers reach their rule by **write-back** — recording an
inspection advances `Housing Unit.last_habitability_inspection`, which is what
makes the condition false, and `records.py` argues at length why that write-back
lives in the controller. So what a rule answers to is not derivable from
`requires`.

What *did* match were the rules that read the produced register to raise a **new**
problem — `housing_corrective_action_open`, `water_test_contamination`. A
habitability walk therefore re-ran the rule that opens a finding against it, and
never the rule whose alert had sent somebody to walk the cabin.

**`ALERT_TASK_MAP` is where this app already states the answer.** Each recipe
names the record that alert's own work produces; read backwards it is the map
from a produced record to the alerts that record discharges. It is the same table
the task was built from, so the two cannot drift.

Both tests are kept, because they are true of different rules.
`water_test_contamination` genuinely requires a Water Test and is genuinely worth
re-running when one is filed — it just is not the rule the task was raised by.

## 2. Two other doors that write compliance registers and swept nothing

`complete_farm_task` was the only caller of the narrowed sweep. Two paths that
move the same registers reached it only sideways, or not at all.

**A signature.** `collect_form_signature` fills the box and completes the Farm
Task the sweep raised — and the completion sweeps. Where there is no such task
nothing swept: nobody has run `generate_tasks_from_compliance_alerts`, which is a
manual tool that is **off by default**; or the task was closed by hand; or the
form was signed from the compliance tab rather than from the board. The row the
worker had just tapped came straight back on the next load and sat there for up
to an hour. It now re-runs the rules for its own box, narrowed by the box's own
`alert_types` — the same tuple `_task_for` matches on and `alert_answered_by`
computes a docname from, so a sixth signature box gets this for nothing.

**An inspection session.** A session is the multi-section version of one
afternoon: one walk round a cabin filing a Housing Inspection and a Detector Test
at their own cadences. Both are exactly what the two housing rules are discharged
through. The same work done as two Farm Tasks cleared both alerts on the spot;
done as a session it cleared neither. One farm, one afternoon, two answers
depending on which screen it was filed from.

Both use the same backwards read of `ALERT_TASK_MAP`, so the doors cannot come to
disagree about which rule a Housing Inspection answers.

**It is still the sweep, called sooner.** Nothing here dismisses an alert because
a signature landed or a session was filed; the rule re-runs and its own condition
decides. A form with two blank boxes has two alerts, and filling one leaves the
other standing.

## 3. `dismissed_alert`, read before the sweep rather than after

`alert_answered_by` finds the **open** alert a signature makes untrue. The three
fixes above make it stop being open **in the same call** — so a lookup performed
afterwards answered nothing on precisely the calls where the work landed and the
alert cleared, which is backwards.

The tool captures it at the moment it answers it and reports it as
`answered_alert`; `submit_form_signature` prefers that over a fresh lookup, and
keeps the lookup as the fallback for the already-signed branch, which sweeps
nothing. The meaning is unchanged and is still not a claim that anything was
dismissed: it names the row the phone should take off the tab it was tapped from.
Whether the sweep then agreed is `compliance_evaluation`'s business to report.

## 4. The completed I-9 that was invisible from the Employee

`seal_signed_document` attached the tamper-evident artefact to the **form**,
which is correct and was the whole of it. Nobody asks to see an I-9 Form: they
ask to see a worker's paperwork, open the Employee, and found nothing. The
sealed, hashed, retained document was one join away from the only place anybody
looks for it.

It is now cross-filed on the Employee the form names — **a second `File` row at
the same `file_url`**, which is what a cross-reference is. Two copies would be
two documents that can drift apart, and the one thing a tamper-evident artefact
must not do is exist twice under one hash.

- **Only the seal travels, never `generated_pdf`.** The working page is redrawn
  every time a signature lands, and a personnel folder accumulating each
  intermediate draw would bury the one document that matters under the four that
  were superseded.
- **A re-seal does not duplicate the link.** Re-sealing is a documented
  operation; a folder that grew a link per re-seal would be a changelog of one
  document. A re-seal after **new ink** is a different artefact with a different
  hash, and both are retained.
- **A form that names nobody is told so.** An employer return is signed by an
  officer and belongs in no personnel folder. `filed: false` with the reason is
  the honest answer; inventing a link would put a 941 in somebody's file.
- **It cannot undo the seal.** The artefact is written and hashed before this
  runs, and it inherits the ordering `tools/signatures.py` opens with: a
  cross-reference that could fail the call would trade the irreplaceable thing
  for the convenient one. A failure is reported in `employee_copy.reason`, with
  the `attach_file_to_document` call that files it without producing anything
  again.

---

## What this does not change

The hourly job is untouched and still does the work that matters most, because
most of what makes an alert stale is not anybody's tap on a phone. A certificate
lapsing at midnight is nobody's signature.

No DocType changed, no field was added, and no existing key changed meaning.
`employee_copy` on the seal and `compliance_evaluation` on the signature and the
session are additive.
