# v0.64.2 — complete means signed

A Form I-9 could reach the status `Complete` with both attestation boxes empty,
and carry a signing timestamp and an IP address for each of the signatures
nobody had made.

---

## 1. What "Complete" was worth

`submit_i9_section_2` ended with `doc.status = "Complete"`, unconditionally. The
signature was optional on the way in and never checked on the way out:

```
section_1_signature: None
section_1_signed_at: '2026-07-24 09:03:36'
section_2_signature: None
section_2_signed_at: '2026-07-24 09:03:42'
status:              'Complete'
```

Two things are wrong with that record and they are not the same thing.

**The status.** A Form I-9 is not complete because its fields are full. Section 1
is the employee's attestation under their own penalty of perjury and Section 2 is
the employer's that it examined that worker's documents; 8 CFR § 274a.2(b)(1)
asks for the attestations. What the app had was a **detective** control — the
missing-signature rules raise two Criticals on exactly this form — and the whole
value of a status called Complete is that somebody can read it without running a
compliance sweep first.

**The timestamps.** `section_1_signed_at` and `section_2_signed_at` are the
8 CFR § 274a.2(h) record of *when the attestation was made*. Filled in for an
attestation nobody made, they are a record asserting more than it knows, and they
assert it in the direction that matters: an inspector reading a timestamp and an
IP has been told somebody signed. A blank column is a missing signature; a
stamped one beside an empty signature column is a contradiction nobody can
explain a year later.

There was a second edge to the same line. Where the **pad** collected Section 2
first and `submit_i9_section_2` ran afterwards, that call overwrote
`section_2_signed_at` with `now()` — replacing the moment the person drew the
signature with the moment somebody typed the rest of the form.

---

## 2. What it does now

**The moment is written only where a signature is.** Both sections, one rule: if
this call is writing ink, it stamps the moment and the address; otherwise it
leaves them alone, including where a signature is already on the record from the
pad.

**`Complete` requires both attestations.** With either missing, the form rests at
`Awaiting Verification` — a status this tool already ACCEPTS as input, so nothing
is stuck — and `unsigned` names what is outstanding in the words a refusal uses.

**The documents are still filed.** Refusing the call outright would throw away
the examination somebody actually performed: which documents were produced, by
whom, on what date, inside the three-business-day window this function has
already checked. That work is real and it is written; what is withheld is the
claim that the form is finished.

**And the signature completes the form.** `i9.advance_if_signed` moves
`Awaiting Verification` → `Complete` when the last outstanding attestation lands,
and `collect_form_signature` calls it. Without this the gate would be a trap: on
a handset the signature arrives at the PAD, not back through the submit call, so
the signature landing has to be the moment something notices.

It moves **one edge**. It will not advance a Draft, will not touch a form
somebody set to Reverification Needed, Expired or Destroyed, will not reopen a
Complete one, and will not advance a form whose Section 2 was never filed
(`verification_date` is what says so). A status machine a signature pad could
drive in any direction would not be one. It never raises: the capture is already
on the record and is the irreplaceable artefact.

The detective control is **kept**. A form parked at `Awaiting Verification`
because nobody signed it is exactly what `i9_section_1_unsigned` and
`i9_section_2_unsigned` exist to chase. This adds a gate in front of them rather
than replacing them.

---

## 3. Two signers on one device — already supported, now asserted

The question this release started from was whether the phone owner can sign
Section 2 on the same handset the employee signed Section 1 on. The server has
supported it since v0.60.0 and nothing needed changing:

| | Section 1 | Section 2 |
|---|---|---|
| capacity | `Employee` | `Employer Representative` |
| roster gate | **none** — the worker signs for themselves | the caller must be an authorized signer for this company |
| badge | must resolve to the form's own employee, or refused | legitimately not the employee's; identifies the verifier |

Requiring the account holding the phone to be an authorized signer in order to
collect a *worker's* signature would conflate the two roles § 274a keeps apart,
which is why three of the five boxes carry no `form_type` and are not gated at
all. `_evidence_role` refuses a pad that opened Section 1 and posted `employer`,
so the capacity cannot be mislabelled in either direction.

One authenticated mobile session, two calls, and the result is two Signing
Evidence rows with distinct capacities and both blocks on the sealed verification
page. `test_ios_contract.test_20a` is that sequence end to end.

---

## 4. What this asks of the app

`test_20` in the iOS contract suite is left asserting `Awaiting Verification` on
purpose, because that is what the wizard's own calls produce today.
`OnboardingWizardViewModel` posts the Section 1 and Section 2 **data** and
neither attestation: Section 1's capture goes up as a loose PNG attached to the
Employee — which is not the column any rule reads — and Section 2's is drawn on
the pad, shown as "Captured" on the Review screen, and never sent anywhere at
all. `submit_form_signature` is called only from the compliance tab, which is
reached *after* the sweep has already raised a Critical.

The app needs two calls it is not making, both to a route that has been published
since v0.57.0:

1. after `submitI9Section1`, `submit_form_signature` with
   `signature_field: "section_1_signature"`, the ink, and the worker's
   `signer_badge` + `verification_method: "Badge QR"` — the badge is what makes
   the evidence row `Recorded` rather than `Unverified`;
2. after `submitI9Section2`, the same call with
   `signature_field: "section_2_signature"` and the phone owner's ink.

The second answers with `form_status_advanced_to: "Complete"`, which is the
signal a "the I-9 is done" screen should read — `form_status` says what the form
reads now, this says whether *this* signature is what moved it. The Review step
already knows whether both captures exist; it does not yet block on them.
