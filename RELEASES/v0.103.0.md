# v0.103.0 — the card says where somebody belongs

Tim, on the badges the pickers actually carry: *"job title, assigned crew, and
camp and cabin number. That would finish off things nicely I think."*

No new tool, no new doctype, no new column. Two lookups, three lines on a card,
and the same three facts on every surface a badge already had.

---

## What was already on the card, and what was not

The job title was there. `_EMPLOYEE_FIELDS` has read `designation` since v0.50.0
and `.bc-role` has printed it since v0.56.0.

The other two were not, and the reason is worth stating because it is the whole
shape of this release: **neither is on the Employee record.**

```
  what a badge should say        where the farm actually keeps it
  ─────────────────────────      ──────────────────────────────────────────
  job title                      Employee.designation                 ✓ had it
  assigned crew                  Employee.reports_to, or a Farm Shift
  camp and cabin                 Housing Assignment → Housing Unit → Parcel
```

So the card had to go and ask two more registers, and everything below is about
asking them in a way that cannot cost anybody a badge.

---

## The crew line, and why the durable answer wins

A card is printed once and then lives in a back pocket through a cherry harvest.
The crew line therefore has to be a fact that is **still true in September**.

`badges._crew` asks four things in this order:

| order | source | why it is where it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Employee.reports_to` | A picker who reports to Ramirez is on Ramirez's crew for as long as the record says so. This is the standing fact. |
| 2 | `Employee.department` | The durable grouping where nobody is named above them. Printed as `department_name`, never as the `Harvest - ETC` docname. |
| 3 | `Employee.branch` | The same, one rung wider. |
| 4 | the one open `Farm Shift` | One morning's roster. A stopgap, and reported as one. |

The obvious build puts the Farm Shift first — it is the most concrete answer to
"whose crew is this person on **today**". It was not built that way, because a
card that printed `SHIFT-2026-0114`'s foreman would be wrong by Wednesday and
unfixable without a reprint.

`crew_source` comes back alongside `crew` for exactly this reason: a caller that
cares about the difference between *"this is who they work for"* and *"this is
who they happened to be with when the card was printed"* can tell them apart
without guessing.

**Ambiguity prints nothing.** Two open shifts naming one person is a roster
somebody has to fix; `dispatch._open_shift_for` already refuses to guess between
them, and that refusal applies twice over to a card that cannot be corrected once
it is laminated.

## The camp and the cabin

`badges._housing` reads the worker's current Housing Assignment and prints
`<parcel> · <unit_name>` — *Mill Creek · Cabin 7*.

**The camp is the parcel.** `create_housing_unit` numbers a unit *within* a
parcel and refuses a second unit of the same name on it, so a farm with two camps
can perfectly well have a Cabin 3 on each. Either half alone is ambiguous to
somebody walking a new starter to their bunk at six in the morning.

**Current means no `end_date`**, which is `housing._current_assignments`' own
definition rather than a second one invented here. `status` is a Select an
operator maintains by hand and `end_date` is what `end_housing_assignment`
actually writes — a card that trusted the Select would print a cabin somebody
moved out of in June. Both are checked; either one saying *ended* is enough.

Where a worker holds two open assignments the **newest wins**. Unlike the crew
line there is no honest way to print nothing: somebody sleeps somewhere, and the
newest assignment is the best guess at where.

## Neither lookup may lose a badge

`_company_logo` set this posture in v0.50.0 and both new lookups keep it. A site
that has not migrated the camp register, a worker who has never been rostered, an
HR app whose Employee has no `reports_to` column — each is **a card printed
without that line**, never a badge nobody can issue.

What goes wrong at a shed door on a Monday morning is a picker with no card. No
cabin number is worth risking one for, so:

* every branch of both functions returns a value; none raises;
* `render/badge_card.py` calls them inside a guard of their own, so if they fail
  the photograph, the name and the QR still print;
* that module now filters its Employee column list through the doctype meta
  before reading, because three optional columns making `get_value` raise on the
  whole list would have cost the card its face.

## Three fixed slots, and why they do not close up

```
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  ▓▓ Example Trading Co        EMPLOYEE BADGE│
  │  ┌────────┐  Ana Reyes                ┌────┐│
  │  │        │  Picker                   │ ▞▚ ││
  │  │  photo │  Crew: Rosa Ramirez       │ ▚▞ ││
  │  │        │  Camp: Mill Creek · Cabin 7└────┘│
  │  └────────┘  BADGE                  ETC-0001│
  │              ETC-0001                        │
  │  No. E-100                       HR-EMP-00001│
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The tempting build gives each line the next free millimetre, so a worker with no
cabin gets a tighter card. It was not built that way. The positions are absolute
millimetres for the reason `CARD_CSS` has always given — wkhtmltopdf lays flex
out a quarter-millimetre out on a card — and a conditional stack would need the
same arithmetic written twice, in Python for the sheet and in Jinja for the Print
Format. Those two would drift. **A blank slot is whitespace on a card; two
layouts that disagree by 3mm is a badge whose photograph moves depending on which
button somebody pressed.**

Each line clips rather than wraps (`.bc-line`). A designation like *Equipment
Operator (Class II)* would otherwise take a second line and push into the badge
ID, which is the one thing on the front that has to stay readable. Clipped is
legible; overlapped is not.

`test_badge_print_format.py` now checks the stack arithmetically — each line's
top plus its own rendered height has to clear the next line's top, and the badge
ID has to clear the footer rule — so a future nudge to a font size cannot quietly
print one line through another.

## Every surface, not just the one card

The facts are resolved once, in `badges._card`, and everything downstream reads
the same dict:

* `generate_employee_badge_qr` — the card data, plus `crew_source`, `shift`,
  `camp`, `cabin`, `unit` and `housing_assignment` as separate keys, so a handset
  linking to a Housing Assignment need not parse `"Mill Creek · Cabin 7"` back
  apart;
* `generate_employee_badge_sheet` — the same on every card of a hiring day's
  sheet;
* `generate_employee_id_card` — the same, through the same `badge_sheet` layout;
* the **Employee Badge Card** Print Format on the Desk, through
  `erpnext_mcp_badge_card`;
* the **wallet pass** a worker carries instead of the card: crew joins role in
  Apple's secondary fields, the camp joins the company in the auxiliary row, and
  both appear as Google text modules. A fact the site does not record is left off
  rather than shown as a labelled blank.

## Tests

`tests_standalone/test_badges.py` gains `TheCardSaysWhereSomebodyBelongs` — all
four crew sources and the ordering between them, the two-open-shifts refusal, an
ended assignment, an assignment ended by Select alone, the camp falling back to
the unit's own parcel, and the negative control that a site recording neither
still issues the badge.

`test_badge_print_format.py`, `test_badge_sheet.py` and `test_wallet_passes.py`
each gain the labelled lines, the negative control that no label is drawn with
nothing after it, and — for the print format — the geometry arithmetic and the
proof that a failing lookup does not cost the photograph.
