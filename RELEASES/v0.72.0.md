# v0.72.0 — the crew's board, in the hands of the person who runs the crew

Five routes, no new tool code, and the first five paths on the mobile table that
an enrolled Field Worker cannot call.

---

## The gap

Sprint 8 built a dispatch board. `api/mobile.py` has published the *worker's*
half of it since v0.17.1 — see your tasks, see the pool, claim one, start it,
finish it with the evidence its contract demands, hand it back with a reason.
Every one of those is a picker acting on their own work.

Nothing on that surface answered the other half. A foreman standing in a block at
two in the afternoon could not ask:

* what is my crew holding right now;
* can I take this job off Ana, who is on the packing line until four, and give it
  to Diego, who is fifty metres from it;
* the gate latch is broken — can I raise that as work now, with the photograph
  requirement on it, rather than remembering it at six.

The tools to answer all three have existed since Sprint 8. They were reachable
from the MCP transport, behind an operator's own per-tool enablement switch, and
from nowhere a handset can reach. An iOS audit against v0.71.0 found no wrapper
and no route for any of the five.

## The eighth gate

`api/guard.py` has documented seven checks since v0.17.1: the kill switch, a
named human, the role gate, the enrolment gate, the rate limit, the work with
every docname and company validated, and the audit row. All seven run on every
call to every method.

**This release adds an eighth, and it is deliberately not in the decorator.**

```python
guard.DISPATCH_ROLES = frozenset({"Foreman", "Farm Manager"})
guard.require_dispatch_role(user, "Dispatching a task to somebody")
```

It is called in each of the five wrappers' own bodies, because it is not true of
the surface — it is true of five methods on it. A picker claiming their own work,
filing a completion or photographing a receipt is the ordinary case this API
exists for, and gating all seventy methods on Foreman would close the app to the
people it was built for.

**The two role names are `dispatch._CRITICAL_ROLES`'s, not a third list.**
`dispatch.py` already draws exactly this line, for Critical urgency on a field
report, with exactly this argument: every field worker believing their problem is
Critical is how Critical stops meaning anything, and every field worker deciding
who does the work is that failure with somebody else's afternoon in it.
`fill_pipeline.FOREMAN_ROLES` adds System Manager, which is right there and
pointless here — the enrolment gate has already refused an operator's own
account, grant or no grant.

**The refusal is worded, and the role gate above it is not.** `_require_farm_ops_role`
answers everything with one sentence so an unauthorised caller cannot learn which
gate they failed. This one names the roles and lists the seven routes a picker
*may* call, because the caller has already proved a named login, a field role and
an Active grant — that dispatch is a foreman's is not a secret being leaked, it
is the one thing they need to be told to stop tapping the button.

**Why the gate is in the wrapper and not delegated.** The onboarding methods
delegate: `tools/employee.py` and `tools/i9.py` each call `require_hr_role()`
before they write, and `api/mobile.py` refuses to copy that up rather than keep
two sets of personnel rules in step. These five tools **have no role check at
all**. What stands in front of them on the MCP transport is the operator's own
`allow_assign_farm_task` switch, and a phone does not go through that switch.
Publishing them without a gate would put *take this job off Ana and give it to
me* on every enrolled handset in the orchard.

## The crew scope

`dispatch.list_dispatched_tasks` takes `worker_id` and reads that worker's
assignments. It will read anybody's — "which worker" is the whole of its
argument, and on the MCP transport that is correct.

On a handset it is not a scope. An account able to name anybody is an account
able to walk the payroll one docname at a time and read what every person on the
farm is doing today.

So **the workers are computed, not accepted**:

1. the shifts this caller is the `foreman` of and which are OPEN — `end_datetime`
   unset, which is `shifts.status_for`'s own rule rather than the stored `status`
   column, and not `cancelled`;
2. every crew member rostered on them;
3. the caller, whether or not they rostered themselves.

`employee` may narrow that set and nothing else. A name that is not on it is
**refused by name** rather than answered, because a foreman who typed the wrong
docname and a foreman who is probing are told different things by an empty list
and the same thing by a refusal.

**`worker_id` is not a parameter.** `routes.bind` reduces a body to the keys a
signature declares, so a client sending the tool's own spelling has it dropped
and gets the whole crew — which is the direction this fails safely in, while the
refusal above makes the other direction loud.

**A left `left_at` does not remove somebody from the board.** Whoever clocked out
at noon still holds whatever they were sent to that morning, and dropping them is
how an unfinished job stops being anybody's. The field is reported on every crew
entry instead, so the dashboard can grey the row rather than lose it.

**A foreman with no open shift gets their own board and a sentence saying why.**
Not an empty answer, which reads as "no work today", and not an unscoped one.

## What the three writes will not accept

| Argument | Why it stops here |
|---|---|
| `assigned_to_name` | The tools write it onto the task **and** the assignment in place of the name the Employee register holds. A dispatch record that can be made to say somebody was sent who was not is not a dispatch record. |
| `creates_record`, `creates_record_data` | Which compliance record completing the task produces, and the fields pre-filled into it. This is `record_data` under another name, and the answer is `complete_task_via_mobile`'s: the phone has no business composing it. |
| `draft` | A task raised from a handset that lands in Draft is invisible to every other handset — the foreman believes they dispatched something and nobody can see it. Everything raised here is published. |
| `source_alert` | One task per alert is a rule with a refusal behind it, and `rectify_alert` is the route that owns that link. |
| `materials_used` | The tank mix `complete_farm_task` draws down out of stock. A spray task's mix is decided before anybody drives anywhere, which is what a template is for. |

That last row is why both template routes are in the same set: work that has to
produce a compliance record comes off a template, where the evidence contract,
the record it builds and the checklist were all decided at a desk.

**`reassign` and `reason` are forwarded, not restated.** Taking work off somebody
already holding it needs both, and that refusal stays in `dispatch.py` where the
sentence written onto the closed assignment is composed. It is not copied up the
way `reject_task` copies its own, because this refusal is CONDITIONAL: `reassign`
means nothing on a task nobody holds, and a wrapper demanding a reason for
dispatching unclaimed work would refuse the ordinary case to guard the rare one.

## The three template tools with no route

`get_farm_task_template`, `create_farm_task_template` and
`update_farm_task_template`. Reading one template in full is what the list
already carries enough of; **authoring** the shape of a recurring job — its
evidence contract, the record it builds, its checklist — is a decision made at a
desk with the regulation open, not at a tailgate.

A method with no route 404s, which is the whole design of `farmops_api/routes.py`,
and the both-directions assertion in `test_farmops_api.py` is what keeps that a
decision rather than an omission.

## Tests

Nineteen new cases in `TheForemanDashboard` (`tests_standalone/test_farmops_api.py`),
driven over the real WSGI stack rather than in process:

* every one of the five refused 403 for an enrolled Field Worker holding a
  perfect credential, an Active grant and the right entity;
* the refusal names the seven routes that *are* a picker's;
* the board is the crew on the caller's own open shift — Ana in, the foreman in,
  a stranger out;
* a crew name narrows it, a non-crew name is refused, another foreman's shift is
  refused;
* `worker_id` in the body neither narrows nor widens, and is not in
  `accepted_arguments`;
* dispatch of unclaimed work; the two-step refusal of a reassignment and the
  reassignment that satisfies it; `assigned_to_name` cannot be written from the
  body; an Employee of another entity reads as not found;
* a task raised from the handset is Available with its contract on it, one raised
  with no contract is refused by name, and the five forbidden arguments have no
  parameter to land in;
* the template register answers and one task raised from a template carries the
  template's shape.

Full suite: 8100 tests, green.
