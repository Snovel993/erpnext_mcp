# v0.66.0 — the nouns everything else is written in

Nineteen tools over the master data every other document points at, and one word
that means three different things.

---

## The gap was one sentence long

This app could list a purchase order, age a receivable into buckets, post a
journal entry and file the evidence for it. It could not name the chemical on the
order.

Every one of those documents is written in terms of records this app had no way
to create: an Item, a Supplier, a Customer, a Warehouse, an Item Group. So every
workflow that ended in a document ended instead at *somebody open the Desk and
create the master first* — which is not a workflow, it is a handoff, and it lands
on the one person who was supposed to be freed up by the rest of the catalogue.

Sprint 1 of the gap-closure plan closes that. Nineteen tools: ten reads, nine
writes. They wrap stock ERPNext doctypes, so all nineteen carry
`available=_app_installed("erpnext")` and go unavailable **by name** on a
Frappe-only bench rather than failing inside a query.

| Doctype | Read | Write |
|---|---|---|
| Item | `list_items`, `get_item` | `create_item`, `update_item` |
| Item Group | `list_item_groups` | `create_item_group` |
| Supplier | `list_suppliers`, `get_supplier` | `create_supplier`, `update_supplier` |
| Customer | `list_customers`, `get_customer` | `create_customer`, `update_customer` |
| Warehouse | `list_warehouses` | `create_warehouse` |
| Prices | `list_price_lists`, `get_item_price` | `set_item_price` |

## The word that means three things

This is the release's one real idea, and it is the thing to read before calling
anything here.

A model that has learned "pass `company` to scope the answer" from the rest of
this catalogue will pass it to all nineteen of these. On these doctypes it cannot
mean the same thing three times, because ERPNext does not store it the same way
three times:

**A Warehouse really is company-scoped.** `Warehouse.company` is a column. The
docname carries the abbreviation — `Chemical Shed - OML`. Filtering is exact, and
this is the one place where the obvious reading is the right one.

**An Item is not.** ERPNext moved per-company defaults out of `Item` and into the
`item_defaults` child table in v12. An Item with **no default row at all** is
usable by every company on the site. So `list_items(company=…)` cannot mean "this
company's catalogue" — it means "the items this company has set a default for",
and everything else disappears from the answer.

That is the dangerous one, because the failure is a *shorter list*, and a shorter
list looks exactly like a complete one. So the tool applies the filter and says
what it did:

```json
{"count": 1,
 "company_scope": "company was applied as 'has an Item Default row for Orchard Meadow, LLC'. An ERPNext Item is NOT company-scoped — an item with no default row at all is usable by every company on this site, and is not in this list. Omit company to see all items."}
```

**A Supplier and a Customer are neither.** Stock ERPNext puts no company column
on either; they are site-wide. Three options were available and two of them are
wrong:

- *Refuse the call.* Obstructive over an argument that costs nothing.
- *Drop it silently.* The failure mode this whole app is built against — the
  caller believes they narrowed the answer and they did not.
- *Validate it and report it as not applied.* A company that does not exist is
  still a mistake worth hearing about, whether or not this record could have held
  it.

The third is what shipped, in `company_scope` on every list and every create.

## Nothing here is a draft, and saying so is the feature

The brief for `create_item` said *creates as draft*. It cannot: none of these
doctypes is submittable. There is no docstatus on an Item, no draft state to
create it in and no submit step to promise later. It is live the moment it is
inserted.

Everywhere else in this app, a create tool that says "draft" means a docstatus 0
document that a separate, separately-switched tool submits — so a reader who
carried that expectation here would carry it into a purchase they believed was
still uncommitted. Every create in this release therefore returns

```json
{"submittable": false,
 "note": "An Item has no docstatus and therefore no draft state: this one is live. Pass disabled to keep it out of transactions."}
```

`disabled` is the nearest real equivalent, it is a real field on Item, Supplier,
Customer and Warehouse, and `create_item` will set it on request.

## A reorder level belongs to a warehouse

`update_item` takes `reorder_level` and `reorder_qty`, and ERPNext stores them on
an `Item Reorder` row **keyed by the warehouse they apply to**. "Reorder at 50"
with no shed named is not a thing the doctype can hold.

So the tool resolves the warehouse from `reorder_warehouse`, then from the item's
own default, and refuses with that sentence when there is neither — rather than
writing the rule against whichever warehouse sorted first, which would be a
correct-looking rule on the wrong building. A second write against the same
warehouse updates that row instead of adding a second one.

## Predicting the docname before writing

`create_warehouse` borrows the mechanism `create_account` uses. ERPNext names a
Warehouse `"<warehouse_name> - <company abbr>"`, so the docname is computable
before the insert — and computing it is what turns a duplicate into

> a Warehouse named 'Stores - OML' already exists. ERPNext names warehouses
> '\<name\> - \<company abbr\>', so this name is taken in this company. Nothing
> was created.

rather than a framework uniqueness error naming a column. The same mechanism is
why two companies can each have a `Stores` and neither collides.

## `set_item_price` matches on the whole key

A price is not a field on an Item. It is an `Item Price` row: one per item per
price list per UOM, optionally narrowed to one customer or supplier and to a date
window. So a "set" tool has to decide whether it is creating or updating, and the
key it decides on is the whole of `(item, price_list, uom, customer, supplier,
valid_from)` — which is what ERPNext's own duplicate check uses.

Matching on the item and the list alone would find a customer's negotiated rate
and overwrite it with the list rate. And when the key matches **more than one**
row, the tool refuses and names them: that site has duplicates ERPNext would have
refused, and picking one of them silently is how a negotiated rate disappears
with a success message on top of it.

`get_item_price` has the mirror of the same restraint. `price_list_rate` is
filled **only** when exactly one row applies; two rows and no date is not a
price, and choosing between them is ERPNext's pricing-rule engine's job, not this
tool's. Pass `as_of` and `applicable` narrows to the rows whose window covers
that date — with an open `valid_upto` treated as still in force, which is what
most rows on a real site look like — while the full list stays, because "what was
it in March" and "what prices exist" are both questions somebody asks.

## Three switches nobody could reach

Not part of the sprint, found while adding to the form. `allow_get_available_actions`,
`allow_list_asset_state_history` and `allow_log_asset_state_change` were present
in the settings DocType's `fields` and absent from its `field_order`, so the form
never rendered them. The parity test that would have caught it checks that every
tool HAS a switch, not that the switch has a place to appear — a control an
operator cannot reach is not a control. All three now sit beside their siblings.

## What the suite covers

`tests_standalone/test_masters.py`, 112 tests. The three classes worth reading
first are `ItemCompanyScope`, `PartyCompanyScope` and the company test in
`Warehouses`: one per meaning of the word, including the empty-set trap — a
company with no item defaults must get **nothing**, not everything, because a
filter built from an empty list that falls through to "no filter" answers a
question about one company with another's catalogue.

The double grew to match: `Item`'s two child tables, `Customer`, `Warehouse`,
`Price List`, `Item Price`, the party group and territory trees, and a
`WarehouseDocument` controller reproducing ERPNext's `autoname` — without which
the predicted docname, the collision check and the name the response hands back
would all be fictions at once. `Supplier` and `Customer` are deliberately left
with **no** company column, because that is what makes the "reported as not
applied" behaviour testable rather than decorative.
