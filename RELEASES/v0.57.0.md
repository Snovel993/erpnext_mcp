# v0.57.0 — the row you can do something about

v0.55.0 taught this app to find the boxes nobody signed. Four rules watch a
column rather than a clock, the dispatch board puts each finding on the phone of
somebody who can fix it, and one call files the capture.

What the compliance calendar did with all of that was print a sentence.

> **I-9 Section 1 was completed but carries no employee signature — Critical**
> Section 1 must be signed no later than the first day of employment.

A foreman can read that and not act on it, and the reason is not indifference.
The pad that fixes it lives behind a different tab, findable only by knowing
which Farm Task the sweep raised and that it was a signature task at all. The
docname is not in the sentence. Deriving one from the employee's name is how ink
lands on the wrong person's form.

So a row is now a tap, and **where it lands is the alert's own doing.**

---

## 1. The alert carries the address, not just the prose

A missing-signature alert now publishes a `signature_request` — the object
`API_CONTRACT.md` §14.1 describes, the same one a Farm Task carries:

```json
{
  "doctype": "I-9 Form",
  "docname": "I9-2026-0007",
  "signature_field": "section_1_signature",
  "signer_role": "employee",
  "form_label": "Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification",
  "section_label": "Section 1. Employee Information and Attestation",
  "attestation": "I am aware that federal law provides for imprisonment and/or fines for false statements…",
  "employee": "HR-EMP-00042",
  "employee_name": "Juan Lopez",
  "printed_name": "Juan Lopez"
}
```

The first three keys are the address and the rest is what the person holding the
phone is shown before they draw on it. A request missing any of the three is not
a signature route: the app falls through to the linked task, and then to the
alert's own detail. That is the right failure — a pad addressed at nothing
collects a signature twice, once into the void and again wherever the form
really lives.

### The server owns every word the signer reads

`attestation`, `form_label` and `section_label` are on the box, not on the
phone, for the same reason `source_alert_explanation` is: the person signing and
the auditor reading the file should be looking at the same sentence, and a
handset should not be paraphrasing 8 CFR § 274a.2. Where the server is silent
the app falls back — the raw doctype, the humanised fieldname — and where it has
nothing it omits the block rather than inventing what a form attests.

**The name under the ruled line follows the role, not the form.** On Section 1
it is the employee's, because they are the one attesting. On Section 2 it is the
verifier's, read off the record's own column, because printing the employee's
name there would be a claim about who examined the documents — on the one
document where that is the whole question. Where nobody is named yet the key is
simply absent.

### One role the app has no caption for, on purpose

The employer's tax return carries `signer_role: "officer"` and
`signer_label: "Signature of officer or authorized agent"`. The app has captions
for four roles and `officer` is not one of them — §14.1a keeps an unrecognised
role verbatim, shows it humanised, and posts it back unchanged, and `signer_label`
wins over anything the app would compose. A 941 is signed by an officer of the
employer, not by the "authorized representative" the I-9's caption names, and
this is the difference travelling correctly with no client release. `form_label`
for that box is read off `form_type`, so a signer is told whether they are
signing a 941, an OR-WR, an OQ or a WA-ESD.

### Derived, not stored, and the reason is the table it comes from

`signature_request` is computed when the calendar is read, off
`tools/signatures.SIGNATURE_BOXES` — **the same closed table
`collect_form_signature` gates its writes on.** Three consequences, in ascending
order of how much they matter:

* an alert raised by v0.55.0 and still open gets its address the moment the site
  upgrades. No patch, no sweep, no migration of alert rows;
* the address is already a fact about the rule and the record — `alert_key` is
  built from exactly the pair this reads back — so a copy stored on the alert
  would be a second one to keep in step;
* **a pad can only be opened at a column this app would accept ink into.** A
  request composed anywhere else could name a box the write path refuses, and
  the failure would be silent: a signature collected into nothing.

Farm Tasks raised from these alerts now carry the same object, read off the same
alert. The pad opened from the Available tab and the pad opened from the
compliance tab are addressed identically, because there is one source for the
address rather than two.

## 2. `dismiss_compliance_alert` — the one door out that is not "do the work"

Compliance Alert grows one column, `can_dismiss`, and it **defaults false**.

That default is the design rather than caution. An overdue housing inspection is
not a notification: waving it off from a handset leaves a cabin uninspected and
the calendar quiet about it, which is precisely why the mobile surface shipped
with no dismiss at all. But some alerts genuinely are stale — one raised against
a lease terminated in May, a duplicate of a filing already made elsewhere — and
only somebody who can see the whole picture knows which. So the permission is
granted per alert, in advance, by ticking **May Be Dismissed From The Field** on
the Compliance Alert, and the button exists only where it was granted.

| | `dismiss_alert` | `dismiss_compliance_alert` |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | none | refuses unless `can_dismiss` |
| For | somebody at a desk with the source record open in the next tab | a handset in an orchard, a model reading a calendar |
| Reason | mandatory, a sentence | mandatory, a sentence |
| Record | `dismissed_by` / `dismissed_on` / `dismissed_reason` | the same columns, through the same code |

`dismiss_alert` is unchanged and stays ungated. Gating it would be a breaking
change wearing a feature's clothes, and the operator it serves is not the caller
this gate is about.

**The sweep never writes `can_dismiss`.** It neither grants the permission nor
takes it away — the same treatment a snooze gets, and for the same reason: it is
a judgement a person made about this alert, and the sweep does not get to
overrule them by looking at the record again.

**The gate is enforced on the server, not on the phone.** The app hides its
Dismiss button where `can_dismiss` is false and its own contract calls that UI
courtesy; a refusal that exists only in a client is not one.

The reason is the entire audit trail for an obligation nobody discharged, so it
is required, a single word is refused, and it is written by the same function
both routes use — an auditor asking "who decided this did not need doing" gets
one answer with one shape whichever door was used.

## 3. `submit_form_signature` — the route the pad was already posting to

v0.55.0 published the signature write as `collect_signature`, which declares
`field` and `signature_base64`. The app was written against §14.2, which sends
`signature_field` and `signature_image`. `farmops_api/routes.bind` reduces a
request body to the keys the handler's signature declares — that filter is what
makes `record_data` and `worker_id` unreachable from a phone — so the contract's
own body arrived at the v0.55.0 method with **neither the field name nor the
picture in it**, and was told the signature was missing.

Both methods now exist. Neither grows a second spelling of an argument, and a
handset already in the field is not asked to change to get an answer.

The new one answers §14.3:

```json
{
  "doctype": "I-9 Form", "docname": "I9-2026-0007", "field": "section_1_signature",
  "form_status": "Complete", "file_url": "/private/files/…",
  "task": "FT-2026-00071", "task_state": "Completed", "task_completed": true,
  "signed_on": "2026-08-10 09:14:22", "already_signed": false,
  "dismissed_alert": "CA-2026-00339"
}
```

**`dismissed_alert` is not a claim that an alert was dismissed.** Nothing here
dismisses one; the sweep does that by looking at the record again and finding the
box filled, which is the only honest way for an alert to go away. What this names
is the row the phone should take off the tab it was tapped from, found by key —
an alert's docname is derived from its rule and its record, so the alert a
signature answers is computable rather than queryable.

**It is idempotent, which §14.4 asks for by name.** A submission whose answer
never made it back over a marginal link gets retried, and a worker shown an error
for a signature that already landed is a worker who signs again. A box that
already carries an attestation answers success with `already_signed: true` and
**nothing is overwritten** — replacing an attestation somebody made under penalty
of perjury is a deliberate act with an `overwrite` flag on it, and that flag is
not reachable from this route.

**`task` travels and closes the work in the same transaction.** The alert the
phone tapped carries the Farm Task the sweep raised, and routing around the task
list must not route around closing the task. Closing still goes through
`complete_farm_task`, which refuses a completion filed by somebody who was not
holding the work — that refusal is *reported* rather than fatal, because the
signature is the compliance artefact and the task is bookkeeping about it.

**`signed_on`, `image_format`, `gps_lat` and `gps_lon` are dropped**, by the
documented mechanism rather than by accident. The timestamp is the interesting
one: §14.2 stamps it when the pad opened, and the column beside the image is the
8 CFR § 274a.2(h) record of *when the attestation was made*. A handset that could
set it could backdate it, so the server stamps its own and answers with what it
wrote.

## 4. Nothing that was there has moved

Every addition is optional and additive, and there is a test class that says so
by name. An alert sent exactly as v0.55.0 sent it still lists, still sorts and
still opens its detail; `can_dismiss` reads false on a site whose column has not
migrated yet, which is the safe direction for a permission to fail; and the
twenty-four keys the calendar emitted before this release all still mean what
they meant.

## Upgrading

```bash
bench --site <site> migrate
```

That adds `can_dismiss` to Compliance Alert and seeds
`allow_dismiss_compliance_alert` — **off**, like every other write switch. Turn
it on to let the app's Dismiss button work at all; individual alerts still have
to be marked dismissible one at a time, which is the point.

`refresh_compliance_alerts` is worth running once afterwards, though nothing
needs it: the addresses appear on alerts already open, because they are worked
out when the calendar is read rather than when the alert was raised.
