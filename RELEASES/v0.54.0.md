# v0.54.0 — where they work, and where they sleep

Since v0.51.0 the handset has read a driver's licence at the tailgate, parsed the
AAMVA barcode, and matched the name against people who worked last season. What
it produced at the end of that was an Employee record carrying a name, a date of
birth and the company that employs them.

Which is most of a hire and none of an assignment. Nothing said which camp
somebody reports to, nothing said whether they were Seasonal or Permanent, and
nothing at all said which cabin they were sleeping in — so the foreman finished
the wizard, walked away, and the housing register got written that evening in
the office by somebody working from memory. Or did not.

Three new mobile methods finish the wizard's last two steps.

---

## 1. The four dropdowns are the site's own, not the app's

`list_onboarding_reference_data` returns **Branch, Department, Designation and
Employment Type in one call**.

This is exactly the failure `list_i9_document_types` removed from the I-9's
document picker in v0.47.0, and it has a sharper edge here. Those four are
**Link fields**: `create_employee` checks every one of them against *this site's*
records and refuses a value that names nothing. So a Swift array compiled into
the app is not merely stale — it is a wizard that collects five screens of
information and then fails on the last one with *"Picker is not a Designation on
this site"*, from a phone with no way to discover what is.

One call rather than four, because they are read together once when the step
opens, and four round trips over a tailgate LTE connection is four chances to
half-populate a form.

**A master this site does not have comes back empty and is named in
`masters_absent`** — never omitted, never an error. All four ship with Frappe HR;
a bench without hrms has none of them, and the honest answer there is a wizard
that offers no choices for that field rather than a hire that cannot start.
`create_employee` agrees: `_clean` does not check a Link whose target doctype is
absent, and never has.

Departments are scoped to the caller's entities — Department is the only one of
the four carrying a company on a stock Frappe HR — and **group departments are
dropped**, because `is_group` marks a node in the tree rather than somewhere a
person is assigned, and an Employee pointed at one double-counts in every report
that rolls up.

**It carries no HR role gate**, deliberately and unlike `search_employees`.
There is nothing on these rows about a person: a job title, a camp name, an
employment class. Gating them would mean the wizard's own dropdowns needed a role
the rest of the field surface does not.

---

## 2. `branch` is writable, which is why the step could not record it

`tools/employee.WRITABLE` carried `designation`, `department` and
`employment_type` — and not Frappe HR's own operating-unit field. So the
Assignment step could ask which camp somebody reports to and had nowhere to put
the answer.

The allowlist is **nineteen** now. It is still a closed list, written out, and
`branch` is checked the way its three neighbours are: a Link, against this site's
own Branch records, with the site's meta asked before anything is assumed.

The same field reaches three callers, because they are one piece of code:

* `create_employee` on the MCP registry
* `create_employee` on the mobile surface, which delegates to it
* `onboard_employee`, which delegates to it as well

**`onboard_employee` gained `department` and `employment_type` at the same
time.** It has taken `designation` since it shipped and neither of the other two,
which made *"what do they do"* answerable off an onboarding and *"what are they
hired as, and where"* not. `employment_type` is the one that matters most: it is
the field that says whether somebody is Seasonal, which is the fact an H-2A
roster, an ACA hours count and a piece-rate wage statement all turn on.

---

## 3. The camp has a read

`list_available_housing` is beds and bodies per unit — capacity, current
occupants, open beds, condition, and whether the unit can take anybody tonight.

**It counts occupants and does not name them.** `list_housing_units` returns an
`occupants` array of employee names, and who sleeps in which cabin is a personnel
fact that has no business on a picker's phone merely because the vacancy count
does. The count is what fills a dropdown; the names are read in the Desk, or
through `get_employee_housing_history` on the MCP console. That split is the
whole reason this read carries no HR role gate where `assign_housing` does.

**Shower blocks and shops are not in the answer at all.** They are Housing Units
with a capacity of zero, `create_housing_assignment` refuses an assignment into
one by name, and a dropdown that offers a bath house is a dropdown whose next
screen is a refusal.

**A condemned cabin is listed with the reason rather than silently missing.** A
foreman who cannot find the unit they expected needs to be told it is marked
Uninhabitable — a shorter list tells them nothing and they go and look. Same for
a full one under `include_full=true`.

**A cabin nobody has measured is not reported as full.** Capacity zero means
unmeasured, not "no beds"; a camp whose capacities were never entered would
otherwise come back with every bed taken and nothing saying why.

## 4. The camp has a write, and it refuses what the console only warns about

`assign_housing` puts one person in one cabin from one date, delegating to
`create_housing_assignment` — so the overlap rule, the refusal of a shower block,
the refusal of a condemned unit, the deposit arithmetic and the Section 119 note
are all the same code an operator gets on the console.

**One rule is deliberately stricter here.** `create_housing_assignment` reports
*"now holds 5 against a recorded capacity of 4"* in `warnings` and writes the row.
That is right on a console, where an operator sees the warning, weighs it and
means it — a barracks really does take a fifth bunk some seasons.

It is wrong on a phone. Nothing on the Housing step displays a warning, the
foreman has already walked to the next truck, and a bed that does not exist
becomes somebody sleeping in a cab. So the occupancy is counted **before** the
write and the refusal names the unit, its capacity and how many are already in
it.

`allow_multi_occupancy` — the one argument that would turn that check off — **is
not in the signature**, so the phone cannot send it. Under capacity the method
passes it as true on the caller's behalf, because a four-bunk cabin with one
person in it is the ordinary case and the tool refuses a second assignment
without the flag.

**It carries the HR role gate.** A Housing Assignment names a person, a building
and the dates between them. It is the audit trail defending an IRS Section 119
exclusion and the record an ORS 653 wage claim about a housing deduction is
answered from. That is a personnel record, and the same gate `search_employees`
applies by hand applies here — so an operator enrolling an onboarding phone
enrols it as a **Farm Manager**.

## 5. `Parcel.branch` — the join between a person and a cabin

An Employee carries a **Branch**. A Housing Unit stands on a **Parcel**. Nothing
connected the two.

That is not a cosmetic gap. It is the reason the wizard's Housing step could not
follow from its Assignment step: having just asked which camp somebody was hired
to, the phone had no way to ask for that camp's cabins. The alternative was iOS
fetching every parcel, fetching every unit, and working the mapping out itself —
a second copy of a rule, on the client, that goes stale the first time somebody
adds a parcel.

Parcel gains a `branch` column.

**Data, not a Link, and that is the same decision `Housing Assignment.employee`
records.** Branch belongs to Frappe HR. A Link to it would make Parcel fail to
migrate on every site that has not installed hrms, and a land register that
cannot be installed without an HR app is a land register nobody keeps. So the
value is stored as text and **validated against the Branch table when there is
one** — a parcel tagged with a branch that does not exist is refused at the
record, because that typo is a camp the hiring wizard would show as having no
cabins at all, and it would look exactly like a camp that is full.

**It is carried across a conveyance.** A parcel that moves from the trust to the
LLC is the same forty acres with the same cabins on it; dropping the branch would
empty a camp's housing list on the day of a paper transaction nobody in the
orchard saw.

### The mapping is resolved once, server-side

`list_onboarding_reference_data` returns `parcels` on every branch row.
`list_available_housing` resolves `branch` through **the same function**, so the
mapping the wizard was *shown* and the mapping the housing list *filters on*
cannot come apart. The phone passes the branch it just hired somebody into and
gets the right cabins back.

**A camp may hold several parcels and all of them are searched.** A labor camp
that grew across a fence line is two parcels, and a mapping that could only hold
one would drop half the cabins on exactly the operations big enough to need the
feature. `parcels` is the real answer; the scalar `parcel` is set only when there
is exactly one, and is null for none **and** for several — a scalar that silently
picked the first of two would send half a camp missing.

### Three ways it can fail, three different answers, none of them empty

An empty camp reads on a phone as *"no room"*. That is the one wrong answer this
endpoint can give, so it never gives it:

| What happened | What comes back |
| --- | --- |
| The branch names no Branch record | **Refused**, naming it — a typo resolves to no parcels and would otherwise look like a full camp |
| Real branch, no ground tagged to it | The whole list, `branch_filter_applied: false`, and `branch_note` naming `update_parcel(branch=…)` as the fix |
| Site has not migrated the column | The whole list, and `branch_note` naming the migration |

`parcel` is still accepted and is narrower than `branch`. Passing both filters to
the intersection, which is what asking for one parcel of a two-parcel camp means.

**The reverse mapping is a stated limit, not an omission.** One column on Parcel
holds one branch, so two branches sharing a parcel is not representable. A camp
spanning parcels is; a parcel split between camps is not.

## Routes

    POST /farmops/api/mobile/list_onboarding_reference_data
    POST /farmops/api/mobile/list_available_housing
    POST /farmops/api/mobile/assign_housing

and the same three at `/api/method/erpnext_mcp.api.mobile.<method>`.

**The iOS side is separate, tracked work.** `MobileAPI.swift` names none of the
three yet, so they sit in `PENDING_IOS_INTEGRATION` rather than in `MOBILE`, and
`test_ios_contract` transcribes no mirror for them — a mirror of a Codable that
does not exist would invent the contract instead of copying it. They move up in
the release that lands the Swift side.

## Upgrading

    bench --site <site> migrate

**The migrate is required this time.** `Parcel.branch` is a new column on this
app's own doctype, and until it exists `list_available_housing` cannot resolve a
branch — it says so in `branch_note` and lists every unit rather than none, so
nothing breaks in the meantime.

Then tag the ground. For each camp:

    update_parcel(parcel="Mill Creek", branch="Mill Creek Camp")

A branch with no parcel tagged to it is not an error and not hidden — it appears
in `branches_without_parcels`, which is the list of camps still to do.

`Employee.branch` needs nothing: it is a field on a stock Frappe HR Employee, and
a site that does not have it reports it through `fields_not_on_this_site` exactly
as any other absent field, with the hire still completing.
