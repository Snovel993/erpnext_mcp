# v0.50.0 — a badge is issued here now, and a bucket leaves the phone

The badge audit of 2026-08-07 (`docs/reviews/badge_qr_pipeline_audit_2026_08_07.md`)
found the Badge → QR → Bucket pipeline about **sixty per cent built**, with the
missing forty concentrated in two places:

1. **Nothing issued a badge.** `link_badge_to_employee` *recorded* a string an
   operator typed or scanned; it never minted one, and there was no printable
   card. So the only real badges in the business were printed by `farm_app`
   (Flask) and encoded a uuid4 `QRToken` that ERPNext had never heard of.
2. **Nothing sent a capture.** `MobileAPI.syncBucketEntries` was declared in the
   iOS client and had **zero call sites**. `BucketCaptureSession.commitEntry`
   appended a `BucketEntry` to an in-memory array and stopped. Every bucket
   scanned in an orchard died when the view was dismissed.

Everything between those two points — the scanner, the transport wrapper, the
doctype, the badge register, the session totals, the payroll reshape — already
existed and was already tested. What was broken was the joins.

Both are closed. So is the consequence of the second one: because a badge could
be **any string at all**, a soda can's barcode, a Wi-Fi join code and an
operator's own login QR were all badges as far as this app was concerned.

---

## 1. `generate_employee_badge_qr` — the issuer that did not exist

```json
{"employee": "HR-EMP-00042", "company": "Cherry Farms LLC"}
```

Mints a readable badge ID, records it in the Bucket Log Badge Map register, and
returns the QR for the card as a base64 PNG alongside the name, designation,
photograph URL and the initials that go where a photograph is missing.

**A minted badge is `<company abbreviation>-<sequence>`** — `CF-0001`. §7 of the
audit put the decision plainly and it is worth repeating: piece-rate attribution
is a payroll record, so the identifier that decides who gets paid for a bucket
should live in the system that pays, with a retirement flag an HR manager can
flip, and should be readable aloud over a radio at 6am when the scanner will not
focus on a scuffed card.

**Idempotent without `regenerate`.** Somebody who already holds a live badge gets
*that* badge's QR back rather than a second identifier. Reprinting a card that
went through a wash cycle is the common request and must not consume a number.

**`regenerate: true` is the lost-card path and it retires what it replaces.**
`active` was always the retirement flag and nothing enforced that a person had
one ticked row. Two live cards for one picker is not a harmless duplicate — it is
a lost card that still resolves, which is the exact situation the flag exists
for. A retired number is never reissued: the counter reads the highest, not the
count.

**The QR is drawn before the register is written.** A bench with no encoder
refuses *before* it has issued a badge nobody can print.

## 2. `generate_employee_badge_sheet` — a crew at once

Up to 100 cards per call, each with name, photograph (or initials), designation,
badge ID and QR. Issues to anybody with no badge and reuses the live one where
there is one. **One employee's failure does not lose the sheet**: a name that
resolves to nobody, somebody who has left, an entity this caller cannot reach —
each is reported by name in `errors` and every other card is still printed. A
hiring day with one bad row in the list is not a hiring day with no badges.

## 3. `resolve_badge` — the read between a scan and a name

The call every other badge-shaped feature was blocked on. `add_worker_to_shift`
takes an **Employee docname** and a camera produces a **badge string**, so the
crew clock could scan a whole crew and roster none of it. The bucket loop had the
same gap more quietly: it could show a foreman the code it read and never the
picker's name, so a mis-scan looked exactly like a good scan until the data
reached somebody at a desk.

**It refuses rather than answering empty.** Never issued, retired, and belonging
to somebody who has left are three sentences, because they are three situations
with three fixes and "not found" collapses them into one. A badge from another
entity reads as unknown rather than "wrong company": confirming a badge exists
elsewhere on the site is not a fact this read should hand to whoever is holding a
card.

Pass `shift` and the answer carries `on_shift` and `joined_at` — whether this
person is clocked in right now, which is what turns an identification into an
admission. Read-only, **on by default**, on `READ_LIMIT`: it is a PII lookup keyed
on a string anybody holding a card can produce.

## 4. Which strings are badges — two layers, refusing different things

**The shape** (`bucket_bridge.validate_badge_id`) is a floor, not a grammar:
letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.`, between 4 and 64 characters, not opening a JSON
document. That accepts a minted `CF-0001` **and** a 36-character legacy uuid —
refusing the second would be a reprint of the whole workforce on upgrade day —
and refuses every URL, every Wi-Fi payload and every `api_key:api_secret`.
Including the payload `generate_mobile_login_qr` produces, which scanned at a
badge step used to become a badge ID with a live secret inside it.

**The refusal does not repeat what it refused.** `bucket_bridge._display` quotes
twelve characters and a length, and `guard.redact_payloads` replaces a
credential-shaped argument on its way into MCP Action Log. A check whose whole
job is catching a credential scanned by mistake must not put it in a
`ValidationError`, in the audit register and on a screen in an orchard.

**The register** is the new `badge_policy` on `sync_bucket_entries`:

| | unmapped badge | retired badge | employee has left | not on the shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lenient` (tool default) | filed, employee blank | filed, employee blank | filed | not checked |
| `strict` (what the phone sends) | refused by name | refused by name | refused by name | refused by name, with `shift` |

`lenient` is the v0.44.0 behaviour and stays the default **on purpose**: a
capture taken before anybody mapped the card is filed with no employee and a
later `link_badge_to_employee` backfills it, which is right for a Desk import and
is why a badge scanned before it was mapped still gets paid.

`strict` is what `api/mobile.sync_bucket_entries` sends, and it is **not a body
key** — a handset cannot relax it. Badges are minted by this app now, so a phone
scanning a string this site never issued has scanned something that is not a
badge, and filing that produces a piecework row nobody can ever claim.

The **shape** check runs under both, because nobody is going to map a Wi-Fi join
code to a picker.

## 5. The phone half — `fafo_ios`

- **`BucketEntryQueue`** — a durable, file-backed capture queue. A committed
  bucket survives a force-quit, a crash and the walk back to the truck; the
  launch sweep no longer deletes its frozen frame as residue, because the entry
  that referenced it now exists to claim it.
- **`BucketSyncEngine`** — drains it in batches against `sync_bucket_entries`,
  one company per call, stopping at the first failure. A **duplicate counts as
  landed** (the server deduplicates by `entry_uuid`, so that is agreement); a
  **refusal is kept and shown**, not retried forever and not silently dropped.
- **The coverage bug this found — in the model-audit trail, not in pay.**
  `BucketEntry.coveragePercent` holds the ML model's own output, which is a
  fraction — every screen renders it as `Int(coverage * 100)`. The server's
  diagnostic column is a percentage validated 0…100, so sending `0.94` unscaled
  would have been accepted and recorded a full bucket's diagnostic as **0.94%
  full**, making that trail read as nonsense. `BadgeAPI.payload` scales it, and a
  value already past 1.0 is passed through so a converted caller is not doubled.
  **No figure on a slip was affected either way:** the gate is binary, pay is the
  count of Accepted buckets times the piece rate, and `coverage_percent` is not
  an input to it. v0.50.1 writes that rule down and asserts it on both sides.
- **`BadgeFormat`** — the shape check at the camera, transcribed from
  `validate_badge_id`, so a login QR is refused before a round trip and before a
  foreman has walked away believing the bucket was logged.
- **`BadgeDirectory`** — badges this handset has resolved before, on disk, so a
  scan produces a name with no signal. A server refusal *deletes* the entry, so
  the cache can never outlive a retirement. Cleared on sign-out.
- **`BadgeAPI.resolution`** — offline is `.unreachable`, not `.rejected`. A phone
  out of range must not lose a morning's picking; the capture is queued and
  validated when it lands.
- **`CrewClockSession`** — wired to `start_shift`, `resolve_badge` and
  `add_worker_to_shift`, and to `end_shift` with the supervisor signature the
  close requires. A scan that resolves shows the picker's name; one that does not
  shows the server's sentence and re-arms the camera. A clock-in the farm has not
  accepted is counted on screen and retried, because a clock-in assumed sent is a
  worker with no Attendance row.
- **The scan screen's two buttons now do different things.** "Skip badge" and
  "Confirm" both called `commitEntry(badgeID: nil)`, so the affirmative-looking
  button silently produced a capture that fails validation at sync — a bucket the
  foreman was told was logged and nobody would ever be paid for. It is now
  "Discard" and "Log without badge", and the second one says what it means.

## 6. Counts

389 tools (was 386): 178 read, 211 mutating. The two issuers are MUTATING and
ship **off**; `resolve_badge` is a read and ships **on**. A bench with no
`segno`/`qrcode` loses the two that draw and keeps the one that turns a scan into
a name.

## 7. Upgrading

`bench --site <site> migrate` for the three new settings switches; there is no
doctype change. Nothing about existing badges changes: matching is still an exact
string comparison with no format assumption, so an old `farm_app` uuid mapped
with `link_badge_to_employee` and a new `CF-0042` minted here both resolve to the
same person for as long as the transition takes.
`generate_employee_badge_qr(badge_id=…)` adopts an existing printed card outright.

**One behaviour change to know about before a phone upgrades.** The mobile
`sync_bucket_entries` wrapper now sends `badge_policy=strict`, so a capture whose
badge is not in the register is **refused** rather than filed unattributed. On a
site still running `farm_app` cards, map them with `link_badge_to_employee`
before the handsets update — or issue new ones and let the old cards be retired
as they come back in. The MCP tool's own default is unchanged.
