# v0.40.0 — the payroll was right and the ledger had never heard of it

**v0.30.0 built an engine that computed a payroll slip. v0.35.0 fed it the hours
the foreman had already written down. v0.36.0 drew the tax forms the slips add up
to. And a completed payroll run produced Farm Payroll Slips, correct to the cent,
and a general ledger with nothing in it.**

Wages are the largest number on a farm's income statement. They were also the
one number somebody keyed in twice — out of a payroll report, into a journal
entry, by hand, every fortnight, with a transposition waiting in it and no
second source to catch it against. That is the loop this release closes.

Four tools. Three DocTypes. One pure module. No account name anywhere in the
source.

---

## The problem, stated the way an operation states it

A farm runs payroll on the 15th. The engine works: hours off the shift register,
overtime resolved per workweek at forty, wages split by work state, federal and
state withholding computed from the worker's own W-4. Forty slips, each right.

Then somebody opens the ledger and types it in. Gross to field labour. Federal
withholding to a payable. FICA to a payable. Net to the clearing account. And
the employer's own share of FICA — which the engine also computed, and which is
7.65% of gross, and which appeared on no record at all — from memory, or not at
all.

The failure is not that this is slow. It is that it produces a second copy of
the payroll, in a different system, keyed by a person, and when the two disagree
there is no way to know which one is wrong.

---

## No account name is shipped, and that is the design

The obvious shortcut is a default chart: gross pay to `5300 - Wages`, FICA to
`2130 - FICA Payable`, and so on. It would work on the site it was written on.

It would be **quietly wrong everywhere else** — and quietly wrong in a chart of
accounts is not an error message. It is a year of wages sitting in an expense
line nobody looks at, until the tax preparer asks why field labour halved.

So the mapping is a **record**: `Farm Payroll Account Mapping`, one per company,
one row per payroll component, with `debit_account` and `credit_account` on the
row. `configure_payroll_accounts` writes it. `get_payroll_account_mapping` reads
it back with the gaps named and each component explained. Neither of them has an
account name in its source, and neither does `payroll_gl.py`.

A company with no mapping cannot post payroll, and the refusal says which tool
creates one. That is the right failure: better a farm that has not configured
its accounts yet than a farm whose wages went somewhere nobody chose.

---

## Eleven components, and the split is about who owes what

**Six are employee-side.** Together they are the two sides of gross pay, so all
six are required whatever the amounts are — a mapping missing `Net Pay` cannot
post a payroll however small the payroll is.

| Component | Side | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `Gross Pay` | debit | Total earnings — the wage expense itself |
| `Federal Tax` | credit | Federal income tax withheld |
| `SS Employee` | credit | The worker's 6.2% share |
| `Medicare Employee` | credit | The worker's 1.45%, plus Additional Medicare |
| `State Tax` | credit | Everything a state withholds from the worker |
| `Net Pay` | credit | What is actually paid — clearing, bank or cash |

Withheld money is not an extra expense. Gross pay is already the whole cost to
the worker's side of the entry, and each withholding is a liability carved out
of it. That is why five of the six are credits against one debit, and why they
sum to it exactly.

**Five are employer-side**, and each is an expense **and** a liability, because
it is money the farm owes on top of gross rather than money carved out of it:

| Component | Sides | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `SS Employer` | debit + credit | The farm's matching 6.2% |
| `Medicare Employer` | debit + credit | The farm's matching 1.45% |
| `FUTA` | debit + credit | Federal unemployment, first $7,000 per worker |
| `SUTA` | debit + credit | State unemployment at this employer's own rate |
| `State Employer Other` | debit + credit | Paid Leave Oregon employer share, OR workers' comp, WA PFML employer share, WA L&I |

They stay five components rather than one because they are **remitted to five
different places on five different schedules**. Employer Social Security and
Medicare go to the IRS on the 941 alongside the withheld amounts. FUTA goes to
the IRS on the 940 annually. State unemployment goes to the state agency
quarterly. Paid Leave Oregon, workers' compensation, WA PFML and L&I go
elsewhere again. A single "payroll tax payable" account makes every one of those
reconciliations a spreadsheet exercise.

A farm that genuinely wants them in one account can point five components at one
account. That is a decision it made, rather than one this app made for it.

### The eleventh component, which the specification did not name

`State Employer Other` was not in the brief for this release, and it exists
because `state_withholding.py` computes employer amounts that are **not**
unemployment insurance: Paid Leave Oregon's employer share, Oregon workers'
compensation, Washington PFML's employer share and the WA L&I employer rate. A
mapping with `SUTA` and nowhere else for the employer side would have dropped
all of that out of the books silently.

Dropping it loudly was not an option either. It is a cost the farm has incurred.

## The employer taxes were computed since v0.28.0 and stored nowhere

To post the employer half, the figures have to exist on a record. They did not.

`calculate_federal_withholding` has returned `social_security_employer`,
`medicare_employer` and `futa_employer` since v0.28.0, and the state engines have
returned their employer totals since v0.29.0 — and all of it lived in a nested
`federal_detail` that `run_payroll_for_period` threw away. The slip written to
the database carried the worker's deductions and none of what the employer owed
on top of them.

Farm Payroll Slip now carries `social_security_employer`, `medicare_employer`,
`futa`, `state_unemployment`, `state_employer_other` and
`total_employer_taxes`; `calculate_full_payroll` returns them at the top level
plus `total_cost_of_employment`. **No total moved.** These are the same numbers
the engines already produced, lifted to where they can be kept.

That is worth having for its own sake, quite apart from the ledger: "what does
this worker cost" is a question a farm asks constantly, and gross pay is not the
answer to it.

### Slips written before this release carry zeros, and posting one says so

A pre-v0.40.0 run posts its wages correctly and books no employer taxes, because
it has none recorded. The result carries a warning naming the worker and saying
to re-run the period.

It is a warning rather than an inference from four zeros, and the distinction is
the point: an employer with no employer taxes is a real thing — a household
employer below every threshold — and an entry that quietly left them out is not.
Four zeros cannot tell those two apart. A missing field can.

## State unemployment is new, and defaults to zero

`State Tax Configuration` gains `suta_rate` and `suta_wage_base`.

Both are **employer-entered**, for exactly the reason workers' compensation is: a
SUTA rate is assigned to one employer by one state agency out of that employer's
own experience rating. Oregon's 2025 taxable wage base is $54,300 and
Washington's is $72,800, but both move every year and neither tells you the rate.
There is no table anybody could ship.

The rate defaults to zero, so **a site that enters no rate computes exactly what
it computed before this release**. The addition is a place to put a number, not a
new charge. The wage base is consumed by year-to-date gross the way FUTA's $7,000
is — a period computed in isolation would restart it and over-collect on anybody
who had already crossed it — and a base of zero means no cap, which is the honest
reading of a rate entered with no base beside it.

SUTA sits beside FUTA rather than inside it because FUTA's credit already assumes
state unemployment tax was paid. They are separate liabilities to separate
agencies and they reconcile against each other, which they cannot do from one
account.

## Drafts only, and there is no second tool

`post_payroll_to_gl` inserts through `mutate.insert_draft_journal_entry` — the
one function in this app that writes a Journal Entry, with its belt-and-braces
assertion that refuses to report success if anything ever submitted on insert —
and stops.

There is no `submit_payroll_journal_entries` and there is not going to be one,
for the same reason there is no `post_journal_entry`. Arithmetic anybody can redo
and a statement about money leaving the farm are different acts, and they want
different people in front of them. Submitting stays `submit_journal_entry`,
behind its own switch, where a human is looking at one entry at a time.

---

## Posting twice is the failure this guards hardest against

A payroll posted twice is a doubled wage expense against a doubled liability. It
is the easiest mistake in the world to make from a chat client that lost its
scrollback, and it is invisible until a month-end that does not tie.

Every draft is linked back onto the run's `gl_postings` table, and a run with
live entries against it is refused **by name**, with the docnames in the message.

**The check is about the ledger, not about the table.** A run whose drafts were
deleted, or whose entries were cancelled, can be posted again — because then
there is genuinely nothing in the books, and refusing would be this app
remembering something the ledger does not. A deleted entry's link row is pruned;
a cancelled entry's is kept, because it is still there and still part of what
happened to this payroll.

### Five refusals, reported at once

| Blocker | Why |
|---|---|
| `payroll_status` | Only Calculated or Submitted runs post. A Draft has no computed slips; a Cancelled run is one somebody withdrew |
| `no_mapping` | The company has no account mapping, and no default exists to fall back on |
| `incomplete_mapping` | A required component has no account. Named, by component and by side |
| `already_posted` | Live journal entries exist against this run |
| `unbalanced` | A slip whose gross is not its deductions plus its net |

All of them, every time, rather than one per round trip. A caller who fixes one
refusal and is met with the next has been sent round the loop for no reason, and
a payroll posting is not a thing anybody wants to iterate on.

## The preview refuses nothing, on purpose

`preview_payroll_gl` returns every line of every entry, both totals, the balance
check, and `would_post: false` with the blockers listed.

An incomplete mapping is **exactly** what somebody calls a preview to find out
about. A preview that raised instead of reporting would be useless for the one
job it has.

The preview and the posting call one pure function — `payroll_gl.build_payroll_journal_entries`
— so the entry described and the entry inserted cannot drift. The only difference
between the two tools is whether anything is inserted afterwards.

---

## Lines merge, and say what they were merged from

The ordinary mapping sends employee Social Security and employee Medicare to one
FICA payable account, and employer Social Security and employer Medicare to one
payroll tax expense. Left as separate lines that is a six-line employer section
where the books want two, and every one of those lines reconciles to the same
balance anyway.

So lines on the same account and the same side are summed into one, and the
line's `user_remark` names every component that built it. The per-component
breakdown survives on the result. Nothing is lost; the arithmetic is just done
before the entry is written rather than by whoever reads it.

---

## Consolidated by default, per-employee when it earns its keep

A forty-person biweekly payroll is one journal entry in consolidated mode and
forty in per-employee mode, and the general ledger does not want forty.

Per-employee earns its keep where labour is costed by person, or where a single
slip has to be reversed on its own. Either way the run's own record keeps the
per-person detail, so nothing is lost by consolidating. The default is on the
company's mapping and overridable per call, and both modes book identical totals
— there is a test asserting exactly that.

## The four tools

| Tool | Kind | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `get_payroll_account_mapping` | READ, on | The mapping, the gaps, and what each of the eleven components is for |
| `preview_payroll_gl` | READ, on | Every line the run would produce. Writes nothing, refuses nothing |
| `configure_payroll_accounts` | MUTATING, off | Writes the mapping. Merges by default; `replace=true` discards |
| `post_payroll_to_gl` | MUTATING, off | Draft Journal Entries, linked back onto the run |

`configure_payroll_accounts` refuses a **group account** when the mapping is
written rather than when it is posted from. A mapping is written once and posted
from every fortnight afterwards, so a group account stored today is a payroll
refused in six weeks by somebody who was not the one who configured it.

It also refuses a debit on `Gross Pay`'s credit side and a single `account` on a
two-sided component. Putting the employer's Social Security expense and its
liability in one account is a decision, not a shorthand, and a tool that guessed
would be guessing about the books.

## Tests

101 new standalone tests, 5,478 in total, all green.

Eight claims, in `tests_standalone/test_payroll_gl.py`:

1. **The components are the unit** — eleven of them, each reading its amount off
   the slip under any of the names a slip carries it under, each knowing its
   side, and none of them knowing an account.
2. **The mapping is a record** — an incomplete mapping is incomplete by name and
   by side; an employer component with no amount this run is not a gap, and one
   with an amount is.
3. **The entry balances** — gross debited equals every withholding plus net
   credited, exactly, including on figures that do not divide cleanly; each
   employer component balances against itself; a slip whose own arithmetic is
   broken is caught rather than posted.
4. **Lines merge** — two components on one account and one side become one line,
   and the line still says what it was built from.
5. **Consolidated and per-employee** — the two modes book identical totals.
6. **Posting writes drafts only** — docstatus 0, the remark naming the run and
   the slip, and the entry linked back onto the run.
7. **Posting twice is refused** — including the two cases where it is not: a
   cancelled entry and a deleted one.
8. **The refusals** — every blocker, by name, all at once, and nothing written by
   a refused call.

Plus an end-to-end test that puts hours on the shift register, runs the real
payroll engine, checks that the employer taxes reached the slip, and posts the
whole thing to a balanced journal entry.
