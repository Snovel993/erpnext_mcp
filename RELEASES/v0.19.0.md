# v0.19.0 — the training register

**2026-08-03.** The compliance calendar could see every document on the farm and
nothing a person knew.

Eleven rules watched certificates, policies, cabins, water, filings and audits.
Not one watched **training** — which is what the Worker Protection Standard asks
for every twelve months, what Oregon's heat rule asks for annually before the
first shift at 80 °F, what FSMA Subpart C asks for on hiring and periodically
thereafter, and what a GAP auditor asks for by name with the signature attached.
All of it lived in a binder, and the way an operation found out that a handler's
card had lapsed was that somebody looked, or that an inspector did.

This release is the twelfth rule, the doctype under it, four MCP tools, and a
worker-training section on every audit packet.

Suite: **3,514 → 3,588 passing** (74 new).

---

## The one idea

**One afternoon in a shed answers four audits, so it is one record with four
tags.**

A single session covering hygiene, pesticide safety and heat satisfies USDA GAP,
WPS and OR-OSHA at once — provided the trainer covered all three curricula.
Filing it three times produces three records that disagree by August. Filing it
once with three tags produces one record that three packets pull from, and that
is the whole architecture:

- `Employee Training Record.regimes` is a tag list over a closed vocabulary of
  eight: FSMA, GAP, GlobalGAP, PrimusGFS, NOP, WPS, OR-OSHA, Other.
- `content_topics_covered` is **required**, because it is what makes a tag
  defensible rather than optimistic. Oregon's heat rule names six topics that
  must be covered annually; a record claiming OR-OSHA without them is a record
  an inspector will disallow. "Heat, water, shade, symptoms, reporting,
  emergency response" is a curriculum. "Safety meeting" is not.
- `generate_audit_packet` pulls the subset each auditor is entitled to see. A GAP
  packet takes GAP and WPS training. An EPA packet takes WPS alone — a packet
  containing a worker's organic-handling training invites a question nobody
  wanted to answer, exactly as a DOL packet containing a GlobalGAP certificate
  would.

**Matching is by tag, never by substring.** `"GlobalGAP"` contains `"GAP"`, so a
`LIKE '%GAP%'` filter would put every GLOBALG.A.P. record into a USDA GAP packet
— a quiet, wrong-evidence bug that would surface in a room with an inspector in
it. Every read goes through `training.matches()`, which compares parsed tokens,
and there is a test asserting a GlobalGAP record stays out of a GAP packet so
nobody optimises the Python filter into SQL later.

## The §112.161 fields are on the doctype from its first version

This is the decision worth defending at length.

FSMA **21 CFR 112.161** does not merely ask that a training happened. It asks
that the RECORD carry the farm's name, the date *and time* of the activity, the
signature or initials of the person who performed it, and — for worker-training
records specifically, under **§112.161(b)** — a supervisor's review, dated and
signed within a reasonable time after the record was made.

An operation running USDA GAP alone generally has none of the last three. That is
why "missing supervisor sign-off on the §112.161(b) records" is one of the most
commonly cited FSMA findings against farms **whose actual practice is fine**.

Adding those columns in v0.22 instead would mean every record written between now
and then has them empty, and there is no honest way to fill them later: a
signature backfilled in the week before an inspection is not evidence that
somebody signed at the time, it is evidence that somebody signed in the week
before the inspection. The columns are cheap now and impossible to retrofit
truthfully.

So the doctype ships with `person_performed_signature`, `supervisor_reviewed_by`,
`supervisor_reviewed_on`, `supervisor_signature`, `activity_datetime`, `regimes`,
`content_topics_covered` — and `farm_name_snapshot`, which the spec did not list
and §112.161(a)(1)(i) requires: a lookup through the Company link at report time
answers with today's name, and an inspector paging through last season's logs is
entitled to the name that was on the gate then.

**What is missing is reported, never refused.** An operation that trained its
crew and recorded it imperfectly is in a better position than one that trained
its crew and recorded nothing, and a controller that refused the record would
guarantee the second. Every read returns `fsma_112_161_gaps` in the rule's own
terms.

**`sign_training_supervisor_review` is a separate tool, deliberately.** The
rule's phrase is "within a reasonable time **after** the record is made" — a
sequence, not a form field. A call that took both signatures would make it
trivial to record both at the same instant, and simultaneous timestamps are the
shape of a record an inspector reads as assembled rather than kept. The tool
reports the lag and says so when it is long.

## The twelfth rule

`training_expiring` is the first rule about a **person's qualification** rather
than a document's date, and the consequence is sharper than a certificate's.

| State | Severity | Why that number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lapsed | **Critical** | A handler whose WPS training is over twelve months old cannot lawfully perform an application (40 CFR 170.501; OAR 437-004-6501). This takes somebody off the sprayer, not off a list. |
| ≤ 30 days | **Critical** | Courses run on a published schedule. Inside thirty days the next one may already be after the lapse, so booking is no longer reliably enough. |
| ≤ 90 days | **Warning** | What arranging a retraining actually takes: trainer, crew, language, and a room. |
| No expiry | **nothing at all** | A new-hire orientation and a PSA grower certificate do not lapse. A renewal alert nobody can ever clear is how a compliance calendar stops being read. |

The alert message carries the **regimes** and what actually stops being lawful,
because "expires in 12 days" and "WPS handler training expires in 12 days and he
cannot lawfully spray after that" are different decisions for the person reading
a phone at seven in the morning.

It reads `expires_date`, not `status` — same argument as `certification_expiring`:
a derived column is only correct on documents somebody happened to save, so the
expired records would be exactly the ones still reading Active.

It has a dispatch recipe, so an alert becomes a Farm Task like every other. Its
`creates_record` is deliberately **empty** even though there is a training doctype
now: completing the task is arranging and delivering a retraining, and no builder
in `complete_farm_task` can invent the topics covered or the trainee's signature.
A task that auto-filed a training record with neither would produce exactly the
document an auditor disallows. The task closes with findings text and a
signature; `record_training` files the evidence.

## The four tools

| Tool | Kind | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `record_training` | MUTATING, ships OFF | One event, tagged with every audit it answers. Refuses an unknown regime by name, a future date, an expiry before completion, and a record with no topics. A renewal **adds** a record and names what it supersedes. |
| `list_trainings` | Read | Filtered by `regime` / `status` / `expiring_within_days` / `unreviewed_only` / person / period. `status` computed as of **today**, not read off the column. |
| `get_training` | Read | Full record, the person's history, the §112.161 gaps, and the **retention answer with its citation**. |
| `sign_training_supervisor_review` | MUTATING, ships OFF | The §112.161(b) review. Refuses a self-review, a cross-entity supervisor, and a review dated before the training. |

Guards are `create_employee`'s, **imported rather than copied**: role gate on
System Manager / HR Manager / HR User / Farm Manager, company scope through
`Employee.company`, kill switch per tool, audit row on every call. The reads are
gated too — a training record names a worker and is read in a wage claim.

## Retention, which nothing on the site answered before

| Regime | Years | Citation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NOP | **5** | 7 CFR 205.103(b)(4) — five years beyond creation |
| OR-OSHA | 3 | no published universal ag window; three is the defensible inspection reach-back |
| FSMA | 2 | 21 CFR 112.164(a)(1) — two years past the date the record was created |
| WPS | 2 | 40 CFR 170.309 — two years at the establishment |
| GAP / GlobalGAP / PrimusGFS | 2 | scheme practice |

**The longest tag governs.** A record tagged GAP *and* NOP is a five-year record,
because destroying it at two would destroy the NOP evidence. Computed on read
rather than stored, because the answer changes when a regime is added to an
existing record and a `destroy_after` column that was right in 2026 and wrong in
2027 is worse than no column at all.

## Audit packet integration

`generate_audit_packet` gained a **worker training** section on every audit type,
scoped to that type's own regimes, plus a `regime` argument that narrows it to
one scheme.

`regime` is **part of the idempotence key**. A WPS-narrowed GAP packet and a full
GAP packet are different documents with different contents; filing the second
over the first would silently replace a buyer's evidence bundle with a narrower
one, and the operator would find out when the buyer did.

The section discloses rather than filters: records with no trainee signature, no
supervisor review, or already expired by the period end are counted and named in
the packet. An auditor who finds that themselves asks a much harder question than
one who was shown it.

It is scoped by **when the training happened**, not by whether it is still
current. An auditor asking about last season is asking what the crew had been
taught by then, and silently dropping a since-lapsed record would overstate the
position.

`generate_compliance_packet` gained `regime` as a **top-level argument rather
than a filter on each packet type**. Both packet types this app ships are
accounting artefacts, and putting a WPS key in a reconciliation's filter schema
would be putting a worker-training question on a form about a bank account. It is
an annex — it changes nothing the packet computes and staples the training
evidence for one regime to the back of it, over that packet's own period
(`period_start`/`period_end`, or the Fiscal Year's own dates, or all time — which
it states rather than guesses).

## Deviations from the spec, and why

**`regimes` is a delimited tag list, not a Frappe Table MultiSelect.** A real
Table MultiSelect costs two extra doctypes — a `Compliance Regime` master holding
eight records and a child table linking to it — plus a seeder to put those eight
records on every site before anybody can file a training record. That is
precisely the post-deploy configuration step the spec's fixtures requirement was
written to remove, and it is table sprawl for a closed list of eight tokens that
has changed twice in fifteen years. The vocabulary and every read of it live in
`erpnext_mcp/training.py` so the controller, the rule, the tools and the packet
builder cannot disagree, and matching is by token so the `GlobalGAP`/`GAP`
collision cannot bite.

**No Frappe `fixture` for the compliance rule.** There is nothing to fixture:
compliance rules in this app are code, registered at import time in
`alerts/rules.py`, so `training_expiring` is live the moment the app is upgraded
— no post-deploy configuration, which is what the spec asked for. `hooks.py`
forbids `fixtures` by name, and `test_hooks.py` enforces it. The doctype installs
on `bench migrate` the same way every other doctype in this app does.

**The role gate includes `HR User`.** The spec named HR Manager / Farm Manager /
System Manager; the tools import `employee.HR_ROLES`, which is those three plus
`HR User` — Frappe HR's own read/write role, and the one a real HR clerk holds.
Restating a narrower list here would have meant two role gates on the personnel
surface that drift apart.

## Regulatory and architectural gaps caught while building

Recorded here rather than fixed, because each is its own piece of work:

1. **`Compliance Alert` has no `regime` field.** Both research documents ask for
   one and list the values. Alerts are grouped by `category` today, which cannot
   answer "show me everything OR-OSHA would cite us for". Training records now
   carry regimes; the alerts they raise do not.
2. **`OAR 437-004-1090` and `-1130` are still in the codebase.** The Tilth/OR-OSHA
   research doc §7 confirms the live rules are **-1120** (labor housing) and
   **-1131** (heat illness). `alerts/rules.py:DETECTOR_DAYS` cites `-1120`
   correctly; other references should be swept.
3. **No `Heat Exposure Event` doctype.** OAR 437-004-1131 requires a per-shift
   record above 80 °F (shade established, water stocked, cool-down breaks taken
   or refused, high-heat practices at 90 °F). Training proves the crew was
   taught; nothing proves the shift was run correctly.
4. **`training_topic_taxonomy` is free text.** The research doc asks for an enum
   (`Heat-Illness-Prevention`, `WPS-Handler`, `Respirator-Fit-Test`, …).
   `training_type` is deliberately free text so a curriculum rename does not
   force a schema change, but that means "has every handler had WPS training this
   year" is a text match rather than a query.
5. **No respirator fit-test cadence or medical-clearance tracking.** Modelled as
   an ordinary training record with a twelve-month expiry, which works, but
   §1910.1020 exposure/medical records carry a **thirty-year** retention this
   app's retention table does not express.
6. **`farm_location_gps` is not on the record.** §112.161(a)(1)(i) asks for farm
   name *and location*; the name is snapshotted, the location is not. Training
   happens in a shed rather than in a block, so it is the weakest instance of the
   requirement — but the Spray Record and Water Test have the same gap and it
   matters more there.
7. **The §112.161(b) review has no cadence rule.** The tool records the lag and
   flags anything over thirty days, but nothing sweeps for records that have gone
   unreviewed — an alert on "worker training records older than 14 days with no
   supervisor review" would be the natural thirteenth rule.
8. **`docs/tool-catalog.md` said 206 tools while the catalogue held 210.** Fixed
   here, and it is worth noting the class: the count is asserted in
   `test_protocol.py` for `registry.TOOLS` and nowhere for the documentation.
