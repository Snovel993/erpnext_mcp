# v0.67.1 — the form was complete and three of its boxes were empty

I-9 `I9-2026-0001` — employee `HR-EMP-00008`, Timothy Polehn — reached status
`Complete`, had its PDF rendered and attached, and carried:

```
date_of_birth:  None        (the Employee record says 1978-11-05)
email:          ''
phone:          ''
status:         'Complete'
generated_pdf:  /private/files/I-9-….pdf
```

The iOS onboarding wizard never sent those three fields when it called
`submit_i9_section_1`. That is the cause. What made it a release rather than a
one-line fix is what happened next: **there was no tool in this app that could
put them in.**

---

## 1. Every I-9 tool moved a form forward

`submit_i9_section_1` takes a **Draft** and leaves it at `Section 1 Complete`.
`submit_i9_section_2` takes `Section 1 Complete` or `Awaiting Verification` and
leaves it at `Complete`. `reverify_i9` appends a Section 3 row. `destroy_i9`
ends the record. `render_i9_pdf` and `attach_signed_i9` write files.

Not one of them writes a Section 1 column on a form that has left Draft. So the
state `I9-2026-0001` was in — filed, signed, complete, rendered, and missing
three boxes Section 1 asks for — was a state with no exit. The record was a
federal one. An inspection under 8 U.S.C. §1324a would have been shown a page
with an empty date of birth and a status saying the form was finished.

The narrow reading would have been to widen `submit_i9_section_1` to accept a
non-Draft. That is the wrong shape, and section 3 says why.

---

## 2. `patch_i9_section_1`

**MUTATING, default off.** `allow_patch_i9_section_1` in ERPNext MCP Settings,
under I-9 / Compliance, beside the switch for the tool it complements.

Takes the form by docname or by the person it belongs to — `i9_form`, `name`,
`employee`, `employee_name`, resolved by `_resolve_form`, the same way
`render_i9_pdf` resolves it.

Writes **four columns and no others**:

| Field | Why it is on the list |
| --- | --- |
| `date_of_birth` | A date the employee gave the employer. Mistyping it is a typing mistake |
| `email` | An address to reach them at |
| `phone` | Same |
| `ssn_last_four` | Four digits. Stripped from a longer number; a shorter one is refused rather than stored short |

Each of the four is a **transcription** of something the employee already told
the employer, so correcting one changes nothing the form attests to.

---

## 3. What it refuses, and why refusing beats ignoring

`legal_first_name`, `legal_middle_name`, `legal_last_name`, `other_last_names`,
the four address fields, `citizenship_status`, `alien_registration_number`,
`i94_admission_number`, `foreign_passport_number`, `foreign_passport_country`,
`alien_work_authorization_expiry`, `section_1_signature`, the four preparer
fields — and `ssn`.

Those are the **attestation itself**. Section 1 is signed under penalty of
perjury, and a Form I-9 whose sworn answers were edited after the signature was
made is a form whose signature no longer covers what it says. They are changed
by re-attesting.

**They are refused by name rather than dropped**, and that is the decision worth
arguing for. `submit_i9_section_1` takes every one of them, so a caller reaching
for a correction reaches for the names it already knows. A tool that ignored
them would return a success reporting that the form was corrected while the
wrong name was still on it — which is a worse outcome than the error, by a long
way, because nobody goes and looks afterwards.

The same reasoning runs one step further: **a refused field takes the whole call
with it.** Sending `legal_first_name` alongside a valid `phone` writes neither.
A partial success would leave the caller believing the refused half landed.

`ssn` — the nine-digit argument — is refused for a different reason. It reaches
the encrypted `ssn_full` column only where `store_full_ssn` is on in I-9
Settings (see `tools/i9.py`'s header for why that is a switch). A correction
path that quietly took nine digits would route around that switch.

**A value is required for every field named.** This corrects a field to the
right answer; it does not clear one. A tool whose entire justification is "a
wizard failed to send three fields" should not also be the quickest way to empty
three more.

**Statuses.** `Section 1 Complete` and `Complete`. A `Draft` is refused and told
to use `submit_i9_section_1` — that is the tool carrying Section 1's own rules,
including that an Alien Authorized to Work must answer with one of the three
identifiers, and a patch tool that accepted a Draft would be a second way in
that skips them. A `Destroyed` record is refused for the reason `render_i9_pdf`
gives about the same status.

**It moves no status and signs nothing.** A `Complete` form stays Complete, and
`section_1_signed_at` / `section_2_signed_at` are untouched: fixing a typo does
not make the employee have signed on a different day. v0.64.2 spent a release on
those two columns asserting more than they knew, and this release does not
undo it.

## 4. Who may make one

**System Manager, HR Manager or HR User.**

That is `employee.HR_ROLES` less **`Farm Manager`**, and the subtraction is
deliberate. `HR_ROLES` is what this app gates *hiring* on, because on this site
the farm manager is the person who actually hires. A filed I-9 is a different
object: it is the retained record an inspection is shown, and who may amend one
after the fact is the personnel-records question rather than the hiring one.

`_require_correction_role` reads the identity the same way
`employee.require_hr_role` does — `security.caller_identity()` where a handset
or Desk session authenticated one, `frappe.session.user` (the MCP System User)
on the ordinary MCP path — and is a separate function rather than a parameter on
that one, so the decision lives where somebody correcting an I-9 would look
for it.

---

## 5. The audit row records who changed what, not what it now says

New action on the I-9 Audit Log Select: **`section_1_correction`**. It carries

```json
{"fields": ["date_of_birth", "email"],
 "was_blank": ["date_of_birth", "email"],
 "status": "Complete",
 "corrected_by": "claude@fafo.farm",
 "reason": "iOS onboarding wizard did not send Section 1 contact fields"}
```

and **not the values**. That is the rule `submit_i9_section_1` already follows
for the immigration identifiers, and the reason is unchanged: an audit row is a
second doctype, and a date of birth or four SSN digits copied into a JSON blob
on it is one more place a personal identifier lives.

It is also the right record on its own terms. What an inspection asks of a
corrected I-9 is *who changed what, and when* — which is exactly what the paper
procedure records when somebody lines through an entry, writes the correction,
and initials and dates it. The values are on the form. Frappe's own Version row
carries the before.

`reason` is optional and worth sending: it is the sentence an inspection reads
beside the change.

---

## 6. The rendered page is redrawn

`generated_pdf` is the copy an inspection is shown. A page still carrying the
empty date of birth this call just filled in is the record and its printable
copy disagreeing about the one fact somebody would print it to prove.

So `_redraw_generated_pdf` calls `render_i9_pdf` with `overwrite=true`. The File
that was there stays attached to the record, so the copy somebody already
printed is not lost.

Three behaviours, and `pdf` in the result always has the same shape so a caller
needs one code path:

- **Rendered before** → redrawn, `regenerated: true`, with the new `file_url`
  and what it `replaced`.
- **Never rendered** → nothing drawn, and a note pointing at `render_i9_pdf`.
  Producing a federal form nobody asked for is this app deciding something that
  is not its to decide. This is the same decision `signatures._redraw` makes.
- **Renderer failed** (no `pypdf`, template missing) → the correction is kept
  and the note says the page is now stale. A correction thrown away because a
  redraw raised afterwards is the larger problem.

---

## 7. Known gap, named rather than closed

A form resting at **`Awaiting Verification`** has a filed Section 1 — Section 2's
documents are recorded and one attestation is outstanding — and it is **not** a
correctable status here. v0.67.1 shipped the two statuses that were asked for.
There is a test that asserts the refusal and says in its docstring that it is
recording a gap, so widening `CORRECTABLE_STATUSES` later is a one-line change
with a test already pointing at it.
