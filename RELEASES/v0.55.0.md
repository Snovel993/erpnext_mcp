# v0.55.0 — the box nobody signed

Every I-9 and W-4 rule this app has shipped watches a **clock**. Verification is
overdue. Work authorization expires in ninety days. Retention has run out and the
form may be destroyed. Each of them measures a distance from a date, and each of
them is silent about a form that was filled in perfectly, filed on the third
business day, and signed by nobody.

That form is not a late I-9. It is a document that attests to nothing.

8 CFR 274a.2(b)(1)(i)(A) asks the employee to sign, under penalty of perjury,
that they are who they say they are and may lawfully work here. §274a.2(b)(1)(ii)(B)
asks a named representative of the employer to sign, under the same penalty, that
they examined the documents the form lists. An unsigned Section 1 is the
commonest finding an ICE inspection writes up, and it is *substantive* rather
than technical: the operation has recorded that it checked, and produced no
evidence that anybody did.

Four rules find those boxes. The dispatch board puts each one on the phone of
somebody who can actually fix it. One call files the capture.

---

## 1. Four rules that watch a box rather than a date

| Rule | Fires when | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `i9_section_1_unsigned` | An I-9 past Draft has no `section_1_signature` | Critical |
| `i9_section_2_unsigned` | A `verification_date` is recorded and `section_2_signature` is empty | Critical |
| `i9_supplement_b_unsigned` | Any reverification row on a live I-9 has no `section_3_signature` | Critical |
| `w4_signature_missing` | An Active W-4 has no employee `signature` | Warning |

All four are `date_field_role: State` with both thresholds at `-1`. Nothing bands
and nothing counts down, deliberately: the deadline these forms live under is
already watched by `i9_verification_overdue`, a signature does not become more
missing on the fourth day, and stacking a second countdown on the same record
would put two rows on one calendar for one afternoon's errand.

**Section 2 is gated on `verification_date`, not on `status`.** A form still
awaiting verification has an empty Section 2 and nothing to have signed — raising
on it would be `i9_verification_overdue` under a second name. A form carrying a
verification date is one where somebody looked at the documents; this rule asks
whether they then put their name to what they saw.

**The W-4 rule will fire broadly on the first sweep after upgrade, and that is
the finding rather than a fault in the rule.** `submit_w4` has never captured the
employee's signature. Every W-4 this app has written is unsigned, and an unsigned
W-4 is invalid — Publication 15 tells the employer to withhold as Single with no
adjustments until it is signed, which for a married picker claiming three
dependents is the difference between a correct cheque and a complaint. It is a
Warning rather than a Critical because it is correctable and carries no per-form
civil penalty; the two I-9 rules are Critical because §274a does.

### Why one of the four is built in

`i9_section_1_unsigned`, `i9_section_2_unsigned` and `w4_signature_missing` are
**records and nothing else** — declarative rules an operator can edit, each
asking one question of one column on one row, which is exactly what the
declarative vocabulary is for.

`i9_supplement_b_unsigned` cannot be, and the reason is the test for whether a
future rule belongs in the vocabulary or in code. **Supplement B lives in a child
table**: an I-9 carries a `reverifications` row per reverification or rehire, each
with its own attestation. "Does this form have an unsigned reverification" is a
question about a SET of rows, and answering it folds that set to a count and a
newest date. That is the same aggregation `audit_action_overdue` does over
corrective actions, and it is why that one is built in too.

The nearest declarative primitive, `latest_child_field_threshold`, folds a child
table to its newest row and then compares a **number** — `passes_threshold` takes
two floats and answers False on anything else, deliberately, so a thermometer
column somebody filled in as "warm" cannot raise a heat alert. An empty Attach
field is not a number, and widening that primitive to take presence operators
would put a non-numeric comparison inside the one function written to refuse them.

It raises **one alert per form**, not per row. Two unsigned entries on one worker
are one conversation with one authorized signer holding one form; two rows on the
calendar would read as two people to chase.

## 2. The task goes to somebody who can actually fix it

This is the part that needed new machinery.

Sections 1 and the W-4 are signed by the **worker**. The work is therefore not
signing — it is *finding* somebody who is out on a crew somewhere and putting a
phone in front of them. Nobody at a desk can do that. The person who can is
whoever already knows which block that crew is on this morning, which is what
`Employee.reports_to` records.

Sections 2 and Supplement B are the **employer** attesting, and who may make that
attestation is exactly what the authorized-signer roster in `tools/signers.py`
records. Routing one of these to anybody else would be raising work its holder is
not permitted to complete — the sign call would refuse them, having already put
the task on their list. Whoever already examined the documents comes first:
§274a.2 makes the attestation *the signer's own*, so finishing what you started
beats asking a colleague to attest to an examination they were not present for.

Neither is reachable by a `producer_assigned_to_expression`. That vocabulary
reads a COLUMN ON THE ROW THAT TRIPPED — `row.foreman`, and the foreman is on the
shift — and both of these walk to a doctype the tripped row does not mention.

So `ALERT_TASK_MAP` and a rule's `extra_parameters` gained
**`producer_assignee_resolver`**: a name from a closed registry of reviewed,
shipped lookups, beside the existing sandboxed expression. It is the same trade
`builtin_scanner` makes on the rule side — the SHAPE of the lookup is code
because it walks doctypes an expression cannot reach, and everything tunable
about it stays on the record.

Every failure answers `""` and says which one it was on the report: no
`reports_to` column on this site, no supervisor on that employee, an empty roster
(which means *unrestricted*, so there is no name to route to), a roster whose
signers have no Employee record. Each leaves the task on the `hr_admin` pool.
**Never `Dispatched` with nobody on it** — visible, urgent and unreachable is the
worst of the three ways for dispatch to fail.

### Two new columns on Farm Task, and a title people can read

`subject_doctype` / `subject_docname` is **the record a task is about**. It is
not `location`: a location is somewhere a worker can be SENT and is what the pool
listing filters on, and an I-9 is not a place. It is written for *every*
generated task, not only the signature ones — "what is this task about" is a
question every board has had and answered by making somebody open the alert.

And the task is called **"Collect I-9 Section 2 signature for Juan Lopez"** rather
than "… — I9-2026-0043". `MC-Cabin-01` *is* what a foreman calls that cabin;
`I9-2026-0043` is what nobody calls Juan Lopez. A recipe may name one column on
the source record to be called by, and falls back to the docname where it is
empty.

## 3. `collect_form_signature`

    store the image  →  write it onto the form  →  close the task  →  redraw the PDF

Four boxes, and the list is closed: `I-9 Form.section_1_signature`,
`section_2_signature`, `section_3_signature` (a child row) and
`W-4 Form.signature`. A field outside them is refused with the list — an endpoint
that wrote an image into any column somebody named would be an arbitrary write
with an Attach-shaped hat on.

### It takes base64, which `attach_signed_i9` refuses to

`attach_signed_i9` says at length that no bytes cross its boundary, and it is
right about the file it handles: a signed I-9 is a **scan of a page**, megabytes,
taken on a camera, and it goes up through `stage_file_chunk` /
`finalize_staged_file` because a link that drops halfway through eight megabytes
has to be resumable.

A signature capture is not that file. It is what a finger drew on a glass
rectangle: a few kilobytes of monochrome PNG, produced in one gesture, complete
or not at all. Chunking it would be three round trips and a staging session to
move less data than the JSON body around it — and every one of those is another
place to lose a signature between the drawing and the storing, with the person
who drew it already walking away.

Both doors are open, and **the 512 KB ceiling is what says which is which**: a
hundredfold over a real capture, an order of magnitude under a scanned page.
Anything larger is told to use the other door by name. The format is read off the
**first bytes**, never off a filename — this call stores what it is sent under a
name it composes itself, and a signature box holding an HTML document that a
filename called `.png` is exactly what `api/files.py` keeps `.svg` off its
allowlist to avoid.

### What it refuses, before anything is stored

* a field outside the four;
* a caller who may not **write** the form — through Frappe's own permission
  check, not a role list compiled into this app, because who may write an I-9 on
  a given site is a question that site's rules answer;
* a caller not on the authorized-signer roster, **for the two employer boxes
  only**. Section 1 and the W-4 are signed by the worker under their own penalty
  of perjury; requiring the account holding the phone to be an authorized signer
  would mean the only people who can collect a worker's signature are the people
  authorised to sign *for* the employer, which is precisely the conflation §274a
  keeps apart;
* a box that already carries a signature, unless `overwrite` — replacing an
  attestation silently is the one write here that could not be noticed
  afterwards;
* a destroyed I-9, whose documents no longer exist.

### What it does not refuse

**A task it could not close, and a PDF it could not redraw.** Both are reported;
neither undoes the signature.

Closing goes through `complete_farm_task`, which will not take a completion from
an account that was not holding the task — *"a completion filed by somebody who
was not there is not a chain of custody, it is a rumour"*. This is not the place
to route around that. An office account that collected a signature on behalf of
the foreman holding the task has done something real, and what should happen is
that the foreman closes their own task, not that the office closes it in their
name. The result says which task was found and why it stayed open.

The ordering runs this way round because **the signature is the compliance
artefact** and the rest is bookkeeping about it. A phone in an orchard that
captured a signature, wrote it to the federal record, and then could not reach
the PDF renderer has not failed — it has done the part §274a asks for, and
rolling that back to keep four things tidy would throw away the only
irreplaceable one. The reverse would be wrong: closing the task first and then
failing to store the image leaves a board saying the signature was collected and
a form saying it was not.

The PDF step is **regeneration only**. A form that has never been rendered gets
nothing — producing a federal form nobody asked for, on a phone, as a side effect
of collecting a signature, is not this call's decision to make. What it will not
leave behind is a rendered page gone stale, because `render_i9_pdf` stamps
captured signatures into the page content and flattens it: an unsigned printable
copy of a signed form is the record and its printout disagreeing about the one
fact somebody would print it to prove.

Stored private, always. Logged to **I-9 Audit Log** as `Signature Collected`.

## 4. On the mobile surface

`collect_signature` — `POST /farmops/api/mobile/collect_signature`. The HR role
is required with no exception, exactly as `upload_signed_i9` requires it and for
the same reason: this writes the document the employer is inspected on, and an
account that could put a signature on its own Section 2 could attest that
somebody examined documents when nobody did.

`worker_id` is not an argument. Which task closes is worked out from the form and
the alert type — **both**, so an I-9 missing two signatures, held by two
different people, does not have the wrong one closed.

Listed in `PENDING_IOS_INTEGRATION`: the server half is complete and the handset
half is a signature pad opened over a task's `subject_docname`, which
`MobileAPI.swift` does not name yet.

## Upgrading

`bench --site <site> migrate` adds `subject_doctype` / `subject_docname` to Farm
Task, `allow_collect_form_signature` to the settings (**off**, like every other
mutating tool), and seeds the four rules. Seeding checks by `rule_id` before it
writes, so a site that has already edited or disabled one keeps it that way.

The first sweep afterwards will raise every unsigned form the register has. That
is the point, and the W-4 count will be all of them.
